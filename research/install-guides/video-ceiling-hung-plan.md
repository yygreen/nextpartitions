## 1. Consensus order (4 manufacturers: Bobrick, ASI Global, Bradley, Metpar; AMPCO extra; Hadrian for one point)

| 10 | Sleeves pushed up to the ceiling and secured | 4 of 4 |
| 11 | Panels fastened to the wall, supports out | 4 of 4 |
| 12 | Doors and hardware; final checks | 4 of 4 |

Safety and general points the video carries: hang only from structural steel, never the ceiling grid, tile, drywall or framing; if the steel isn't there, stop and tell the general contractor (all; AMPCO and Bobrick most directly). Heavy parts: support them until they are fixed. Doors go on only once the openings are right and the pilasters are tight. Each latch must open from outside before handover.

## 2. Where sources differ (and what the video does)

- **Who fits the rods.** Bobrick and Metpar have the installer put rods through the steel; ASI Global and AMPCO expect them in place before the ceiling is finished and only check them. Video: the rods are already there in step 1 and checked in step 2; captions don't say who fitted them.
- **Bracing.** Bradley braces tall ceilings, Metpar adds a back rail for plastic, Bobrick and ASI rely on the structure. Video: a sway check for everyone, then a ghosted brace with "Bracing, where the system calls for it"; the brace fades out again so the finished run shows no extra parts.
- **Panels first or alternating.** Bradley alternates panel and pilaster from the wall; the others set the panels first. Video: panels first (3 of 4).
- **Hanging hardware.** Makers differ (bar inside the pilaster top, a bracket on top, a nut stack). Video: a generic threaded rod with a support nut under a hanging bar and a top nut above it, seen through the pilaster top in a cut-away, and nuts above and below the steel flange.

Rendered by `render-all-chd.sh` (2560 x 1440, 30 fps).

The order changes from the overview in one place: the pilasters are leveled on their support nuts **before** the brackets connect them to the panels, so the brackets go on at the final height (the consensus order levels first too; the overview combined both into one step).

The hanging hardware is drawn generic: a top nut and a support nut run onto each rod with a gap between them, and a slotted bar inside the pilaster top slides into that gap. The bar's slots open to the front, so the pilaster is lifted a little high and forward, pushed back onto the rods, then lowered onto the support nuts. Inside views come from turning that one pilaster see-through.
