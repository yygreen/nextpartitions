# Toilet partition installation: manufacturer consensus by mounting style

Research for our own general installation overview guides (overhead braced, floor mounted, ceiling hung, floor to ceiling). Read 2026-11-26. Everything below is in our own words. No manufacturer text, drawings or exact figures go on the pages; the numbers in "Facts noted, not for the page" are background only.

How to read the counts: "n of N" counts **manufacturers** whose instructions show the step, not documents. ASI Global Partitions and the older Global Partitions booklet are one manufacturer. AMPCO is not a brand Next Partitions resells; it is included as an extra independent source and counted separately in brackets where it matters. "Ammco" in our brand list is taken to be All American Metal Corp (AAMCO), matching the "Aamco format" pricing sheet; please confirm.

## 1. Overhead braced

### Order each source gives (own words)

- **Bobrick** (HPL, compact laminate, composite): lay out anchor points → set floor anchors → wall brackets (and wall posts) → panels onto temporary supports and into wall brackets, unfastened → brackets onto stiles → stand stiles on the anchors, fix panels to stile brackets, plumb, set door openings, tighten anchors, fix panels to wall → headrail → doors and door hardware → shoes → cleanup. The composite (Sierra) booklet instead rough-levels all floor anchors first, then builds stile by stile from the wall, using a length of headrail across two stiles as a level.
- **ASI Global** (solid plastic; steel in the older Global booklet): review drawings and confirm backing → mark pilaster and panel lines and floor fixing points → wall brackets and floor fixings (plastic: shoes screwed to the floor; steel: L-brackets and shoe clips, loose) → panel on a support into wall brackets, unfastened → brackets onto pilasters → set pilasters, adjust leveling bolt → level panel and gaps, fix panel to wall and pilaster, remove support → plumb pilasters (headrail laid on loosely as a guide) and secure with headrail → fix pilaster to shoe (plastic) / slide shoe down onto clips (steel) → doors and hardware.
- **Bradley** (phenolic, powder-coated and stainless steel): layout and plumb lines on walls → wall brackets → leveling screws into pilaster bottoms → brackets onto pilasters → floor shoes/brackets screwed down along the pilaster line, starting from the wall → wall pilasters first, then alternate panel (on a spacer) and pilaster across the run, checking plumb and level as you go → secure shoes (phenolic) → headrail, fixed to wall bracket and each pilaster once door openings are checked → shoes (steel guide puts them after the headrail) → hinges and doors (only once openings are right, pilasters plumb and headrail on) → door hardware.
- **Metpar** (phenolic, solid plastic, steel): layout and drill wall and floor fixings → prepare stiles (brackets, leveling bolt, slide plinth on but don't fix) → panel on support blocks into wall brackets, unfastened → floor clips/L-brackets, stand stile, push panel into stile brackets, plumb, wedge behind panel at the wall → plumb and level stiles (headrail set on loosely as an aid), fix stile to panel, set door openings with the floor brackets → headrail on, fixed to each stile and the wall, panel fixed to wall brackets, floor bolts tightened, plinth slid down → end headrail return → doors and hardware. The plastic manual interleaves the last steps: anchor headrail to one stile, hang that door, set its gap, then plumb the next stile and anchor the headrail to it.
- **All American Metal (AAMCO)** (powder-coated and stainless steel): verify drawings against site → mark pilaster line, floor fixings, panel lines, bracket heights → wall brackets; floor anchors with shoe clip and L-bracket, loose → panel on spacer into wall brackets, unfastened → U-brackets on pilasters, slide shoe on and tape it up, stand pilaster on L-brackets, adjust jack bolt, plumb, fix panel to pilaster (wedge at the wall first) → set each door opening from the wall pilaster, tighten floor brackets, level pilaster tops across the run, fix panels to wall → lower shoes onto clips → headrail, with return headrail to the back wall and end caps → hinges and doors, rest position, latch, keeper, hook, pull.
- **Scranton Products, Eclipse** (HDPE, post-and-rail system): anchor posts to the floor → level and attach headrail to posts and wall → door hinges → wall brackets → side panels → keepers, doors, fixed panels. Scranton itself says Eclipse installs very differently from a conventional partition; treat it as the outlier, not part of the consensus.
- **AMPCO** (stainless steel, baked enamel; extra source): safety and site review, confirm blocking → snap centerlines on floor and walls → wall brackets → floor L-plates → panels on 12-inch supports into brackets, unfastened → U-brackets on pilasters → start at the pilaster on the highest point of the floor; shoe on, pilaster onto L-plate, adjust leveling bolt, level and plumb → set gaps, fix panel to pilaster and wall → headrail, final door-opening adjustment, headrail fixed to pilasters and wall → secure shoes → doors and hardware → test emergency access.

### Consensus order

1. Check the site, the drawings and the delivery (6 of 6, +AMPCO).
2. Lay out pilaster line, panel lines and floor fixing points from the drawings (6 of 6).
3. Fix wall brackets into backing (6 of 6).
4. Set the floor fixings, left loose or adjustable (5 of 6: all but Scranton's post system).
5. Prepare pilasters: panel brackets, leveling bolt, shoe slid on and held up (5 of 6).
6. Rest panels on temporary supports in the wall brackets, not yet fastened (5 of 6).
7. Stand pilasters on the floor fixings and engage the panels (5 of 6).
8. Plumb and level the pilasters, set door openings, then fasten panel to pilaster, floor fixings and wall brackets (5 of 6).
9. Install the headrail across the pilaster tops and into the wall, with a return at an open end (6 of 6).
10. Secure the shoes (5 of 6, timing varies).
11. Hang doors, set their rest position, fit latch, keeper and accessories (6 of 6).
12. Final adjustment, emergency-access check and cleanup (4 of 6: Bobrick, ASI Global, Metpar, AAMCO; + AMPCO).

### Where sources differ

- **Headrail timing.** Everyone puts the headrail before the doors. Bobrick, Bradley, AAMCO and AMPCO fix it after all pilasters are plumbed and fastened; ASI Global and Metpar plumb each pilaster and "lock" it with the headrail as they go; Metpar's plastic manual alternates headrail anchoring with hanging one door at a time; Scranton Eclipse puts the headrail up first, with the posts, before any panel.
- **Headrail as a leveling aid.** ASI Global, Metpar and Bobrick (composite) lay a headrail loosely across pilaster tops to check level before fixing anything.
- **Shoes.** Before the headrail (AAMCO, Bradley phenolic, Global steel); after the headrail (Bradley steel, ASI plastic, Metpar, AMPCO); last of all, after the doors (Bobrick). All agree the shoe is slid onto the pilaster early and only dropped and fixed once the pilaster is in its final position.
- **Panels all at once vs. alternating.** Bobrick, ASI Global, AAMCO, Metpar and AMPCO set panels on supports first, then stand pilasters. Bradley starts with the wall pilaster and alternates panel, pilaster, panel. Bobrick's composite booklet works compartment by compartment from the wall.
- **Floor fixing.** Bradley and ASI (plastic) screw a shoe or bracket to the floor and set the pilaster into it; the others use L-brackets or studs with a leveling bolt or nuts.
- **Where to start.** AMPCO starts at the pilaster on the highest point of the floor (floors slope to drains); Bradley takes its level line from the highest point; the others start from a wall.

### Advice specific to overhead braced

- The headrail is what keeps the run rigid: fix it to every pilaster and to the wall, put joints over a pilaster, and add a return headrail back to the wall at an open end (Bobrick, Bradley, Metpar, AAMCO).
- Level the pilaster tops to each other before fixing panels, so the headrail sits flat (ASI Global, AAMCO, Bradley).

## 2. Floor mounted (floor anchored, no headrail)

### Order each source gives (own words)

- **Bobrick**: lay out anchor points → set floor anchors with the lower nut and washer at a set height → (composite booklet: rough-level all anchors to a string line across the room) → wall brackets → panels on supports, unfastened → brackets on stiles → stiles onto anchor studs, top nuts loose → fix panels to stile brackets, plumb stiles, shim between panel and wall, set door openings square and plumb, tighten anchors, fix panels to wall → doors and hardware → shoes clipped over the anchor → support bracket to the wall for very wide stiles → cleanup.
- **ASI Global (Global steel booklet)**: review drawings, confirm floor and wall backing → mark pilaster and panel lines and anchor points → drill and set wedge anchors in the concrete, wall brackets → panel on support, unfastened → U-brackets on pilasters → support nut, shoe clip and washer on each anchor; shoe slid onto pilaster; pilaster onto anchors, top nuts loose → level with the support nuts, plumb, fix to panel, set door openings, tighten top nuts → drop shoe onto clips → remove panel support → doors.
- **Hadrian** (standard metal, floor mounted): set out the front line and floor stud positions from the drawings → fix floor studs → wall brackets, rest the panel on a block in them → shoe onto wall pilaster, set it on its studs, plumb and tighten, fix to wall brackets → hinges on that pilaster → next pilaster: mark and fit U-brackets, shoe on, set on studs, engage panel, fix → hang door, set rest angle, stop and keeper → repeat compartment by compartment. Hadrian recommends finishing one compartment, door included, before the next.
- **AMPCO** (extra source): snap centerlines → wall brackets → drill floor, set drop-in anchors with threaded rod and lower nut and washer → panels on supports → brackets on pilasters → starting at the highest floor point: shoe on, pilaster onto rods, top nut on, level and plumb with nuts above and below, not tight → set gaps, fix panels to pilaster and wall → final door-opening adjustment, tighten top nuts → doors → secure shoe.

### Consensus order

1. Check the site, drawings and delivery; confirm the floor can take anchors (3 of 3, +AMPCO).
2. Lay out pilaster line, panel lines and anchor points (3 of 3).
3. Drill and set the floor anchors (threaded studs or bolts) with lower leveling nuts (3 of 3).
4. Fix wall brackets into backing (3 of 3).
5. Rest panels on temporary supports in the wall brackets (3 of 3).
6. Prepare pilasters: brackets on, shoe slid on and held up (3 of 3).
7. Lower pilasters onto the anchor studs, top nuts loose (3 of 3).
8. Level with the nuts, plumb, set door openings, fasten panels to pilasters, tighten the anchors, fasten panels to the wall (3 of 3).
9. Hang doors and fit hardware (3 of 3).
10. Drop and secure the shoes over the anchors (3 of 3, timing varies).
11. Final checks and cleanup (2 of 3: Bobrick, ASI Global; + AMPCO).

### Where sources differ

- **Whole run vs. compartment by compartment.** Bobrick and Global set every panel and pilaster, then do all doors. Hadrian does one compartment at a time, door included; Bobrick's composite booklet also goes stile by stile from the wall.
- **Pre-leveling the anchors.** Bobrick sets all anchor nuts to one level line before any pilaster goes up; Global and AMPCO level each pilaster on its own nuts as they go.
- **Shoes.** Global drops them as each pilaster is tightened; Bobrick and AMPCO leave them to the end.
- **Extra support.** Bobrick adds a wall support bracket at the foot of very wide end stiles.

### Advice specific to floor mounted

- Without a headrail, each pilaster stands only on its floor anchors, so the anchors must be fully set in sound concrete and the pilaster tightened plumb in both directions before the door goes on (all).
- Floors slope to drains: take heights from the highest point (AMPCO) or set anchors to a common level line (Bobrick).

## 3. Ceiling hung

### Order each source gives (own words)

- **Bobrick** (with TB-32 bulletin): structural support above the ceiling is designed by the architect or engineer and supplied by others; ceiling anchors (threaded rod through the steel, nuts each side, then the shoe retainer and leveling hardware) go in **before the finished ceiling** → wall brackets → panels on supports → brackets on stiles → stiles hung on the rods, nuts loose → fix panels, plumb, set openings, tighten → doors → shoes up to the ceiling. If the ceiling structure can't be made adequate, Bobrick says to choose floor-anchored or overhead-braced instead.
- **ASI Global** (solid plastic and the steel booklet): review drawings; confirm wall backing and ceiling support; check the threaded rods are where the drawings say and project the right amount below the ceiling → mark pilaster and panel lines relative to the rods; wall brackets → panel on a support, unfastened (it may need raising or lowering to line up with pilaster bottoms) → brackets on pilasters → shoe slid over the top end → support nuts and washers on the rods, pilaster mounting bar on, top nut loose → level with the support nuts, plumb, fix to panel, set door openings, tighten → shoe up to the ceiling → fix panel to wall, remove support → doors (notched for the bottom hinge).
- **Bradley** (stainless steel; phenolic product sheet): "C" channel above the ceiling is required and not included; ceiling-hung isn't recommended for high ceilings (product sheet) → layout → wall brackets → brackets on pilasters → threaded rods into the channel, support nuts set to one level → wall pilasters first, then alternate panel (on spacer) and pilaster; shoe slid on the top and taped; pilaster hung on the rods; check plumb and level, adjust with the support nuts → secure shoes; cross bracing between pilasters on tall ceilings → hinges once openings are correct and pilasters plumb → door hardware.
- **Metpar** (phenolic, solid plastic): structural support not supplied by Metpar, confirm before starting → layout → stile brackets (full-height brackets for plastic ceiling hung) → panel on support blocks → threaded rods through the overhead support, nuts above and below, pilaster hung, height adjusted so pilaster and panel bottoms line up, plumbed, nuts tightened → shoe on → panel fixed to front brackets, support removed → plastic: a support rail along the back of the pilasters to the side walls for stiffness → doors.
- **AMPCO** (extra source): rods installed above the finished ceiling from the factory bolt layout; if the structural steel isn't there, stop and tell the contractor → keep the distance from steel to pilaster as short as possible, since more drop means more sway → panels on supports → shoe on, pilaster onto rods, level and plumb with nuts above and below the bar, not tight → gaps, fix panels → final door-opening adjustment, tighten → doors.
- **Hadrian** (spec sheet only): Hadrian-supplied ceiling studs must be used; its manuals (not reachable) say ceiling hung needs an overhead structural member, normally a channel, by others.

### Consensus order

1. Before the ceiling closes in: structural steel above the ceiling, by others, with hanging rods at the pilaster positions (4 of 4, +AMPCO, +Hadrian).
2. Check the rods are in the right places and project correctly; check wall backing (4 of 4).
3. Lay out pilaster and panel lines, plumb down from the rods (4 of 4).
4. Fix wall brackets into backing (4 of 4).
5. Rest panels on temporary supports in the wall brackets (4 of 4).
6. Prepare pilasters: brackets on, shoe slid over the top and held (4 of 4).
7. Hang pilasters on the rods on support nuts, top nuts loose (4 of 4).
8. Level (pilaster bottoms in line with panels), plumb, set door openings, fasten panels, tighten the nuts (4 of 4).
9. Add any bracing the system calls for (cross bracing, back rail) (2 of 4: Bradley, Metpar; AMPCO's advice to keep the drop short is the same concern).
10. Push shoes up to the ceiling and secure (4 of 4).
11. Fasten panels to the wall, remove supports (4 of 4).
12. Hang doors and fit hardware; final checks (4 of 4).

### Where sources differ

- **Who installs the rods.** Bobrick and Metpar have the installer fit rods through the steel; AMPCO and ASI Global expect rods already in place before the ceiling is finished and only check them.
- **Bracing.** Bradley adds cross bracing on taller ceilings; Metpar adds a back support rail for plastic; Bobrick and ASI rely on the structure alone (Bobrick says the structure must be stiff enough for door swing and impact).
- **Alternate vs. panels first.** Bradley alternates from the wall; the others set panels first.

### Advice specific to ceiling hung

- The structure above the ceiling carries the whole compartment and the door impact; it must be designed and installed by others before the ceiling closes in, and if it isn't there or isn't adequate, stop (Bobrick, AMPCO, Metpar, Bradley, Hadrian).
- Keep the hanging distance short and the rods tight; long drops sway (AMPCO, Bradley cross bracing, Bobrick rigidity note).
- No headrail and nothing at the floor: the floor stays clear for cleaning, so the floor finish doesn't matter, but the ceiling layout does.

## 4. Floor to ceiling

### Order each source gives (own words)

- **Bobrick** (compact laminate, HPL): ceiling anchors set before the finished ceiling, floor anchors set → wall brackets → panels on supports → brackets on stiles (full-height stiles) → stiles onto floor and ceiling anchors, fix panels, plumb, set openings, tighten → headrail where the extended-privacy system uses one → doors → shoes top and bottom → cleanup.
- **ASI Global** (solid plastic; steel booklet): confirm wall backing and ceiling support; check ceiling rods (plastic) or mark ceiling fixings (steel) → mark pilaster and panel lines; floor and ceiling fixing points → wall brackets, floor wedge anchors or L-brackets, ceiling bolts or L-brackets → panel on support → brackets on pilasters → shoes slid over both ends → set pilaster between the fixings, adjust jack bolt, loose → level and plumb, fix to panel, set door openings, tighten top and bottom → shoes up to the ceiling and down to the floor → fix panel to wall, remove support → doors.
- **Bradley** (phenolic, powder-coated and stainless steel, Bradmar plastic): layout and plumb lines → wall brackets → leveling screws and brackets on pilasters → floor shoe screwed down; pilaster stood in it to find where the ceiling bracket goes; ceiling bracket fixed (projecting the pilaster face up to the ceiling) → wall pilasters, then alternate panel and pilaster; top shoe slid on and taped → secure shoes, top shoe flush to the ceiling → hinges and doors → hardware. Steel pilasters may have a telescoping top section to suit the ceiling height.
- **Metpar** (FT700, phenolic, plastic, steel): layout → stile brackets → panel on taller support blocks → floor studs in expansion shields, plinth slid on, stile lowered onto studs, nuts above and below → top of stile and panel aligned, panel pushed into brackets, stile plumbed, wedge at the wall → fix stile to panel, set openings → panel fixed to wall → doors.
- **AMPCO** (extra source): L-plates on floor and ceiling (or an adjustable bar at one end) → panels on supports → shoes over both ends, top one taped → pilaster onto both L-plates, leveling bolt adjusted, level and plumb, shoes not fixed yet → gaps, fix panels → final door-opening adjustment → shoes fixed → doors.

### Consensus order

1. Check site, drawings and delivery; confirm wall backing and structure above the ceiling at each pilaster (4 of 4, +AMPCO).
2. Lay out on the floor and transfer the pilaster positions plumb to the ceiling (4 of 4).
3. Fix wall brackets into backing (4 of 4).
4. Set floor fixings, then ceiling fixings plumb above them (4 of 4).
5. Prepare pilasters: brackets on, shoes slid onto both ends (4 of 4).
6. Rest panels on temporary supports in the wall brackets (4 of 4).
7. Set pilasters between floor and ceiling fixings, adjust height, loose (4 of 4).
8. Plumb, set door openings, fasten panels, tighten top and bottom (4 of 4).
9. Seat shoes at the ceiling and the floor and secure (4 of 4).
10. Fasten panels to the wall, remove supports (4 of 4).
11. Hang doors and fit hardware; final checks (4 of 4).

### Where sources differ

- **Finding the ceiling point.** Bradley stands the pilaster in its fixed floor shoe to locate the ceiling bracket; the others mark both ends from the layout (plumb bob or laser).
- **Ceiling structure.** Bobrick and ASI (plastic) use threaded rods into structure above the ceiling, as for ceiling hung; Bradley, Global steel and AMPCO fix L-brackets to the ceiling with anchors into adequate structure.
- **Pilaster length.** Pilasters are made to the ceiling height on the drawings; Bradley's steel system telescopes, others expect field checks of the ceiling height at each pilaster.
- **Headrail.** None, except Bobrick's extended-privacy system, which can carry one.

## General advice most sources agree on (all styles)

Site readiness
- Read the whole booklet and keep the manufacturer's layout (shop) drawings at hand; all dimensions and bracket positions come from them (all).
- Check that the room's actual widths, depths and ceiling height match the drawings before drilling (Bobrick, AAMCO, ASI, Metpar, Bradley, AMPCO).
- Unpack, count parts against the packing list and inspect for damage; report freight damage at once and don't start with parts missing (ASI Global, Bobrick, Bradley, Metpar, AMPCO).
- Floors and walls clean, smooth and free of debris; finished surfaces (tile) in place (Bradley).
- Store panels and doors flat, never leaning or stacked unevenly; leave protective film on until the end (Bradley, Bobrick, AMPCO).
- Plastic (HDPE) needs time to adjust to the room and should be installed in a climate-controlled space out of direct sun (Bradley Bradmar).

Wall backing and structure (installed by others)
- Every wall bracket and headrail bracket needs solid backing (blocking) in the wall; the partition maker doesn't supply it (all).
- Ceiling hung and floor to ceiling need structural support above the ceiling, designed and installed by others before the ceiling is finished (Bobrick, Bradley, Metpar, Hadrian, AMPCO, ASI).
- Floor anchors go into sound concrete; other floors need different anchors (Bobrick, Global, AMPCO).

Plumb, level and openings
- Pilasters must be plumb both ways and level with each other; adjust with leveling bolts or nuts, not by forcing panels (all).
- Floors slope to drains: take the reference height from the highest point (AMPCO, Bradley).
- Set each door opening to the drawing, square and plumb, before final tightening (all).
- Leave the gaps the drawings show between panel and wall and between panel and pilaster (Bradley, AMPCO, AAMCO, Bobrick).

Drilling
- Use the bracket as the template; drill blind pilot holes where the screw must not show and never through a finished face unless the instructions say so (Bobrick, Bradley, Metpar).
- Back up the material when drilling through it to avoid break-out; take care not to dimple steel faces (Bradley).

Doors
- Hang doors only after the openings are right and the pilasters are fixed (and the headrail is on, where there is one) (all).
- Set the hinge cam so each door rests where the drawings say (partly open or closed); accessible-stall doors close on their own (Bobrick, Global, AAMCO, AMPCO, Metpar).
- Line the keeper up with the latch and check the door can be lifted from outside to clear the keeper for emergency access (AMPCO, ASI Global, Metpar).

Final checks and handover
- Check doors swing freely, latch and keep even gaps; tighten all fasteners; remove labels and film; clean with mild soap and water only, never acid, tile or abrasive cleaners (Bobrick, ASI Global, Metpar, AMPCO).
- Warn other trades: tile and grout cleaners damage partitions and hardware (ASI Global, Bobrick).
- Leave care and warranty information with the owner (Bradley, Hadrian, AAMCO).
- Local code compliance is the installer's responsibility (Bradley).

What is installed by others (typical)
- Wall backing/blocking; structural steel above the ceiling (and often the hanging rods) for ceiling hung and floor to ceiling; the finished floor, walls and ceiling; any lateral bracing the engineer calls for.

## Safety points every source stresses

- Wear proper protective equipment (Bobrick, Bradley, Metpar, AMPCO).
- Don't install without confirmed backing and structural support; for ceiling-hung, if the overhead steel isn't there, stop and tell the contractor (all; AMPCO and Bobrick most directly).
- Components are heavy; use more than one person to lift and place them, and keep them on solid temporary supports until fixed (Bradley; supports used by all).
- Review the jobsite for hazards and report them to the site superintendent (AMPCO).
- The emergency-access feature of each latch must work before handover (AMPCO, ASI Global, Metpar).
- Failure to follow the instructions can cause injury or damage and voids the warranty (Bradley, AMPCO).

## Facts noted, not for the page

- Concrete anchors: roughly a 2-inch minimum embedment for floor anchors (Bobrick wedge anchor; Bradley plastic anchors), 2-1/2 inches in the Global steel booklet, 3-inch drilled depth for AMPCO drop-in anchors.
- Temporary support height for panels: 12 inches above the floor in most (Bobrick, Global steel, AAMCO, AMPCO, Bradley steel); 14 inches ASI plastic; 13-1/2 or 15 inches Metpar.
- Ceiling rods project about 2 to 2-1/2 inches below the finished ceiling (ASI Global); Global steel limits the gap between finished ceiling and the bottom of the steel to about 1 inch.
- Bobrick TB-32: ceiling structure must carry roughly 275–295 lb per compartment and resist door swing and impact; mounting system designed by the architect or engineer.
- Bradley: ceiling hung not recommended over 8-foot ceilings (product sheet); cross bracing standard at 9-foot ceilings and above (steel ceiling-hung guide).
- Typical door gap 3/16 inch (Bobrick, Bradley).
- Plastic: allow about 24 hours to acclimate (Bradley Bradmar).
- Typical bracket heights and panel elevations vary by maker and come from the shop drawings; don't publish them.
