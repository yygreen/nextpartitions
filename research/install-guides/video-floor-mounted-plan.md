# Floor mounted installation video, detailed version: plan

Built as `site-content/video/install-3d/install-fmd.js` / `install-fmd.html` / `render-all-fmd.sh`, on the pattern of the detailed overhead braced video (`install-obd.js`) and the hardware module (`hardware.js`). Research: `floor-mounted-check.md` and `consensus.md` in this folder; competitor comparison in `research/competitor-videos/one-point-partitions.md`.

Length 4:14 (254 s), 1440p, 30 fps. Ten steps. Every step with hardware opens with a kit card (studio scene, parts named underneath). Each kind of fastening is shown once in close-up, then the rest go on fast in a wide shot. Hardware is generic (simple shapes), not any manufacturer's design. No exact figures: heights, gaps and openings are "to the drawings".

What makes this style different, and gets its own shots: the anchors set in the concrete, the leveling nuts brought to one level line taken from the highest point of the floor, the shoe slid up and held while the pilaster comes down onto its anchors, top nuts finger tight until everything is plumb and level, no headrail (each pilaster stands on its anchors alone), and the push test at the end.

## Camera rules

- Kit cards cover the big cuts (into the anchor close-up, into the wall-bracket close-up, into the pilaster step, into the door step).
- Pilaster tops are at about 70 in this scene, the ceiling at 96: moving into or out of a stall, the camera goes over the line of pilasters and doors at about 84, directly above it, never through a panel, door or pilaster.
- Wall fixings sit on the tile face, 0.06 in front of the wall line.

## Hardware stack on each anchor (bottom to top)

Threaded anchor set in the concrete · washer and nut clamping it to the floor · leveling nut and washer at the common level line · the pilaster's leveling bracket · washer and top nut. The shoe covers all of it at the end.

## Left out on purpose

- Headrail, its brackets and the headrail kit (this style has none).
- Stiffeners (no floor mounted source uses them).
- Shoe clips on the anchors (one maker's detail); the shoe is "secured" with a screw shown generically.
