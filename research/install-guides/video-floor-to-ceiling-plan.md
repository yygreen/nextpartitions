Built from `consensus.md` section 4 (floor to ceiling), its general and safety sections, and the floor to ceiling guide in `site-content/design/install-guides/guides.mjs`. General points every style shares (all or most sources): solid backing behind every wall bracket; structure above the ceiling designed and installed by others before the ceiling is closed; reference heights from the highest point of a sloping floor; plumb with the leveling hardware, never by forcing panels; doors only once the openings are right; accessible door closes on its own; every latch must open from outside in an emergency; heavy parts need two people and temporary supports.

## Where sources differ

- **Finding the ceiling point.** One maker stands the pilaster in its fixed floor shoe to find where the ceiling bracket goes; the others mark both ends from the layout with a plumb bob or laser. The video uses the majority way (plumb up from the floor marks).
- **What the ceiling fixing goes into.** Threaded rods up to structure above the ceiling (two makers), or an angle bracket screwed through the ceiling into adequate structure (two makers plus AMPCO). The video shows a generic ceiling bracket bolted up into steel above the ceiling, drawn as an x-ray outline: the point is that the fixing goes into structure, not into the ceiling grid or board.
- **Pilaster length.** Made to the measured height; one steel system telescopes. All expect the ceiling height checked at every pilaster. The video shows a leveling bolt at the foot that raises the pilaster snug to the ceiling bracket.
- **Headrail.** None, except one maker's extended-privacy system. The video has none.
- **Shoe timing.** All slide the shoes on before the pilaster goes up; most seat them after tightening and before the doors. The video does that.

## Detailed video: about 4:08, ten steps, 16:9 at 1440p

Built to the overhead braced detailed pattern (`install-obd.js`) and the competitor notes in `research/competitor-videos/one-point-partitions.md`: a kit card opens every step that adds hardware, one fastening of each kind goes in on screen in close-up (hole, plug or anchor, bracket, screws or bolts), then the rest of the same parts go on quickly in a wide shot. What the competitor leaves out stays in: captions in plain words, labels, the backing and the structure above the ceiling, layout carried up to the ceiling, plumb guides, the gap at the top closing, door adjustment and a final check. Hardware is generic (`hardware.js`); no figures beyond "to the shop drawings".

The camera stays below the ceiling and never passes through a panel, door or pilaster: close-ups stay on outside faces, and the one view inside a stall goes in over a door (doors stop well below the ceiling) or through an opening before its door is hung.
