General points every style shares (all or most sources): solid backing behind every wall bracket; structure above the ceiling designed and installed by others before the ceiling is closed; reference heights from the highest point of a sloping floor; plumb with the leveling hardware, never by forcing panels; doors only once the openings are right; accessible door closes on its own; every latch must open from outside in an emergency; heavy parts need two people and temporary supports.

## Where sources differ

- **Finding the ceiling point.** One maker stands the pilaster in its fixed floor shoe to find where the ceiling bracket goes; the others mark both ends from the layout with a plumb bob or laser. The video uses the majority way (plumb up from the floor marks).
- **What the ceiling fixing goes into.** Threaded rods up to structure above the ceiling (two makers), or an angle bracket screwed through the ceiling into adequate structure (two makers plus AMPCO). The video shows a generic ceiling bracket bolted up into steel above the ceiling, drawn as an x-ray outline: the point is that the fixing goes into structure, not into the ceiling grid or board.
- **Pilaster length.** Made to the measured height; one steel system telescopes. All expect the ceiling height checked at every pilaster. The video shows a leveling bolt at the foot that raises the pilaster snug to the ceiling bracket.
- **Headrail.** None, except one maker's extended-privacy system. The video has none.
- **Shoe timing.** All slide the shoes on before the pilaster goes up; most seat them after tightening and before the doors. The video does that.
