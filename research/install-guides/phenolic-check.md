## 1. The material

- **Thinner than laminate, and two thicknesses.** Panels are thinner than pilasters and doors in every source that gives sizes (Bobrick G, ASI E, Scranton I; Bradley A and Metpar C size brackets to match). Laminate is one thickness throughout and thicker (Bobrick G). The model uses the common proportions (panel two thirds of the pilaster and door thickness, both well under laminate's); the video shows no figures.
- **Solid through, dark core at the edges.** Black core is standard (Bobrick G requires black edges; ASI E, F; General H); a colour-through core is an option (ASI F, General H). Edges are machined and eased or bevelled (ASI E, F).
- **Heavy.** Bradley A warns parts are heavy and may need more than one person; Wilsonart J the same for compact sheet.
- **Store flat.** Bradley A and ASI E: lay it flat, don't lean or stack unevenly, to avoid warping. (Bradley's "store upright" note belongs to its honeycomb product, not solid phenolic.)
- **No edge sealing.** No source asks for it: the edge is the solid core (our inference; no source says it outright, so the video doesn't claim it).
- **Care.** Mild soap and water, wipe dry (Metpar C; ASI D care page).

## 2. Hardware and fastening

- **Brackets.** Stainless U-shaped (stirrup) brackets are standard: ASI D/E (two-ear and U brackets), Bradley A (stirrup, standard), Metpar C (one- and two-ear brackets), General H. The bracket's opening is sized to the part it holds, so panel brackets are narrower than pilaster brackets (Bradley A, Metpar C). Full-height (continuous) stainless or aluminium brackets are an option (Bradley A, ASI D, F; Bobrick G for its heavy-duty option).
- **Into the phenolic, two ways:**
  - **Through-bolts** (a barrel nut from one side, a screw from the other) through panels, and through pilasters and doors for hinges and keepers: Bradley A, Metpar C (everywhere, "step bolts and barrel nuts"), ASI D/E. The hole is drilled through on site using the bracket as a template (Bradley A, Metpar C).
  - **Screws into pilot holes that stop short of the far face**: brackets onto pilasters, the latch, coat hook and headrail (Bradley A; ASI D uses sheet-metal screws for brackets on pilasters). Wilsonart J: leave material below a blind hole and back the far face when drilling through to stop break-out (Bradley A says the same with a support block).
  - **Factory threaded inserts** for hinges and latch, machine screws into them (Bobrick G, which forbids screwing straight into the core). Where sources disagree: Bobrick wants inserts; Bradley screws into pilot holes; Metpar and ASI through-bolt. The video shows through-bolts and pilot-hole screws, says nothing about inserts, and carries the line "follow the manufacturer's instructions".
- **Tamper-resistant heads** (Torx pin or button heads) on every fastener (Bradley A, Metpar C, ASI E, Bobrick G). The generic screws in the video don't show a drive type.
- **Pilaster foot.** A leveling bolt in the foot of the pilaster (Bradley A drills a pilot hole for a leveling screw; ASI D threads it into an insert; Metpar C a leveling bolt over an L-bracket). Floor fixing then differs: Bradley screws the shoe to the floor and stands the pilaster in it; Metpar sets L-brackets on floor plugs and bolts through them; ASI uses a floor stud and mounting bar. The video uses the Metpar-like route (floor L-brackets on plugs, leveling bolt, bolted through) because it shows each fixing clearly; it is generic and labelled as one way.
- **Hinges.** Surface-mounted (wrap-around or vault/cam type) through-bolted hinges are standard (Bradley A, ASI D/E/F, General H, Metpar C cam hinge); a continuous (piano) hinge is an option (Bradley A, ASI F). The rest position is set at the hinge (cam or pintle).
- **Shoe.** Slid onto the pilaster before it stands and fixed to it last (Bradley A, Metpar C, ASI D).
- **Headrail.** Same anti-grip aluminium rail as other materials, fixed to every pilaster and to the wall (Bradley A screws into pilot holes in the pilaster; Metpar C through-bolts).

## 3. Order of steps (phenolic, overhead braced)

Same consensus order as `consensus.md` section 1, with these phenolic points:

1. Check drawings, backing and parts; keep the material flat until it goes up (A, C, D, E).
2. Lay out pilaster line, panel lines, bracket heights from the highest floor point (A, C).
3. Wall brackets into backing, plugs and screws (A, C, D).
4. Floor fixings at each pilaster (A shoe, C L-brackets, D stud/bar).
5. Panels on 12-inch-ish blocks into the wall brackets, unfastened (A, C, D).
6. Prepare pilasters: shoe on and held up, leveling bolt in the foot, brackets screwed on through pilot holes (A, C, D).
7. Stand pilasters, plumb and level (leveling bolt), set door openings, then drill through the panel at the brackets and through-bolt; fix the floor brackets; panels to wall brackets (A, C, D).
8. Headrail to every pilaster and the wall (A, C, D).
9. Shoes down and fixed to the pilaster (A, C, D).
10. Hinges (through-bolted), doors, rest position, latch, keeper, coat hook, pull, bumper (A, C, D).
11. Test latches and emergency access; clean and wipe dry (C, D; consensus).

## 4. Cautions worth a caption

- Pilot holes stop short of the far face, so no screw tip shows (A, J).
- Drill through with a block behind the hole to stop break-out (A, J). Not shown in the video (a hand behind a panel is hard to show clearly); noted here.
- Field cutting and drilling: sharp carbide (or diamond) bits and blades (J); Bradley A lists a circular saw and jigsaw for headrail cuts only. Phenolic parts arrive cut to size; the video doesn't show cutting.
- Don't overtighten screws into phenolic (Fundermax via search summary only; not read, so not used).
