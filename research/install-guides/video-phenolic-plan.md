## What makes it a phenolic video

- **Material on screen:** thin solid parts, pale finish on the faces and a dark core on every edge (visible in every close-up), panels thinner than pilasters and doors. A material card at the start shows a panel, a pilaster and a door sample next to a thicker laminate sample with its particleboard core. No thickness figures on screen.
- **Hardware:** U-brackets whose opening fits the panel (wall and pilaster), floor L-brackets on plugs, a leveling bolt in the pilaster foot, through-bolts (barrel nut one side, screw the other) drilled on site through the panel with the bracket as a guide, screws into pilot holes that stop short of the far face, through-bolted hinges and keeper (barrel nuts visible on the far face), latch screwed into pilot holes.
- **Order:** pilasters are prepared (shoe on and held up, leveling bolt, brackets) before they stand, then bolted through once plumb, level and the openings are set.

## Captions (plain words, no signposting)

Intro: "Here is how phenolic partitions go together."
1. First, check the room against the approved drawings. / Every bracket needs solid backing inside the wall. / Phenolic is solid all the way through, with a dark core at the edges. / Panels are thinner than pilasters and doors. Keep it all flat until it goes up.
2. Mark the pilaster line across the front, / each panel line back to the wall, / and carry each panel line up the wall.
3. Wall bracket kit: U-brackets, screws and wall plugs. / Drill into the backing and push in a wall plug. / Screw the bracket to the wall. / Its opening is sized to fit the panel. / Repeat at each bracket height on every panel line.
4. Floor brackets: L-brackets, screws and floor plugs. / Drill the floor at each end of a pilaster and plug the holes. / Screw each bracket down on the pilaster line. / Repeat at every pilaster.
5. Rest each panel on blocks at the height on the drawings. / Phenolic is heavy: lift panels with two people. / Each panel drops into its wall brackets, unfastened for now.
6. Pilaster kit: U-brackets, screws, a leveling bolt and the shoe. / Slide the shoe on and hold it up. / Thread the leveling bolt into the foot. / Screw the brackets on through pilot holes. / Pilot holes stop short, so no screw shows on the other face. / Then stand each pilaster on its floor brackets, / with the panel between its brackets.
7. Level the pilaster tops with the leveling bolts. / Get each pilaster plumb both ways, / and set the door openings to the drawings. / Through-bolts: barrel nuts and screws. / Drill through the panel, using the bracket as a guide. / A barrel nut goes in from one side, / and a screw from the other. / Bolt each pilaster to its floor brackets, / and each panel to its wall brackets.
8. Headrail kit: pilaster brackets, screws, wall bracket and wall plugs. / Screw a bracket onto every pilaster top. / Slide the headrail over the brackets, / screw it to each one, / and fix its end to the wall.
9. Lower each shoe to the floor, / and screw it to the pilaster.
10. Door hardware: hinges, latch, keeper, pull, coat hook and through-bolts. / Bolt the hinge halves through the pilaster, / hang the door, / and bolt the other halves through the door. / Screw the latch on through pilot holes, and bolt the keeper through the pilaster. / Then the coat hook, / and the pull on the outside. / The accessible stall's door closes on its own. / Adjust each door until the gaps are even.
11. Test every latch, including from outside. / Check every fixing one last time. / Clean with mild soap and water, then wipe dry.
End: "Always follow the manufacturer's instructions for your order."

## Open points

- Wording (captions, the overview notes, "Opens from outside in an emergency") goes to the lawyer before the video is published.
- Fixing methods differ by brand (threaded inserts at Bobrick; through-bolts at Metpar and ASI; pilot-hole screws at Bradley); the floor fixing shown (L-brackets with a leveling bolt) is one of three in the sources. The video presents one generic way and points to the manufacturer's instructions.
- Not shown: drilling with a block behind the hole to stop break-out, cutting (parts arrive cut to size), continuous brackets and piano hinges (options).
- Hardware stays generic; no drive type (tamper-resistant heads) is modelled.
