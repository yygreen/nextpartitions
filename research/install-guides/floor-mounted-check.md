# Floor-mounted partitions (floor anchored, no headrail): research for the overview video

Read 2026-11-27: consensus.md section 2 plus the 11 extracted manuals in scratchpad/manuals1. Own words only; no figures carried over.

### Order of steps, own words
- **Bradley 500 (steel and phenolic)**: read drawings, count parts, confirm wall and floor backing → snap the pilaster line on the floor, mark panel centers on the back wall → plumb lines up the walls; from the highest floor point mark the bracket heights and carry them round the room with a level → wall brackets → brackets on the pilasters (phenolic: first slide the shoe on and tape it up, then bolt a leveling bracket to the pilaster foot) → drill the floor, set wedge anchors clamped down with a washer and nut, add a second (support) nut and washer on each → set every support nut to one height measured from the highest floor point → wall pilaster first, then alternate panel, pilaster along the run: panel on a temporary block into the wall brackets, shoe slid up and taped, pilaster dropped onto the anchors and into the brackets, washer, spacer nut and top nut on; check plumb and level to each other, adjust with the support nuts; drill and screw brackets → when all are up, lower each shoe flush to the floor and screw it to the pilaster → before hinges: openings right, all pilasters plumb and secured to the floor → hinges, doors, latch, keeper.
- **ASI Global steel**: floor must be concrete; review drawings and packing list, confirm wall and floor backing → mark pilaster line, panel lines, anchor points (near each pilaster edge) → drill wall and floor; wall brackets; wedge anchors set and tightened with a clip washer and nut → panel on a support, not fastened → U-brackets on the pilasters (wall pilasters none) → on each anchor a shoe clip, support nut about an inch up, round washer; squeeze the shoe straight, slide it up the pilaster; set pilaster on anchors, top nuts light → level each pilaster with its own nuts, plumb, fix to panel, slide the foot channel on the anchors to set the door opening, tighten top nuts, drop the shoe onto its clips, remove the support → doors. Tip: lay a headrail piece loosely across adjacent pilaster tops as a leveling aid.
- **Hadrian metal (with floor-mount appendix)**: front centerline from the drawings → drill floor stud holes near each pilaster edge, fix studs → wall brackets, panel on a block → shoe onto the wall pilaster, set it on its studs at a small gap above the floor, plumb and tighten, fix to wall brackets → hinges on it → test-fit next pilaster on its studs to mark the U-brackets, fix them → shoe on, set on studs, engage panel, plumb, fix → hang door, set rest angle, stop and keeper → next compartment ("one compartment, door included, at a time").

## 2. Consensus order (4 manufacturers: Bobrick, ASI Global, Hadrian, Bradley; AMPCO extra)

1. Confirm drawings, parts and that walls and floor can take the fixings (floor must be concrete for these anchors): 4 of 4 (+AMPCO).
2. Lay out pilaster line, panel lines and anchor points (two per pilaster, near each edge; one for narrow ones): 4 of 4.
3. Wall brackets at heights measured from the highest floor point: 4 of 4 for brackets; Bradley and AMPCO say "highest point" outright.
4. Drill and set the floor anchors with a lower (support) nut and washer: 4 of 4.
5. Brackets onto the pilasters: 4 of 4 (Bradley and Hadrian before standing; Hadrian test-fits on the studs to mark).
6. Panel on a temporary block, slid into the wall brackets, not fixed: 4 of 4.
7. Shoe slid up the pilaster and held there; pilaster lowered onto the studs and into the brackets; top washer and nut on, loose: 4 of 4.
8. Level on the nuts, plumb both ways, fix panel to pilaster, set the door opening, tighten: 4 of 4.
9. Shoe lowered to the floor and secured: 4 of 4 (timing differs, below).
10. Hang doors and hardware once openings are right and pilasters are fixed: 4 of 4.
11. Final checks and cleanup: Bobrick, ASI (+AMPCO); Bradley and Hadrian cover care and adjustment only.

Start point: from the wall (wall pilaster first): Bradley, Hadrian, Bobrick composite; ASI implied.

## 3. Where they differ

- **Pre-leveling anchors**: common height for all support nuts before any pilaster, taken from the highest floor point: **Bobrick, Bradley (2)**. Per pilaster as you go: **ASI, Hadrian (+AMPCO)**. All allow fine adjustment on the nuts afterwards.
- **Shoe timing**: dropped as each pilaster is tightened: ASI. After all pilasters, before doors: Bradley. At the very end: Bobrick (+AMPCO). Hadrian: not stated. So "before or after the doors" is a fair split; consensus.md's "3 of 3, timing varies" holds, but its step 10 position (after doors) is the minority.
- **Shoe fixing**: snaps onto clips on the anchor (ASI, Bobrick "clipped over the anchor"); screwed to the pilaster (Bradley).
- **Whole run vs compartment**: whole run then all doors: Bradley, ASI, Bobrick. Compartment by compartment with its door: Hadrian.
- **Order of anchors vs wall brackets**: Bobrick anchors first; ASI both in one step; Bradley wall and pilaster brackets before drilling the floor.
- **Wall-end stile support**: only Bobrick (a support bracket at the foot of very wide end stiles). Bradley instead offers short wall-hung pilasters (no floor anchor) as an option. Not in ASI or Hadrian.
- **Stiffeners**: no floor-mounted source mentions them. The stiffener in our files is Scranton's, on its overhead-braced and ceiling-hung plastic system. Leave stiffeners out of the floor-mounted video.

## 4. Hardware, generically (for the 3D model)

- **Anchor**: a threaded steel stud (wedge-type expansion anchor in a drilled hole; AMPCO: drop-in anchor plus threaded rod). Two per pilaster on the pilaster line, each near one edge.
- **Stack on each stud, bottom to top**: (a) washer and nut clamping the anchor down to the floor (Bradley, ASI; on ASI this washer carries a shoe clip); (b) **support/leveling nut** with a washer, spun up to a small height above the floor; (c) the **pilaster's foot**; (d) washer and **top locking nut** (Bradley adds a spacer nut). The pilaster is clamped between the support nut and the top nut, so turning the support nuts raises, lowers or tilts it.
- **Pilaster foot**: steel pilasters have a formed bottom channel/mounting plate with holes or slots for the studs (ASI calls it a mounting channel and slides it on the anchors to set the opening). Phenolic (Bradley) gets a bolted-on angle leveling bracket at the foot.
- **Shoe**: a thin stainless sleeve (open box) around the pilaster foot. It is slid up and taped or held while the pilaster is set, then dropped so its bottom edge sits flush on the floor, hiding studs, nuts and the small gap under the pilaster. Held by clips on the anchor or by a screw into the pilaster.
- **Wall connection**: ear or U brackets (or continuous channels) on the wall and on the pilaster back; panel sits in U brackets with a gap to wall and pilaster.

## 5. Visually showable, specific to floor mounted

- Chalk line on the floor for the pilaster face; anchor marks near each pilaster edge.
- Finding the highest floor point (floor falls to the drain), then a laser or string line/level carrying one height across all anchors; support nuts spun up to that line (Bobrick, Bradley).
- Hammer drill into concrete, dust blown out, anchor tapped in, clamped with the lower nut.
- Shoe slid up the pilaster and taped before the pilaster drops onto the studs.
- Pilaster lowered onto two studs; top nuts finger-tight; level on the face and the edge (plumb both ways), nudging support nuts.
- Straightedge laid loosely across neighbouring pilaster tops to check they're level with each other (ASI tip).
- Sliding the foot on the anchors to set the door opening, then tightening the top nuts.
- Shoe dropping to the floor and clicking onto its clips.
- Before doors: every pilaster plumb, tight, fixed to the floor (Bradley); a "no rocking" push test is our own framing, not stated in these manuals.

## 6. Corrections to consensus.md

1. **Coverage table and the "thinnest style" note are out of date.** Bradley Series 500 Floor-Braced (steel 02a, phenolic 02b) is a full floor-mounted procedure with no headrail; it is read now. Floor mounted becomes 4 (+1): Bobrick, ASI Global, Hadrian, Bradley.
2. **Pre-leveling**: Bradley also sets all support nuts to one height from the highest floor point first, so it's Bobrick + Bradley vs ASI + Hadrian (+AMPCO), not Bobrick alone.
3. **Shoe step**: Bradley lowers and screws the shoes before hanging doors; the consensus order puts shoes after doors. Suggest "shoes dropped and fixed once the pilasters are tight (before or after doors)".
4. **Hadrian line**: its numbered steps are headrail braced; floor mounted is a short appendix (studs, pilaster set at a small gap above the floor, plumb, tighten). The compartment-at-a-time advice comes from the main steps. Worth saying so.
5. **Phenolic detail missing**: slide the shoe on before bolting the leveling bracket to the pilaster foot; a bracket that overhangs stops the shoe dropping (Bradley 02b).
6. **Advice section**: add "wall bracket heights measured from the highest floor point" (Bradley) and the ASI straightedge-across-tops tip. Stiffeners don't belong in the floor-mounted section (Scranton only, other systems).
