## The right material for Ontario's climate

In most Ontario restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules an Ontario restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and California's own access rules both set the sizes, and California's are stricter in places. A remodel can also oblige you to bring the restrooms serving that area up to today's rules; on projects under $209,208 (the 2026 figure) that duty is limited.
- **Fire safety:** building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Ontario uses California's rules, which set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **California extras:** California asks more of a stall than the federal rules. In a large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, not just one. The accessible stall needs more open floor in front of the toilet, so it's deeper, and the aisle outside its door must be 48 inches wide, against 42. Stall doors need a privacy latch, and every restroom door carries California's circle or triangle symbol. Give your layout to your architect before you order.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be signed as all-gender. A shared all-gender room is allowed in place of separate men's and women's rooms if every toilet is in a floor-to-ceiling privacy compartment whose door locks from the inside and shows when it's occupied.
- **Permits:** Ontario follows California's list of work that needs no building permit. The list covers "painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, countertops and similar finish work" and movable "partitions not over 5 feet 9 inches" high. It doesn't name toilet partitions, so ask the Building Department's counter (909-395-2023) whether your replacement needs one. Building a new restroom in an existing commercial space is a tenant improvement, which goes through the city's plan check. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the City of Ontario Building Department or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls an Ontario restroom needs

Ontario counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and California's rules require urinals in the men's room. Every toilet needs a stall, and urinals need screens between them. The women's room must have at least as many toilets as the men's room has toilets and urinals together.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 2/1+1 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
100: 3/1+1 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
200: 4/2+1 | 3/2+1 | 4/2+1
400: 8/3+2 | 4/3+1 | 7/4+2
:::

These are minimums for new buildings, additions and changes of use that bring in more people. An office or shop with 50 people or fewer may have a single one-person restroom. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, California's plumbing rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, and a restaurant dining room or a classroom at one per 30 square feet. Your architect or the City of Ontario Building Department sets the final number.

## Installation in Ontario

Next Partitions supplies the partitions; a contractor you hire installs them. Every order comes with shop drawings: a plan of your room with every panel, door and pilaster sized, which the installer sets out from.

Need an installer? Our directory of [partition installers in Ontario](/installers/california/ontario) lists local installers. Ask for installation in your quote request and we collect their prices for your job.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Ontario

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Riverside–San Bernardino–Ontario area earn a median of $34.08 an hour, about 17% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** California requires the state-set wage on public jobs over $1,000, including city and school district work. For a carpenter in San Bernardino County that is $54.41 an hour plus $29.91 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 8.75% in Ontario: 7.25% statewide, 0.5% for the county transportation authority and 1% city.
- **Tax on installation:** in California, installation labor billed separately isn't taxed.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Ontario are taxable. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration.
