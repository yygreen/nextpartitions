# Plastic vs. Steel Bathroom Partitions: Pros, Cons & Best Uses

Plastic vs steel bathroom partitions is really a question of what's inside the door. A steel partition is a sandwich: two steel face sheets over a honeycomb core. A solid plastic partition is one sheet of plastic all the way through. That difference decides how each handles water, dents, graffiti, fire codes and cleaning.

Steel comes in two finishes, powder coated and stainless, and they suit very different restrooms. This guide compares solid plastic (HDPE) with both, using what manufacturers and the building code publish, then helps you choose.

## What "plastic" and "steel" mean here

"Plastic" partitions can mean three different things, and only one of them is solid plastic:

- **Solid plastic (HDPE):** one sheet of high-density polyethylene. This is what most people mean, and the focus of this guide.
- **Plastic laminate:** a thin decorative laminate bonded to a particleboard core. It looks like plastic, but the core is wood-based.
- **Phenolic:** layers of resin-soaked kraft paper fused into one dense sheet. It's sometimes lumped in with plastic, but it's a different material.

"Steel" or "metal" partitions mean powder coated steel (also called painted metal or baked enamel) or stainless steel. There's a short note on laminate and phenolic near the end.

## How each is built

![Solid plastic (HDPE), powder coated steel and stainless steel partition doors cut through and seen edge on at the same scale](/images/diagrams/material-compare-plastic-steel.svg)

The drawing above cuts a door of each through, edge on and at the same scale. The two steels share a honeycomb core; solid plastic is one sheet.

**Solid plastic (HDPE)** has no core, no skin and no edge band. Doors, panels and pilasters are typically 1" thick, with the color running through and eased or rounded edges.

![Cross-section of a solid plastic (HDPE) partition door edge: one homogeneous 1 inch sheet with a rounded edge](/images/diagrams/material-section-solid-plastic.svg)

1. **Core:** none. The whole 1" thickness is one homogeneous HDPE sheet.
2. **Faces:** the same HDPE with the color running through, so there is no separate finish layer.
3. **Edge:** the same sheet, with no edge band, eased or rounded.

**Powder coated steel** has galvannealed steel faces over a honeycomb core, finished with a baked-on powder coating. Doors and panels are typically 1" thick, with 1-1/4" pilasters. A formed steel molding closes the edges, and corners are welded or reinforced.

![Cross-section of a powder coated steel partition door edge: honeycomb core between two powder coated steel faces, closed by a formed steel edge](/images/diagrams/material-section-powder-coated-steel.svg)

1. **Core:** honeycomb.
2. **Faces:** galvannealed steel sheets with a baked powder coat finish.
3. **Edge:** a formed steel edge around the perimeter that locks the two faces together.

**Stainless steel** uses the same sandwich with Type 304 stainless faces, in a brushed or textured finish.

![Cross-section of a stainless steel partition door edge: honeycomb core between two Type 304 stainless faces, closed by a formed stainless edge](/images/diagrams/material-section-stainless-steel.svg)

1. **Core:** honeycomb.
2. **Faces:** Type 304 stainless steel, in a brushed or textured finish.
3. **Edge:** a formed edge around the perimeter, where the stainless faces are locked or welded together around the core.

## Water and humidity

- **HDPE** can't rust: there's no metal in the panel. It's highly resistant to moisture, mildew and common cleaning agents, and it can be hosed down.
- **Powder coated steel** can rust where the coating is damaged. Keep it to dry, climate-controlled restrooms.
- **Stainless steel** resists corrosion, but it isn't rust-proof everywhere. In the unwashed, humid air above indoor chlorinated pools, stainless rods, bolts and wires under tension have cracked. Doors and rails that get washed haven't shown the problem, but ceiling-hung stalls hang from stressed rods and bolts. Material guides rate stainless low for wet areas.

For showers, pools and locker rooms, solid plastic is the safer of the three.

## Dents, scratches and graffiti

**Steel dents.** Both powder coated and stainless steel can dent and scratch. Graffiti is harder to deal with on painted metal than on other materials, but painted metal can be repainted. On stainless, a textured finish adds vandal resistance.

**HDPE won't easily dent**, but it has the softest surface of the common materials, so it scratches easily. The color runs through, so a scratch doesn't show a different layer. Graffiti usually wipes off with a nonabrasive cleaner. Xylene-based markers are the exception: they can "ghost" into the plastic.

:::expert
In a busy public restroom, what makes you recommend stainless steel over solid plastic, or the other way round?
> I almost always recommend solid plastic. It won't rust or dent, it shrugs off moisture and graffiti, and it costs less. Stainless makes sense in high-visibility spaces like airports, stadiums and upscale offices, where the look matters and the budget allows.
— Abe Gold, CEO, Next Partitions
:::

## Fire ratings and the building code

The building and fire codes regulate toilet partitions as interior finish. The fire performance you need depends on the building's occupancy and where the restroom sits, so confirm it with your architect or local code official.

- **Powder coated steel** generally carries a Class A rating under ASTM E84 (flame spread index of 25 or less). Painted metal has tested as low as 5.
- **Stainless steel** is generally Class A too, but several manufacturers publish no class. Ask for the test data.
- **HDPE** follows a different rule. The IBC (Section 803.9 in the 2015 and 2018 editions) requires HDPE used as interior finish to pass the NFPA 286 room-corner fire test. Untreated HDPE tested as unclassified under ASTM E84 because of its smoke. Fire-rated HDPE is available, but not every color passes, and rated colors can cost extra.

Powder coated steel is the simpler choice where the fire class is strict, since it's published as Class A. With HDPE, specify the fire-rated version and ask for the test report for your exact color and thickness.

## Weight and mounting styles

**Steel is offered in all four mounting styles**, in both finishes: [overhead braced](/mounting-styles/overhead-braced), [floor mounted](/mounting-styles/floor-mounted), [ceiling hung](/mounting-styles/ceiling-hung) and [floor to ceiling](/mounting-styles/floor-to-ceiling). Floor-anchored versions may need concrete floors. Some overhead-braced steel lines come in 69" and 72" heights.

**HDPE is not offered in every style.** Typical lines come overhead braced and floor to ceiling, with ceiling hung on some lines. Several manufacturers don't recommend floor-anchored or ceiling-hung mounting for solid plastic.

Ceiling-hung stalls of any material need a ceiling structure that can carry them. Manufacturers rarely publish steel or HDPE partition weights, so ask for the load before you choose ceiling-hung mounting. The parts aren't interchangeable either: one manufacturer sizes its headrail at 1-1/4" for steel and 1" for solid plastic.

## How the hardware is fixed

- **Powder coated steel:** hardware is typically chrome-plated zamac (a die-cast zinc alloy), with stainless as an option. One published specification attaches it with tamper-proof Torx pin-head through bolts. Some lines offer a concealed hinge on powder coated partitions.
- **Stainless steel:** hardware and leveling shoes are typically 18-8 Type 304 stainless with a satin finish.
- **HDPE:** hinges, latches and brackets are usually aluminum or stainless. One published specification through-bolts 8" aluminum wrap-around hinges to the doors and pilasters with stainless Torx-head sex bolts.

Hardware often has its own warranty, from 5 years on chrome-plated zamac up to lifetime on stainless hardware. See [hinges](/hardware/hinges) and [replacement parts](/hardware/parts).

## Colors and looks

- **Powder coated steel:** a range of solid colors in a finish that resists wear, fading and scuffing. It can be repainted later.
- **Stainless steel:** satin brushed (#4) or textured patterns such as leather grain, diamond or embossed. Some lines use recycled stainless.
- **HDPE:** color all the way through and soft, rounded edges, but a more limited color range than laminate.

## Warranty

Warranties are set by the brand and product line. Published terms differ a lot by material:

- **HDPE:** 15 to 25 years, and 25 is common.
- **Powder coated steel:** 1 to 4 years.
- **Stainless steel:** 5 to 15 years. The longest terms are against rust-out.

Vandalism is usually excluded, installation errors or improper care can void coverage, and some warranties cover material only, with no labor. Your quote names the warranty for your line.

## Cost

Prices depend on brand, line, size and options, so use your quote. One manufacturer's published price index sets painted metal at 1.0, untreated HDPE at 1.9 and stainless steel at 2.7. On that index, stainless costs about 2.7 times as much as painted metal, and HDPE sits in between. Fire-rated HDPE colors can cost more than untreated ones.

## Cleaning

- **HDPE:** warm water and mild liquid soap, and hose down if needed. No abrasive pads or caustic solutions.
- **Powder coated steel:** warm water and very mild soap on a well wrung-out, nonabrasive cloth, then rinse and dry. Never use brushes, steel wool or scouring pads, and keep acid, alkali and bleach-based cleaners off the finish.
- **Stainless steel:** warm water on a damp cloth, wiping along the grain, then dry. A mild detergent or a stainless cleaner handles stubborn marks. No steel wool or scouring pads, which can leave rust stains, and no bleach-based bathroom cleaners, which can leave brown spots.

Follow your manufacturer's care instructions for any of them.

## Best uses by building type

- **Powder coated steel:** offices, retail, restaurants, community centers and other dry restrooms where budget matters more than abuse resistance.
- **Stainless steel:** airports, hospitals and upscale offices, where the restroom is part of the building's finish.
- **HDPE:** schools, universities, gyms, locker rooms, pool and shower areas, and public restrooms where moisture and graffiti are the main concerns, with the fire-rated version specified.

One manufacturer's building-type guide points the same way. It rates HDPE good for heavy-traffic buildings such as K-12 schools, malls and recreation centers, where it rates both steels poor. For standard-use buildings such as offices, healthcare and hospitality, it rates painted metal and HDPE good and stainless poor. For prestige buildings, stainless is its best choice and HDPE and painted metal are poor.

## A short note on laminate and phenolic

![Five partition doors cut through and seen edge on at the same scale: phenolic, solid plastic (HDPE), plastic laminate, powder coated steel and stainless steel](/images/diagrams/material-sections-compare.svg)

**Plastic laminate** is often the cheapest "plastic" option and has the widest color choice, but its particleboard core can swell with too much moisture, and published fire ratings are Class B or C. Keep it in dry restrooms, like powder coated steel. See [plastic laminate partitions](/partitions/plastic-laminate).

**Phenolic** is a solid, hard material for wet and high-traffic rooms, offered in all four mounting styles and rated Class A or B by line. If you're choosing between solid materials, read [HDPE vs phenolic partitions](/blog/hdpe-vs-phenolic-partitions).

## Which should you choose?

**Choose powder coated steel if:**

- The restroom is dry and climate controlled.
- Budget is the main driver.
- You want Class A without extra testing, and the option to repaint later.

**Choose stainless steel if:**

- The restroom is part of a high-end interior.
- You want a metal look with corrosion resistance, and the budget allows.
- It's not a pool hall, or the manufacturer has approved the hanger and fastener grade for one.

**Choose solid plastic (HDPE) if:**

- The room gets wet, humid or hosed down.
- Graffiti and rust are bigger worries than scratches.
- Overhead-braced or floor-to-ceiling mounting suits your building, and you specify a fire-rated grade.

## FAQ

### Are plastic or metal bathroom partitions better?
It depends on the room. Solid plastic (HDPE) can't rust and suits wet, high-traffic restrooms. Powder coated steel is the budget choice for dry restrooms. Stainless steel suits high-end interiors.

### Do plastic bathroom partitions last longer than steel?
Published HDPE warranties run from 15 to 25 years, against 1 to 4 years for powder coated steel and 5 to 15 years for stainless. HDPE won't rust or easily dent, but it scratches more easily than steel.

### Which is cheaper, plastic or steel partitions?
Powder coated steel. One manufacturer's price index puts painted metal at 1.0, untreated HDPE at 1.9 and stainless steel at 2.7. Your quote gives actual prices for your line.

### Are plastic partitions fire rated?
Steel is generally Class A. HDPE must pass the NFPA 286 room-corner test under the IBC, and untreated HDPE doesn't, so specify a fire-rated grade and ask for the test report.

### Can stainless steel partitions be used near a pool?
With care. Stainless rods, bolts and wires under tension have cracked in the unwashed air above indoor chlorinated pools. Choose HDPE or phenolic there, or ask the manufacturer which grade it recommends for hangers and fasteners.

### Is plastic laminate the same as solid plastic?
No. Plastic laminate is a thin laminate bonded to a particleboard core that can swell when wet. Solid plastic (HDPE) is one sheet of plastic all the way through.
