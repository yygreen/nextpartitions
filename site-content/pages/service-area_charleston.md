## The right material for Charleston's climate

Charleston gets about 44 inches of rain a year, on roughly 107 days. In most Charleston restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Charleston restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and South Carolina's own access standard set the sizes. There is no separate state registration: the local building department reviews the plans, and the state reviews plans for public schools and state buildings.
- **Fire safety:** South Carolina's building rules treat toilet partitions as an interior finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) used that way to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** South Carolina sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building, and Charleston enforces the state's numbers without changes of its own. The table below shows what that means.
- **South Carolina extras:** the state's access standard asks for more than the federal one in a few places. The accessible stall needs a vertical grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal bars, and the gap under its partitions must be 12 inches high where the federal rule asks for 9. A room with six or more toilets and urinals also needs a narrower stall with grab bars on both sides, as under federal law.
- **New rules on January 1, 2027:** South Carolina moves to newer editions of its building and plumbing rules that day, and Charleston enforces them from then. The toilet numbers for offices, restaurants and schools and the fire test for solid plastic partitions stay the same. If your permit application falls close to that date, ask the City of Charleston Permit Center which edition applies.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. South Carolina's building rules require separate men's and women's rooms, and the state removed the option of meeting that with a shared all-gender room with stalls. The rules don't say whether one may be added on top of the required rooms, so ask your building department. Since May 2026, state law requires shared restrooms in public schools and public colleges to be designated for one sex, and each school must offer a single-user restroom or an accommodation.
- **Permits:** South Carolina leaves permit procedures to each city. Charleston's commercial alteration permit covers interior renovations, including ones with no floor plan changes. In the city's flood zones (AE and VE on the flood map), the City says all work on an existing building needs a permit, non-structural interior renovations included. The City's permit pages don't name toilet partitions, so ask the City of Charleston Permit Center (843-577-5550) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the City of Charleston Permit Center or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Charleston restroom needs

South Carolina counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs its own stall with a door. Urinals are optional: they can take the place of up to half of the men's toilets in an office, and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, so the table shows the men's side as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. An office that holds 25 people or fewer, and any building or tenant space that holds 15 or fewer, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. A restaurant counts its seasonal outdoor seating in its capacity.

To find your building's capacity, South Carolina's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. When a business applies for its certificate of occupancy, Charleston's Building Inspections Division assigns the maximum number of people for the space and checks that it has enough toilets.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Charleston

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Charleston–North Charleston area earn a median of $24.22 an hour, about 17% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** South Carolina has no state wage law for public construction, so a set wage applies only on federally funded jobs, under the federal Davis-Bacon Act. For a carpenter on building work in Charleston County that federal rate is $16.03 an hour plus $1.16 in benefits, a figure taken from a 2011 wage survey.
- **Sales tax:** 9% in Charleston: 6% state plus 3% in Charleston County taxes, which are 1% local option, 1% for school buildings and the rest for transportation. The part of the city in Berkeley County is also at 9%.
- **Tax on installation:** in South Carolina, a seller who also installs doesn't charge tax on installation labor when it is shown separately on the bill and is reasonable. A contractor who supplies and installs under a construction contract pays the tax when buying the partitions and doesn't add sales tax to the contract.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Charleston are taxable. Freight is taxed when the seller delivers to your site and isn't taxed when the goods ship by common carrier with the sale made at the shipping point. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the South Carolina Department of Revenue.
