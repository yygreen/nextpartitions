## The right material for Durham's climate

The nearest long-term weather station, at Raleigh, records about 46 inches of rain a year, on roughly 114 days, and relative humidity averages 83% in the morning and 53% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Durham restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. A restroom with six or more toilets and urinals combined also needs a narrower stall with grab bars on both sides. Federal law and North Carolina's building rules set the sizes, and the state rules are the same in every city.
- **Fire safety:** North Carolina's building rules count toilet partitions as an interior finish, and solid plastic (HDPE) or polypropylene used as an interior finish must pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** North Carolina's plumbing rules set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. They apply in every city in the state, and Durham's City-County Building & Safety Department enforces them. The table below shows what that means.
- **North Carolina extras:** the state's rules add a vertical grab bar, at least 18 inches long, on the side wall of an accessible stall, which the federal rules don't ask for. They place the toilet paper holder by its distance from the back wall, and the federal rules place it by its distance from the front of the toilet, so the holder has to sit where both are met. In a large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible. Restaurants, theaters and stores that need six or more toilets must also have a family restroom.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** North Carolina's rules call for separate men's and women's restrooms, with signs, once a space holds more than 25 people (30 for an office, 100 for a store). Where each side needs only one toilet, two single-user rooms open to anyone may be used instead, and a family restroom doesn't have to be marked for one sex. The rules in force don't provide for a shared all-gender room with several stalls, so ask the Durham City-County Building & Safety Department before planning one. State law leaves the question of who may use a multi-stall restroom to the General Assembly: state agencies, cities, counties and school boards may not set their own rules.
- **Permits:** the Durham City-County Building & Safety Department issues building permits for both the city and the county. Under North Carolina law, work in a commercial building that costs $40,000 or less needs no permit unless it involves load-bearing structure, a change to the plumbing, heating, air conditioning or electrical systems, materials the building rules don't allow, new roofing or anything the fire rules cover. The list doesn't name toilet partitions. Ask the department (919-560-4144) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Durham City-County Building & Safety Department or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Durham restroom needs

North Carolina counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall with a door, and every urinal needs a privacy screen. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may take the place of up to half of the required toilets in an office, and up to 67% in a restaurant or school, so the table shows toilets only.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 2/2
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 4/4
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 8/8
:::

The school column counts students through grade 8; in grades 9 to 12 the boys' side needs 7 at 400. A public school counts its students by classroom, counts staff separately, and needs at least four toilets or urinals in each student group restroom. An office with 30 people or fewer, or any other space with 25 or fewer, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

North Carolina's building rules count an office at one person per 100 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20, and a public school is counted at 29 students per classroom through grade 8 and 33 in grades 9 to 12.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Durham

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Durham–Chapel Hill area earn a median of $24.78 an hour, about 15% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** North Carolina has no state law setting wages on public construction, so a required wage applies only on federally funded or assisted projects over $2,000. For a carpenter on building work in Durham County that rate is $19.74 an hour plus $3.63 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 7.5% in Durham: 4.75% state, 2.25% Durham County and 0.5% transit.
- **Tax on installation:** in North Carolina, installation labor is taxed, even when it's billed separately, if the job is a repair, replacement or installation on its own. When the work is part of new construction, a remodel or another capital improvement, the contractor pays the tax on the materials and doesn't add sales tax to the contract, and normally keeps a signed capital improvement affidavit (Form E-589CI) on file.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Durham are taxable, freight included. An out-of-state seller with more than $100,000 a year in North Carolina sales must collect the tax. If the seller doesn't collect it, the buyer owes use tax to the state at the same rate.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the North Carolina Department of Revenue.
