# How to Measure for Commercial Toilet Partitions

To measure for toilet partitions you need six things from the room, and a few more from the old stalls if you're replacing them. With these numbers, a quote can be built on your real room, and the shop drawings that follow have fewer surprises.

## What you need

- A 25-foot tape measure, and a second person for long walls.
- A phone for photos. Keep the tape in the shot where you can.
- A sketch of the room seen from above. A hand sketch is fine.

Write every measurement to the nearest 1/8", in inches. Measure to the finished surface: the face of the tile, not the wall behind it.

## Step 1: Measure the room from above

Sketch the room as if you were looking down at it, then add these four measurements:

![Plan of an empty restroom seen from above, with the stall wall run, room depth, entry door and toilet centerlines lettered](/images/diagrams/measure-new-restroom-plan.svg)

- **A.** Room width along the wall where the stalls go.
- **B.** Room depth, from the stall wall to the opposite wall.
- **D.** Entry door location and which way it swings.
- **E.** Toilet centerlines, measured from the side wall.

A and E decide how many stalls fit and where each pilaster goes. D decides where stall doors can swing and what a person sees from the entry.

A few habits make these numbers reliable:

- **Measure each toilet from the same side wall.** Give the distance from that wall to the center of each toilet, one after another, not the gaps between toilets. One starting point means one small error doesn't add up along the wall.
- **Measure the wall in two places.** Walls are rarely perfectly straight or plumb. Measure width A at the floor and again at about door height, and send both numbers if they differ.
- **Mark anything on the walls or floor.** Windows, columns, pipes, radiators, floor drains, a tile base or a change in wall finish partway up. Note where each one is and how far it sticks out.

## Step 2: Measure the room from the side

Now look at the room as if it were cut through. This view is about height and structure: what the partitions stand on, and what they can hang or brace from.

![Section through a restroom showing ceiling height and the floor and ceiling construction](/images/diagrams/measure-new-restroom-section.svg)

- **C.** Ceiling height, floor to finished ceiling.
- **F.** Floor construction (slab, tile over slab) and the structure above the ceiling (open steel, concrete deck, drop ceiling).

C and F decide the mounting style. Overhead braced asks the least of the building. Floor mounted needs structural concrete at every pilaster. Ceiling hung needs structural steel above the finished ceiling at every pilaster, and floor to ceiling needs both a concrete floor and structure at the ceiling. If you don't know what is above a drop ceiling, lift a tile and take a photo. Compare the four styles in [ceiling hung vs floor mounted vs floor to ceiling](/blog/ceiling-hung-vs-floor-mounted-vs-floor-to-ceiling).

## Step 3: Check how the stalls will fit

A row of stalls is the sum of its compartments, so the count comes from width A. In this example, an accessible compartment 60-1/2" wide, an ambulatory compartment 36-1/2" wide and two standard compartments 36" wide add up to 169" along the wall:

![Plan view of a row of toilet compartments, with the accessible, ambulatory and standard compartments](/images/diagrams/sheet-plan.svg)

You don't have to work out the layout yourself. But two quick checks tell you early whether the room has a problem:

- **Do the toilets suit the stalls?** A standard compartment is typically 36" wide, so in a row of standard stalls the toilets sit about 36" apart, center to center. If yours are closer or uneven, say so. The layout can often adjust, but we need to know.
- **Is there room for the accessible stall?** A wheelchair accessible compartment is at least 60" wide, and its toilet centerline sits 16" to 18" from the side wall or partition. Measure that one toilet carefully.

The sizes behind these checks are in our [bathroom stall dimensions guide](/guides/bathroom-stall-dimensions), and the full layout method is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## Step 4: If you're replacing existing stalls

Old stalls are a useful template, because their pilasters already sit where the toilets need them. Record these as well:

- Each compartment's width and depth.
- Each pilaster's position, measured from the same side wall.
- The door widths, and which way each door swings and hinges.
- The thickness of the doors, panels and pilasters.
- The mounting style, the material, and the brand if you can find a label.

If only a door is being replaced, measure the door itself, not the opening:

![How to measure a bathroom stall door for a replacement](/images/diagrams/measure-replacement-door.svg)

- **A** Door width
- **B** Door height
- **C** Opening between pilasters
- **D** Floor to door bottom
- **E** Door thickness
- **F** Swing and hinge side

Door widths aren't round numbers, so write down exactly what the tape shows, to the nearest 1/16". There is more on matching an old door on the [replacement doors page](/hardware/doors), and on whether to repair or replace in [when to replace bathroom partitions](/blog/when-to-replace-bathroom-partitions).

## Step 5: Check the accessible stall

Replacing or adding stalls is the moment to check the accessible compartment, because its size and its door are fixed by the 2010 ADA Standards:

![Plan of a wheelchair accessible toilet compartment at minimum size, with the minimum area, toilet centerline, grab bars, door clear width and door location](/images/diagrams/ada-wheelchair-stall.svg)

- **Width:** at least 60".
- **Depth:** at least 56" with a wall-hung toilet, or 59" with a floor-mounted one.
- **Toilet centerline:** 16" to 18" from the side wall or partition.
- **Door:** at least 32" of clear opening, and it can't swing into the minimum compartment area.

If your measurements don't leave room for this, say so in your quote request. It's much easier to solve on paper than on site. The full rules are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance).

## What to send for a quote

1. Your sketch with measurements A to F.
2. Each toilet's centerline from the side wall, and whether the toilets are wall-hung or floor-mounted.
3. Photos of the whole wall, the floor and the ceiling, and of the old stalls and their hardware if there are any.
4. The number of stalls and urinal screens you want.
5. The material and mounting style you prefer, if you have a preference.

## After you send your measurements

Your measurements produce a quote and then shop drawings: a plan of your room with every panel, door and pilaster sized. Check the drawings against the room before you approve them, because the partitions are made from the approved drawings. On site, the installer sets out the job from those drawings and compares them with the room before starting. ## FAQ

### How do you measure for toilet partitions?
Measure the wall where the stalls go, the room depth, the ceiling height and each toilet's centerline from one side wall. Note the entry door and what the floor and ceiling are made of. Add photos.

### Do I measure to the wall or to the tile?
To the finished surface. If the wall is tiled, measure to the face of the tile, because that's the surface the partitions meet.

### How wide is a standard bathroom stall?
Typically 36" wide. A wheelchair accessible compartment is at least 60" wide, and an ambulatory accessible compartment is 35" to 37" wide.

### How do I measure a stall door for replacement?
Measure the door itself, not the opening: width, height, thickness and the gap from the floor to the door bottom. Note which side the hinges are on and which way the door swings.

### Do I need exact measurements to get a quote?
Close measurements are enough for a quote. Exact ones matter for the shop drawings, and the installer checks the room against the approved drawings before starting.

### Does Next Partitions measure my restroom?
No. We work from your measurements, plans and photos. Your installer verifies the measurements on site before starting.
