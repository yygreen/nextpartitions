## The right material for Seattle's climate

Seattle gets about 39 inches of rain a year, spread over roughly 156 days, and relative humidity averages 82% at 4 a.m. and 63% at 4 p.m. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, marinas, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## Delivery to Seattle job sites

Freight is quoted with the partitions, so the number you get covers delivery to your Seattle address.

**Downtown Seattle:** inside the Downtown Traffic Control Zone, trucks 30 feet or longer can't move during the weekday curfews (6 to 9 a.m. and 3 to 7 p.m.) and need a city permit between 9 a.m. and 3 p.m. Tell us if your site is downtown, so the carrier can plan the delivery window.

## The rules a Seattle restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Washington's own access rules set the sizes.
- **Fire safety:** Seattle's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** the number of toilets and urinals depends on how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means. The county health department, Public Health – Seattle & King County, enforces the plumbing rules.
- **Washington extras:** stalls here need a second, upright grab bar on the side wall, and a wheelchair stall needs more toe room under its partitions (12 inches high, against 9 under the federal rules). In a large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, not just one. Large stores and venues also need a family restroom.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** one-person restrooms in public places must be open to anyone and signed that way. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet and urinal is in its own compartment with full-height walls and a door that locks from the inside.
- **Permits:** Seattle needs no permit for minor repairs or alterations that cost $6,000 or less in any 6-month period, counting labor and parts at market value. Above that, ask the Seattle Department of Construction and Inspections (SDCI) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Last checked November 23, 2026. Rules change, so confirm your project with SDCI or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Seattle restroom needs

Seattle counts toilets by the number of people the building holds, half men and half women. Every toilet needs a stall. Washington sets its own count for schools, with more toilets for girls than for boys. Urinals are optional and may stand in for most of the men's toilets.

|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 1 women's stall<br>1 men's stall | 1 women's stall<br>1 men's stall | 1 women's stall<br>1 men's stall |
| 100 | 2 women's stalls<br>2 men's stalls | 1 women's stall<br>1 men's stall | 2 women's stalls<br>2 men's stalls |
| 200 | 3 women's stalls<br>3 men's stalls | 2 women's stalls<br>2 men's stalls | 4 women's stalls<br>3 men's stalls |
| 400 | 5 women's stalls<br>5 men's stalls | 3 women's stalls<br>3 men's stalls | 8 women's stalls<br>6 men's stalls |

Small businesses may be allowed one shared restroom: up to 25 people in an office and up to 30 in a restaurant. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. Confirm your project with SDCI.

Not sure how many people your building holds? The building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor and a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet. A school is counted at one student per 100 square feet of the whole building. Your architect or SDCI sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Seattle

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Seattle–Tacoma–Bellevue area earn a median of $36.74 an hour, about 26% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Washington requires the state-set wage on every public job, with no minimum project size. The Department of Labor & Industries publishes the current King County carpenter rate in its online wage lookup.
- **Sales tax:** 10.55% in Seattle (6.5% state and 4.05% local), rising to 10.66% on January 1, 2027.
- **Tax on installation:** in Washington, installation labor is taxed like the partitions themselves, at the job site's rate.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Seattle are taxable, freight included. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

Figures checked November 24, 2026. How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Washington Department of Revenue.
