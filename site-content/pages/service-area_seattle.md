## The right material for Seattle's climate

Seattle gets about 39 inches of rain a year, spread over roughly 156 days, and relative humidity averages 82% at 4 a.m. and 63% at 4 p.m. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, marinas, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## Delivery to Seattle job sites

Toilet partitions for Seattle ship by freight carrier, crated, palletized and tracked. Delivery is curbside, with a liftgate on request if the site has no loading dock. Tell us in your quote request:

- **The delivery address,** and whether it's a job site, a warehouse or an occupied building.
- **Dock or liftgate.** A building without a dock needs a liftgate truck.
- **Who receives it.** Crates are heavy: a published 3-stall powder coated steel kit weighs about 300 lb.
- **Where the crates wait.** Store panels flat, not leaned against a wall. Solid plastic may need about 24 hours at room temperature before it's installed, so check the manufacturer's instructions.

Freight is quoted with the partitions, so the number you get covers delivery to your Seattle address.

**Downtown Seattle:** inside the Downtown Traffic Control Zone, trucks 30 feet or longer can't move during the weekday curfews (6 to 9 a.m. and 3 to 7 p.m.) and need a city permit between 9 a.m. and 3 p.m. Tell us if your site is downtown, so the carrier can plan the delivery window.

## The rules a Seattle restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Washington's own access rules set the sizes.
- **Fire safety:** Seattle's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** the number of toilets and urinals depends on how many people use the building, and that decides how many stalls you need. The county health department, Public Health – Seattle & King County, enforces the plumbing rules.
- **Permits:** Seattle needs no permit for minor repairs or alterations that cost $6,000 or less in any 6-month period, counting labor and parts at market value. Above that, ask the Seattle Department of Construction and Inspections (SDCI) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Last checked November 23, 2026. Rules change, so confirm your project with SDCI or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).
