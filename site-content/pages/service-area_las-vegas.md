## The right material for the Las Vegas climate

Las Vegas gets about 4 inches of rain a year, on roughly 26 days, and relative humidity averages 36% in the morning and 20% in the afternoon. The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Las Vegas restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes. Nevada has no separate access rules of its own for private buildings: the city or county building department checks access under its building rules. State law requires public buildings to meet the federal standards.
- **Fire safety:** Las Vegas's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) used as an interior finish to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version, and ask the Las Vegas Building & Safety Division how it applies the rule to partitions.
- **How many stalls:** Las Vegas sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Nevada extras:** in a building owned by the state, a county, a city or a school district, each entrance to a corridor that leads to a restroom needs a sign with symbols, raised letters and Braille. The sign says who may use the restroom and, where it is for one gender, points to the one for the other gender. A building used by the public that has a public restroom and was built on or after October 1, 2017 needs at least one baby changing table, unless its permit or license restricts the admission of children by age.
- **City or county:** these are the City of Las Vegas's rules. The city asks you to check that your address is inside the city limits before you apply for a permit, and its permit page links to a map that shows which building department covers an address.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** in a business open to the public or a public building, Nevada law says a one-person restroom may not carry a men's or women's sign. Las Vegas's building rules let it be marked "All-Gender Bathroom" or "All-Accessible Bathroom" and count it toward the toilets the building needs. A parent with a child, a person with a caregiver and a person of any gender identity must be able to use it. For a shared all-gender room, the city counts toilets from the building's whole capacity instead of half for each sex, so ask the Las Vegas Building & Safety Division what it expects of the stalls. Nevada law bars discrimination in places open to the public on the ground of sex or gender identity or expression.
- **Permits:** Las Vegas requires a permit to alter a building unless the work is on its exempt list. The list covers "partitions that do not exceed 5 feet 9 inches in height" and "similar finish work" without naming toilet partitions, so ask the Las Vegas Building & Safety Division whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Las Vegas Building & Safety Division or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Las Vegas restroom needs

Las Vegas counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. Urinals are optional: in each restroom they may replace up to two thirds of the required men's toilets in a restaurant or school and up to half in an office, and each urinal needs a privacy screen. An office with 25 or fewer people, or any building or tenant space with 15 or fewer, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. Casino gaming floors have their own count: one toilet per 100 men and one per 50 women for the first 400 of each. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Las Vegas

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Las Vegas–Henderson–North Las Vegas area earn a median of $29.99 an hour, about 3% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on state, county, city and school district projects estimated at $100,000 or more, Nevada law requires at least the wage the state Labor Commissioner sets for the region each year from October 1. For a journeyman carpenter in the Clark County region that is $83.02 an hour, benefits included.
- **Sales tax:** 8.375% in Las Vegas: the 6.85% statewide rate plus 1.525% in Clark County taxes.
- **Tax on installation:** in Nevada, installation labor is not taxed when the invoice lists it separately from the partitions. A contractor who supplies and installs partitions as part of the building pays the tax on the materials when buying them.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Las Vegas are taxable. Freight listed separately on the invoice is not taxed, but handling, crating and packing charges are. An out-of-state seller with more than $100,000 in Nevada sales, or 200 or more Nevada sales, in a year collects the tax. If the seller doesn't collect it, the buyer owes use tax to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Nevada Department of Taxation.
