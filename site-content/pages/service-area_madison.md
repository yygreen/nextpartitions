## The right material for Madison's climate

Madison gets about 37 inches of rain a year, on roughly 121 days, and relative humidity averages 83% in the morning and 60% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Madison restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Wisconsin's building rules set the sizes. In Madison, the city's Building Inspection Division reviews commercial building plans of every size in place of the state.
- **Fire safety:** Wisconsin's building rules count toilet partitions as interior finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene finishes to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Wisconsin's building rules set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. Madison adopts them as the state writes them and adds no count of its own for offices, restaurants or schools. The table below shows what that means.
- **Wisconsin extras:** Wisconsin's rules ask for more than the federal ones in three places. The front and one side partition of the accessible stall must clear the floor by 12 inches, not 9, unless the stall is built larger than the minimum. Accessible stalls need an 18-inch vertical grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal bars. A restaurant or store that needs six or more toilets must add a family or assisted-use toilet room.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. The number of toilets is still worked out half for men and half for women.
- **Permits:** the City of Madison's Building Inspection Division issues building permits. Madison's building rules require a permit to alter or improve any building. The division's director may allow minor improvements without one when they cost $500 or less and involve no structural change and no change of use. The rules list no exempt work and don't name toilet partitions. Ask Building Inspection (608-266-4551) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the City of Madison Building Inspection Division or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Madison restroom needs

Wisconsin counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional under Wisconsin's rules: where the men's room needs more than one toilet, urinals may replace up to half of them in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, each with a privacy screen. The table shows the men's side as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. A restaurant that needs six or more toilets in total, as at 400 people, must also have a family or assisted-use toilet room, which counts toward the total. Wisconsin's rules don't say how an odd number splits between stalls and urinals, so confirm that with Madison's building plan reviewers (608-266-4551). At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20, and Wisconsin may accept the real number of occupants instead where the owner can justify it.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Madison

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Madison area earn a median of $30.52 an hour, about 5% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Wisconsin repealed its own wage law for public jobs in 2017, so set wage rates apply only on federally funded work. For a carpenter on building work in Dane County that rate is $42.45 an hour plus $28.78 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 5.5% in Madison: 5% state and 0.5% Dane County. Madison has no city sales tax.
- **Tax on installation:** in Wisconsin, a contractor's charge to install partitions fixed to the building is not taxed, for labor or materials. A contractor who supplies the partitions pays the tax when buying them and doesn't add sales tax to your bill.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Madison are taxable, and so is the seller's delivery charge. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state. Schools and government bodies can buy tax free with an exemption certificate.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Wisconsin Department of Revenue.
