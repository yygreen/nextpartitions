## The right material for San Antonio's climate

San Antonio gets about 32 inches of rain a year, on roughly 81 days, and relative humidity averages 83% in the morning and 55% in the afternoon. In most San Antonio restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a San Antonio restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Texas's own access rules set the sizes. On projects of $50,000 or more, the state reviews the plans; smaller projects must still follow the rules.
- **Fire safety:** San Antonio's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** San Antonio sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Texas and federal stall rules match:** Texas's own access rules for stalls, doors and grab bars read the same as the federal ones, so a stall that meets one meets the other.
- **Baby changing stations and restroom signs:** San Antonio requires a baby changing station in public restrooms of restaurants, stores, event venues, hospitals and city buildings when a restroom is built, enlarged by 50 square feet or more or given more fixtures. Public places must also post a domestic violence resource sign in every restroom.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. In a private building, San Antonio allows a shared all-gender room where every toilet and urinal is enclosed by walls and a door. The city's rules say walls, not partitions, so ask San Antonio Development Services what it accepts before you plan one. Since December 2025, Texas law requires shared restrooms in city, county, school district and public college buildings to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** San Antonio requires a permit to alter or repair a building unless the work is exempt. Its exempt list covers "similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high, without naming toilet partitions, so ask San Antonio Development Services whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with San Antonio Development Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a San Antonio restroom needs

San Antonio counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional. They may stand in for up to half of the men's toilets in an office, and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. An office that holds 25 people or fewer, a store that holds 100 or fewer, or any other space that holds 15 or fewer, staff and customers together, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or San Antonio Development Services sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in San Antonio

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the San Antonio–New Braunfels area earn a median of $23.51 an hour, about 19% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on city, county and school district projects, Texas law requires at least the locally set wage. The federal rate for a carpenter on building work in Bexar County is $16.86 an hour plus $4.17 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 8.25% in San Antonio: 6.25% state, 1.125% city, 0.5% transit authority and 0.375% advanced transportation district.
- **Tax on installation:** in Texas, labor to remodel an existing commercial building is taxed, even when it's billed separately. Labor on new construction is not.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to San Antonio are taxable, freight included. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Texas Comptroller.
