## The right material for Anchorage's climate

Anchorage gets about 16 inches of rain a year, on roughly 116 days, and relative humidity averages 74% in the morning and 64% in the afternoon. The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules an Anchorage restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Anchorage's building rules set the sizes.
- **Fire safety:** Anchorage's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) used as an interior finish to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Anchorage sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Anchorage extras:** accessible stalls here need a second, upright grab bar on the side wall, and a wheelchair stall needs more toe room under its partitions (12 inches high, against 9 under the federal rules). In a large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, not just one. Restaurants, venues and stores that need six or more toilets also need a family restroom.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** Anchorage's building rules require a one-person restroom to be marked as open to everyone, whatever their sex. They allow a shared all-gender room in place of separate men's and women's rooms if every toilet has its own stall with a door and every urinal is screened. A public restroom needs a sign saying whether it is for men, for women or for everyone. The Anchorage Equal Rights Commission enforces the city's law against discrimination by sex or gender identity in businesses and other places open to the public.
- **Permits:** Anchorage requires a permit to alter or repair a building and lists the work that needs none. The list covers painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, countertops and similar finish work, and movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches that aren't fixed in place. It doesn't name toilet partitions fixed to the floor or wall, so ask the Development Services permit counter (907-343-8211) whether your replacement needs one. In the parts of the municipality outside the Anchorage Building Safety Service Area, the city's code makes applying for a building permit optional. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Municipality of Anchorage Development Services Department or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls an Anchorage restroom needs

Anchorage's building rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall, and every urinal needs a screen on each side. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to two thirds of the toilets, so the table shows the men's side as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are Anchorage's minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. A men's room that needs only one toilet can't swap it for a urinal. An office designed for 25 people or fewer, a store for 100 or fewer, or any space for 15 or fewer, may have one shared restroom instead of separate men's and women's rooms. A shared all-gender room is counted on everyone in the building instead of half. A restaurant's seasonal outdoor seating counts toward the number of people. Anchorage counts toilets by its own building rules, so these numbers differ from the ones Alaska's plumbing rules give for the rest of the state.

To find your building's capacity, Anchorage's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

- **Public jobs:** on state, city, borough and school district construction contracts over $25,000, Alaska law requires at least the wage rates the state labor department publishes for the region. For a journeyman carpenter in Anchorage that is $52.54 an hour plus $28.21 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** none in Anchorage. Alaska has no state sales tax, and Anchorage has no general sales tax: its retail sales taxes apply only to alcoholic beverages and marijuana.
