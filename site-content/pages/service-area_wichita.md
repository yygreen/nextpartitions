## The right material for Wichita's climate

Wichita gets about 34 inches of rain a year, on roughly 86 days, and relative humidity averages 80% in the morning and 55% in the afternoon. In most Wichita restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Wichita restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes, and Kansas law makes the federal rules state law without adding its own. Kansas has no state review of plans for access in private buildings. In Wichita the Metropolitan Area Building and Construction Department (MABCD), the building department the city shares with Sedgwick County, checks it.
- **Fire safety:** Wichita's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) used as an interior finish to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Wichita sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building, and lets urinals take the place of up to half of the men's toilets. The table below shows what that means.
- **Access rules in Wichita:** Wichita's building rules say buildings must be designed to the current federal access guidelines. Inside the city limits, Wichita also applies them to private clubs and religious buildings, which the federal rules leave out.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** under Wichita's building rules a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone, and a shared all-gender room may take the place of separate men's and women's rooms where the toilets and urinals have the privacy the plumbing rules call for. Every required public restroom needs a sign near its entrance saying whether it is for men, for women or for everyone. In buildings owned or leased by a government body, a Kansas law in force since February 2026 requires every restroom built for more than one person to be designated for one sex. One-person and family restrooms are still allowed there.
- **Permits:** Wichita requires a permit to alter a building unless the work is on its exempt list. The list covers painting, tiling, carpeting, cabinets and "similar finish work", and partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high that are not fixed and can be moved, but it doesn't name toilet partitions. Ask MABCD at 316-660-1840 whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Metropolitan Area Building and Construction Department (MABCD) or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Wichita restroom needs

Kansas has no statewide building rules, so the rules Wichita shares with Sedgwick County decide. They count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: each urinal may take the place of one men's toilet, up to half of them in any kind of building, and each urinal needs a screen. The table shows men's toilets as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. A building or tenant space where 25 or fewer people are employed doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. Neither does a restaurant or bar with 25 seats or fewer and an assembly area of 735 square feet or less. Confirm the count with MABCD.

To find your building's capacity, Wichita's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or MABCD sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Wichita

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Wichita area earn a median of $24.49 an hour, about 16% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Kansas has no state wage law for public construction, and state law bars cities and counties from setting wage rates for construction contractors, so set wage rates apply only on federally funded jobs. For a carpenter on building work in Sedgwick County the federal rate is $17.62 an hour plus $1.93 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 7.5% in Wichita: 6.5% state and 1% Sedgwick County. Wichita has no city sales tax. Some addresses, several of them downtown, sit in special districts that add 1% to 2%.
- **Tax on installation:** in Kansas, labor to install partitions in an existing commercial building is taxed, even when it's billed separately. Labor on the original construction of a building is not. A contractor who supplies and installs partitions pays the tax when buying them and charges tax on the labor part of the job.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Wichita are taxable at the rate for the delivery address. Delivery charges listed separately on the invoice are not taxed. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Kansas Department of Revenue.
