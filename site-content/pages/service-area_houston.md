## The right material for Houston's climate

Houston gets about 52 inches of rain a year, on roughly 103 days, and the air averages 89% relative humidity at 6 a.m. and 59% at noon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry, conditioned rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Houston restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Texas's own access rules set the sizes. On projects of $50,000 or more, the state reviews the plans; smaller projects must still follow the rules.
- **Fire safety:** Houston's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Houston sets the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Texas and federal stall rules match:** Texas's own access rules for stalls, doors and grab bars read the same as the federal ones, so a stall that meets one meets the other.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. In a private building, a shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. Since December 2025, Texas law requires shared restrooms in city, county, school district and public college buildings to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** Houston requires a permit for remodels and alterations unless the work is exempt. Its exempt list covers "similar finish work" without naming toilet partitions, so ask the Houston Permitting Center whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Houston Permitting Center or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Houston restroom needs

Houston counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. One Houston rule is unusual: where the men's room needs more than one toilet, half of them must be urinals, so you buy fewer men's stalls and add urinal screens.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/1+1 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/~3 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
400: 5/~5 | 3/~3 | 4/2+2
:::

Where the men's side needs 3 or 5, the city doesn't say how "half" rounds, so confirm the split of stalls and urinals with Houston plan review. These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or the Houston Permitting Center sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Houston

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Houston area earn a median of $23.88 an hour, about 18% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on city, county and school district projects, Texas law requires at least the locally set wage. For a carpenter on building work in Harris County that is $33.27 an hour plus $9.73 in benefits, and the City of Houston uses the same rate.
- **Sales tax:** 8.25% in Houston: 6.25% state, 1% city and 1% transit authority.
- **Tax on installation:** in Texas, labor to remodel an existing commercial building is taxed, even when it's billed separately. Labor on new construction is not.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Houston are taxable, freight included. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Texas Comptroller.
