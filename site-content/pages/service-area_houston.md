## The right material for Houston's climate

Houston gets about 52 inches of rain a year, on roughly 103 days, and the air averages 89% relative humidity at 6 a.m. and 59% at noon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry, conditioned rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## Delivery to Houston job sites

Toilet partitions for Houston ship by freight carrier, crated, palletized and tracked. Delivery is curbside, with a liftgate on request if the site has no loading dock. Tell us in your quote request:

- **Where the crates wait.** Store panels flat, not leaned against a wall. Solid plastic may need about 24 hours at room temperature before it's installed, so check the manufacturer's instructions.

## Codes that apply in Houston

- **Accessibility:** the federal 2010 ADA Standards and the 2012 Texas Accessibility Standards (TAS). Projects with a total estimated cost of $50,000 or more are registered with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) and submitted for review; smaller projects must still comply with TAS.
- **Building code:** the 2021 International Building Code with Houston Amendments, in force since January 1, 2024. If you choose HDPE, the building code requires it to pass the NFPA 286 fire test, so ask for the fire-rated version.
- **Plumbing code:** the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code with Houston Amendments. It sets how many toilets and urinals a building needs, and so how many stalls.
- **Permits:** Houston requires a permit for alterations and remodels unless the work is exempt, and its exempt list covers "similar finish work" without naming toilet partitions. Ask the Houston Permitting Center whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Last checked November 23, 2026. Codes change, so confirm your project with the Houston Permitting Center, TDLR, or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).
