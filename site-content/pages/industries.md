# Bathroom Partitions by Industry

The right bathroom partitions depend on who uses the restroom, how hard they use it and how it gets cleaned. A school restroom, a pool locker room and an office restroom call for different materials and different mounting styles. This page matches each type of facility to a material and a mounting style, then sends you to the industry page with the detail.

## Schools

School restrooms take kicks, graffiti, hanging on doors and daily disinfecting. Solid plastic and phenolic are the usual choices. Elementary restrooms often use lower doors and panels than secondary schools; confirm the heights available for your material and manufacturer. If an accessible compartment is designed for children ages 3 to 12, it has its own dimensions and 12 inches of toe clearance (2010 ADA Standards 604.9, 604.8.1.4). [Schools page](/industries/schools).

## Gyms and fitness facilities

Locker rooms and shower areas stay wet. Solid plastic has no core to swell, which is why it suits them. Powder coated steel and plastic laminate are for dry rooms. Stainless steel resists corrosion but is not immune: chloride-laden air in indoor pool halls can stain or crack it, so use it away from the pool and wash it regularly. [Gyms page](/industries/gyms).

## Offices

Office restrooms see steady but gentle use, so finish and privacy matter more than extreme durability. Laminate offers the widest range of patterns. Powder coated steel is the usual lower-cost metal choice, and phenolic and stainless give a slimmer, more premium look. Standard doors and panels are 55 or 58 inches high; privacy versions go to 72 inches. Complete kits ship with hardware, and your contractor or a qualified installer puts them in. [Offices page](/industries/offices).

## Restaurants

Guests judge the kitchen by the restroom, so the finish should match the dining room. It also has to survive grease film, mop water and nightly cleaning. Most restaurant restrooms are small, and overhead braced mounting works with any ceiling type. Choose phenolic or solid plastic if the floor is hosed down. [Restaurants page](/industries/restaurants).

## Community centers

Community centers, libraries, churches and municipal buildings mix toddlers and seniors with a tight budget. Powder coated steel suits dry, lower-abuse rooms and HDPE suits heavily used ones. Older restrooms often need only replacement panels, doors or hardware; see [replacement parts](/hardware/parts). [Community centers page](/industries/community-centers).

## Public restrooms

Parks, transit stations, stadiums and municipal buildings see heavy traffic, daily washdowns and vandalism. Phenolic and stainless steel are the premium choices, with HDPE as the value option. Overhead braced is the standard; floor to ceiling anchors every pilaster at both ends. Raised panels and a smaller floor gap limit reach-under, but the accessible stall still needs its toe clearance. [Public restrooms page](/industries/public-restrooms).

## Rules that apply in every facility

![Plan of a row of toilet compartments: accessible, ambulatory and two standard](/images/diagrams/sheet-plan.svg)

1. **Accessible compartment.** At least 60 inches wide (60-1/2 inches drawn here) with the toilet beside the dividing panel, and at least 56 inches deep with a wall-hung toilet or 59 inches with a floor-mounted one (604.8.1.1). The self-closing door needs a 32-inch minimum clear opening and cannot swing into the minimum compartment area (604.8.1.2, 404.2.3), so a minimum-size compartment usually has an outswing door.
2. **Ambulatory compartment.** 35 to 37 inches wide (36-1/2 inches drawn here) and at least 60 inches deep, with grab bars on both sides and an outswing door (604.8.2). It is required once a restroom has six or more compartments, or six or more toilets and urinals combined (213.3.1).
3. **Standard compartments.** About 36 inches wide, drawn here with inswing doors.
4. **Grab bars** on the side and rear walls of the accessible compartment (604.5).

## FAQ

### What is the best bathroom partition material for a school?
Solid plastic (HDPE) and phenolic are the usual choices because they resist kicks, moisture and graffiti. If you choose HDPE, ask for the fire-rated version and confirm the NFPA 286 requirement with your building official.

### Which partitions suit a gym or pool locker room?
Solid plastic, because it has no core to swell. Powder coated steel and plastic laminate belong in dry rooms. Stainless can be affected by chloride-laden air in indoor pool halls.

### Does an accessible stall need an outswing door?
Not by rule. The door must be self-closing, give a 32-inch minimum clear opening and not swing into the minimum compartment area (604.8.1.2, 404.2.3). In a minimum-size compartment that usually means an outswing door.

### When is an ambulatory stall required?
When a restroom has six or more compartments, or six or more toilets and urinals combined (213.3.1).

### Do you install the partitions?
No. We supply partitions. Follow the manufacturer's instructions and approved drawings, and use a qualified installer.
