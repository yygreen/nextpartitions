## The right material for Miami's climate

Miami gets about 67 inches of rain a year, on roughly 135 days, and the air averages 82% relative humidity at 7 a.m. and 61% at 1 p.m. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, hotels, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (beach and park facilities, marinas, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## Delivery to Miami job sites

Toilet partitions for Miami ship by freight carrier, crated, palletized and tracked. Delivery is curbside, with a liftgate on request if the site has no loading dock. Tell us in your quote request:

- **The delivery address,** and whether it's a job site, a warehouse or an occupied building.
- **Dock or liftgate.** A building without a dock needs a liftgate truck.
- **Who receives it.** Crates are heavy: a published 3-stall powder coated steel kit weighs about 300 lb.
- **Where the crates wait.** Store panels flat, not leaned against a wall. Solid plastic may need about 24 hours at room temperature before it's installed, so check the manufacturer's instructions.

Freight is quoted with the partitions, so the number you get covers delivery to your Miami address.

## Codes that apply in Miami

- **Accessibility:** the federal 2010 ADA Standards and the Florida Building Code, Accessibility.
- **Building code:** the Florida Building Code, Building, 8th Edition (2023). The 9th Edition (2026) replaces it on December 31, 2026, for the Building, Plumbing and Accessibility volumes alike. If you choose HDPE, ask for the version that passes the NFPA 286 fire test, which the International Building Code requires and the Florida code is based on.
- **Plumbing code:** the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition (2023) until then. It sets how many toilets and urinals a building needs, and so how many stalls.
- **Permits:** Miami's list of work that needs no permit covers only partitions "not fixed to the structure" and no taller than 5 feet 9 inches. Toilet partitions are anchored, so ask the City of Miami Building Department before a replacement. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Last checked November 23, 2026. Codes change, so confirm your project with the City of Miami Building Department or your architect.
