## The right material for Tulsa's climate

Tulsa gets about 41 inches of rain a year, on roughly 91 days, and relative humidity averages 81% in the morning and 56% in the afternoon. In most Tulsa restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Tulsa restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes, and Tulsa's building rules add a national accessibility standard on top. The city checks access with the building permit.
- **Fire safety:** Oklahoma's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Tulsa's building rules set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The city's own amendments don't change the counts. The table below shows what that means.
- **New rules expected on January 1, 2027:** Tulsa's ordinances still adopt the 2018 editions of the national model codes, with city amendments. Oklahoma made the 2024 editions the statewide minimum on September 14, 2026. The city says its update to the 2024 editions is in progress, with a pending adoption date of January 1, 2027, and that plans submitted after it adopts them must meet the new codes. Ask the Permit Center which edition applies to your project.
- **Beyond the federal rules:** under the 2018 rules in Tulsa's ordinances, the side wall next to the toilet needs an 18 inch vertical grab bar as well as the horizontal one, which the federal rules don't ask for. Oklahoma's 2024 rules add two more things. The front partition and one side partition of the wheelchair stall need 12 inches of clear space above the floor, where the federal rule asks for 9. A new building's restroom needs a 67 inch turning circle, where the federal rule asks for 60.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked for use by either sex. Under the 2018 rules in Tulsa's ordinances, a building needs separate men's and women's rooms unless it holds 15 people or fewer (25 for an office, 100 for a store). By city policy, an office, factory, warehouse or storage building that holds 49 people or fewer may have one restroom open to everyone instead, if the owner asks, the code official approves and the owner files a notice on the property record. Oklahoma's 2024 rules allow a shared all-gender room if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and every urinal has privacy partitions. Tulsa's 2018 rules don't provide for one, so ask Development Services before planning it. Oklahoma law requires shared restrooms in public schools, from pre-kindergarten to 12th grade, and in state correctional facilities to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** the City of Tulsa's Development Services department issues building permits. The city's list of work that needs no permit includes painting, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, counter tops and similar finish work, and movable fixtures, counters and partitions that are not fixed in place and stand no taller than 5 feet 9 inches. The list doesn't name toilet partitions. The city says a permit is typically needed to build, remodel, move or repair walls, floors, ceilings, windows or doors. Ask the Permit Center (918-596-9456) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the City of Tulsa Development Services Permit Center or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Tulsa restroom needs

Tulsa's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a screen on each side.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. The counts are the same under the 2018 rules in Tulsa's ordinances and the 2024 rules Oklahoma adopted in September 2026. An office with 25 people or fewer, a store with 100 or fewer, or any other space with 15 or fewer doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

Tulsa's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Tulsa

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Tulsa area earn a median of $22.51 an hour, about 23% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Oklahoma has no state wage law for public jobs, so a set wage applies only on federally funded projects, under federal law. For a carpenter on building work in Tulsa County that rate is $14.96 an hour plus $1.55 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 8.517% in Tulsa: 4.5% state, 3.65% city and 0.367% Tulsa County.
- **Tax on installation:** in Oklahoma, an installation charge is not taxed when it is shown separately from the partitions on the bill. A single price for partitions and installation is taxed in full. A contractor who supplies and installs partitions as part of the building pays the tax when buying them.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Tulsa are taxable at the rate for the delivery address. Freight is not taxed when it is listed separately on the invoice. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Oklahoma Tax Commission.
