## The right material for Kansas City's climate

Kansas City gets about 39 inches of rain a year, on roughly 104 days, and relative humidity averages 81% in the morning and 60% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Kansas City restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes, and the city's building code adds a national accessibility standard on top. Missouri has no state review of plans for access: the city checks it with the building permit.
- **Fire safety:** Kansas City's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Kansas City sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Missouri extras:** the national standard that Missouri cities apply through their building codes asks for an 18 inch vertical grab bar on the side wall next to the toilet, which the federal rules do not. On buildings paid for with state, city, county or school district money, Missouri law requires the latest edition of that standard and the wheelchair symbol at the main entrance.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** Missouri has no state law on single-user or all-gender restrooms, and none on who may use which restroom. Under Kansas City's building rules a one-person or family restroom is marked for use by either sex. Above the small-building limits the rules call for separate men's and women's rooms, and they have no provision for a shared all-gender room with stalls. A family restroom, which larger stores and gathering places must have, needs an adult-size changing table that adjusts in height.
- **Permits:** Kansas City's list of work that needs no building permit includes movable cases, counters and partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high, wall or floor mounted cabinets, and painting and similar finish work. It doesn't name toilet partitions. Ask the City Planning and Development Department's Permits Division at (816) 513-1500 whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Kansas City's City Planning and Development Department or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Kansas City restroom needs

Kansas City counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall, and each urinal needs a screen. Urinals may take the place of some of the men's toilets. The model rules cap that at half in an office and two thirds in a restaurant or school, but the cap sits in a plumbing code Kansas City hasn't adopted, so confirm the share with the City Planning and Development Department.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. Separate men's and women's rooms aren't required in a space for 15 people or fewer, in an office for 25 or fewer or in a store for 100 or fewer. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, Kansas City's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or the City Planning and Development Department sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Kansas City

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Kansas City area earn a median of $29.72 an hour, about 2% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on state, city, county and school district projects with a total cost above $75,000, Missouri law requires at least the wage the state sets each year for each trade in each county. For a carpenter on building work in Jackson County that is $68.84 an hour, benefits included.
- **Sales tax:** 8.975% at most Kansas City addresses in Jackson County: 4.225% state, 1.5% county and zoo district and 3.25% city. Addresses inside the Main Street rail district pay 9.975%, and some shopping districts add their own tax. Partitions shipped in from a seller outside Missouri fall under the use tax instead, 7.475% at those addresses. The parts of the city in Clay, Platte and Cass counties have their own rates.
- **Tax on installation:** in Missouri, installation labor is not taxed. A contractor who supplies and installs partitions pays the tax when buying them and does not add tax to the contract.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Kansas City are taxable. Usual delivery charges listed separately on the invoice are not taxed. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Missouri Department of Revenue.
