# Bathroom Partition Guides

These bathroom partition guides help facilities managers, contractors and architects plan a commercial restroom before they ask for a quote. Start with the sizes, check the accessibility rules, then pick a material and a mounting style. Each guide links to the source it relies on, and your architect or local code official has the final word on your project.

## FAQ

### Where do I start when planning restroom partitions?
Measure the room first, using the [stall dimensions guide](/guides/bathroom-stall-dimensions). Then confirm which accessible stalls you need with the [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and choose a [material](/partitions) and a [mounting style](/mounting-styles).

### Do your guides replace an architect or code official?
No. The guides summarize published standards and manufacturer information. Codes and their interpretation vary by jurisdiction, so confirm your layout with your architect, engineer or local code official.

### Can I get a quote before my drawings are final?
Yes. Send the measurements you have, and photos of the walls and floor. Standard configurations are quoted within 2 business hours; custom projects within 24–48 hours.

### Corrections to the four existing guides (fix when they are expanded)

**/guides/ada-compliance**
1. Door swing is wrong. The table says doors must swing out unless the compartment is "at least 60" deep beyond the door swing", and the FAQ says "Yes, ADA stall doors must swing outward." 604.8.1.2 says only that the door "shall not swing into the minimum required compartment area" (60" wide by 56" deep with a wall-hung toilet, 59" with a floor-mounted one). An in-swinging door is allowed in a compartment made larger than the minimum. There is no "60 inches beyond the door swing" rule.
2. Missing door rules from 604.8.1.2: the door must be self-closing; door pulls are required on both sides near the latch; the door opening must be within 4" of the side wall or partition farthest from the toilet (front-partition door) or within 4" of the front partition (side-wall door); 42" clearance is required at a latch-side approach.
3. Missing toe clearance (604.8.1.4): the front partition and at least one side partition need 9" minimum toe clearance above the floor, 6" deep, unless the compartment is over 62" deep (wall-hung toilet) or 65" deep (floor-mounted), or over 66" wide. This decides how low the partitions in the accessible stall can go, so it matters for partitions most of all.
4. The ambulatory compartment (35–37" wide by 60" deep, per 604.8.2.1) is listed without saying when it is required. 213.3.1 requires one, in addition to the wheelchair-accessible compartment, where there are six or more toilet compartments, or where urinals and toilets together total six or more fixtures.
5. Ambulatory toilet centerline is 17–19" from the side wall or partition (604.2), not the 16–18" listed for the wheelchair-accessible stall. Ambulatory compartments need side-wall grab bars on both sides (604.8.2.3).
6. Children's compartments are missing: 60" wide by 59" deep for both wall-hung and floor-mounted toilets (604.8.1.1), with 12" toe clearance (604.8.1.4).
7. Door hardware: "That rules out the standard slide bolt" overstates 309.4, which requires one-hand operation without tight grasping, pinching or twisting of the wrist, at 5 lb maximum force. Slide latches made to meet this are sold (Bradley's PH400 spec describes its indicator slide latch as requiring "no twisting motion"). Also add the hardware height, 34–48" above the floor (404.2.7).
8. "Every commercial restroom in the US must include at least one ADA-compliant stall": 213.3.1 applies where toilet compartments are provided in toilet rooms required to be accessible. Reword to "Where a restroom has toilet compartments, at least one must be wheelchair accessible (213.3.1)". The rest of the grab bar, seat height and 32" clear opening figures check out (604.4, 604.5, 609, 404.2.3).

**/guides/bathroom-stall-dimensions**
1. "Confirm the door swing direction (outswing required for ADA)": not an ADA rule; see ADA item 1.
2. "Privacy panel option 72–84" with 4–6" floor gap" doesn't match the manufacturers checked: Bobrick's maximum height is 71¾" at 4½" floor clearance, with extended 73–96" at 1" on some lines; ASI's are 64" or 72" at 6" or 9"; Bradley's are 72" at 6", or 69" at 9" for ADA stalls. In the wheelchair-accessible stall, panels mounted below 9" need a larger compartment (604.8.1.4 toe clearance).
3. Standard depth "60"" is the plumbing code minimum for a floor-mounted toilet (2018 IPC 405.3.1: 30" wide by 60" deep; 56" for wall-hung), not an industry standard. Manufacturer examples run deeper (Bradley's PH400 example is 61-1/4" to the face).
4. "Narrow / juvenile 30"" is the IPC minimum compartment width for any toilet, not a juvenile size. Children's accessible compartments follow 604.9.
5. The 24" and 22" door widths could not be confirmed in a manufacturer source; check before keeping them. The 58" panel, 12" floor clearance and 82" pilaster height are confirmed (Bobrick, ASI, Bradley).

**/guides/standard-sizes**
1. Ambulatory stall: "Larger facilities (six or more stalls) typically also include an ambulatory stall" is wrong. 213.3.1 makes it required, not typical, and the trigger is six or more compartments, or six or more urinals and toilets combined. The width range is 35–37" (604.8.2.1). The "outswing door" is not stated in the rule; the door just can't swing into the 60"-deep minimum area (604.8.2.2).
2. ADA stall: "outswing or sliding door" (see ADA item 1). "56–59" deep depending on toilet type" leaves out children's compartments (59" for both).
3. Juvenile 30" stalls as "the default in K-5 facilities": no source found; 30" is the IPC minimum width for all compartments. Cut or source it.

**/guides/restroom-layout**
1. Urinal centerline to side wall or partition "13–15"" is below the 15" minimum in 2018 IPC 405.3.1.
2. Urinal to urinal "24–30"" is below the IPC's 30" center-to-center minimum where partitions don't separate the fixtures.
3. Toilet centerline "16–18" (15" minimum, 18" preferred)" mixes two rules: 15" is the IPC minimum for any toilet; 16–18" is the ADA range for the wheelchair-accessible toilet (604.2) and 17–19" for the ambulatory one. "18" preferred" has no source.
4. The "30" minimum" toilet-to-next-fixture spacing applies where partitions don't separate the fixtures (IPC 405.3.1). Add the IPC's 21" minimum clear space in front of each fixture.
5. Lavatory clear floor space 30" by 48" for a forward approach is right (305.3, 606.2). It applies to the accessible lavatory and also needs knee and toe clearance (306).
6. Turning space (60" circle or T-shape) is right (304.3).
7. The "Alcove" definition ("stalls open into a recessed area with the door swing protected") could not be confirmed against a manufacturer planning guide. Bobrick uses "alcove" for a large accessible compartment (5' deep by 8' long with an internal 60" turning space). Confirm before expanding.
