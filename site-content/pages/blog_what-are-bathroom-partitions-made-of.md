# What Are Bathroom Partitions Made Of?

Commercial bathroom partitions are made from five main materials: powder coated steel, stainless steel, plastic laminate, phenolic and solid plastic (HDPE). The bathroom partition materials differ less in looks than in what's inside the panel: a honeycomb core, particleboard, layers of resin-soaked paper, or one solid sheet of plastic. That core decides how a stall handles water, abuse and cleaning, so it's the first thing to understand when someone asks what bathroom stalls are made of.

This guide explains how each material is built, using the manufacturers' own descriptions, and where each one fits.

## The short answer

| Material | How the panel is built | What the manufacturers say it's suited to |
|---|---|---|
| [Powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) | Galvannealed steel faces over a honeycomb core, powder coated | Economy and general use (ASI, Bobrick) |
| [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) | Type 304 stainless faces over a honeycomb core | Corrosion resistance, cleanliness, design appeal (ASI, Bobrick) |
| [Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) | High-pressure laminate bonded to a resin-impregnated particleboard core | Wide color choice; the core can swell with too much moisture (Bobrick) |
| [Phenolic](/partitions/phenolic) | Layers of resin-impregnated kraft paper fused under heat and pressure | Wet and high-traffic areas (ASI, Bobrick, Bradley) |
| [Solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) | One homogeneous sheet of high-density polyethylene | Moisture, mildew and graffiti resistance (ASI, Scranton) |

Bobrick sorts these into four construction methods, which is a useful way to think about them: honeycomb (painted metal and stainless steel), bonded (high-pressure laminate), layered (compact laminate, which is phenolic) and homogeneous (HDPE, and Bobrick's own SCRC material).

## Powder coated steel

A powder coated steel partition is a sandwich. ASI describes its powder coated partitions as galvannealed steel with a honeycomb core "for solid construction and sound attenuation", finished with a thermoset hybrid powder coating that resists wear, fading and scuffing. You'll also see these called painted metal or baked enamel partitions.

In Bobrick's comparison, painted metal's strengths are availability, low cost, the option to repaint, and a Class A interior finish rating. Its weaknesses are that it can rust, dent and scratch, and that graffiti is harder to deal with. That makes powder coated steel a common pick for offices, retail and other dry restrooms where budget matters more than abuse resistance.

See colors and specs on the [powder coated steel partitions](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) page.

## Stainless steel

Stainless steel partitions use the same honeycomb construction with a stainless skin. ASI builds its stainless partitions from type 304 stainless steel, and says a textured finish adds resistance to vandalism. Bradley offers satin brushed and a textured pattern on its stainless line.

Bobrick lists corrosion resistance, cleanliness, design appeal and a Class A rating as stainless steel's strengths; it can still dent and scratch, and it's the most expensive material in Bobrick's comparison. Stainless shows up in airports, hospitals and upscale offices, where the restroom is part of the building's finish.

See finishes on the [stainless steel partitions](/partitions/stainless-steel) page.

## Plastic laminate

Plastic laminate partitions bond a decorative high-pressure laminate (HPL) to a wood-based core. Bobrick specifies a 45 lb density, resin-impregnated particleboard core, and its HPL doors, stiles and panels are 1" thick. ASI's version adds Moisture Guard edge banding that fuses with the core.

Laminate's appeal is choice: Bobrick lists a wide color selection plus scratch, dent and graffiti resistance at a low cost. The trade-off is water. Bobrick notes that the core "may swell when exposed to excessive moisture" and that deep scratches can expose the dark kraft paper beneath the surface. Keep plastic laminate in dry, climate-controlled restrooms, not showers or pool areas.

See patterns on the [plastic laminate partitions](/partitions/plastic-laminate) page.

## Phenolic (compact laminate)

Phenolic partitions have no separate core. They're made by stacking layers of kraft paper soaked in phenolic resin and fusing them under high heat and pressure into one dense sheet. Scranton Products describes black core phenolic as a "resin-impregnated kraft paper core"; Bobrick calls the same family of material compact grade laminate (CGL), with melamine faces, colored face sheets and a black phenolic-resin core.

Two versions are common:

- **Black core phenolic:** decorative faces over a black core, so the edges are black. ASI says its black core phenolic "holds up even under severe moisture conditions from showers, pools, and saunas", and rates it Class B as standard.
- **Color-through phenolic:** the color runs through the core to match the surface. ASI rates its Color-Thru phenolic Class A as standard.

Phenolic panels are thinner than laminate or HDPE panels. Bobrick and Bradley both make their phenolic panels 1/2" thick, with 3/4" doors and stiles (pilasters). Bobrick's materials guide lists water resistance, hardness and resistance to dents, scratches and graffiti as strengths, with one caveat: on black core material, a deep scratch can expose the black core.

See colors on the [phenolic partitions](/partitions/phenolic) page.

## Solid plastic (HDPE)

Solid plastic partitions are made from high-density polyethylene (HDPE), one homogeneous sheet with no core, no skin and no edge band. Scranton Products makes its Hiny Hiders partitions from 1-inch solid HDPE with the color running all the way through. ASI says its solid plastic partitions are "highly resistant to common cleaning agents as well as to moisture, mildew, and graffiti" and "will not easily dent, rust, or delaminate".

Because the color runs through, scratches and gouges can be sanded out, which Bobrick lists as a strength along with water resistance and high recycled content. Bobrick also lists the downsides: HDPE has the softest surface of the common materials and scratches easily, graffiti made with xylene-based markers can "ghost" into the plastic, and the color range is more limited.

**Check the fire rating.** HDPE burns differently from the other materials, so the model codes treat it differently. Bobrick's code summary explains that the IBC, the International Fire Code, NFPA 101 and NFPA 1 regulate toilet partitions as interior finish, and require HDPE and polypropylene partitions to pass the NFPA 286 room-corner test. Other materials can be tested to ASTM E84 instead. In Bobrick's tests, untreated HDPE was unclassified under ASTM E84 because of its smoke development. Fire-rated HDPE is available: Scranton offers fire rating options on Hiny Hiders. If your building needs a rated partition, say so in your quote request, and ask for the test documentation.

See colors on the [solid plastic (HDPE) partitions](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) page.

## Other materials you'll see on spec sheets

- **SCRC (solid color reinforced composite):** a Bobrick material, sold as its SierraSeries. Bobrick describes it as "dyed wood fibrous material reinforced with polycarbonate and phenolic resins fused into a homogeneous piece", color-through, and rated Class B.
- **Compact grade laminate (CGL):** Bobrick's name for phenolic, used in its DuraLineSeries partitions and Evolve cubicles.
- **Cubicle systems:** European-style cubicles such as Bobrick's PRIVADA (HPL and wood veneer laminate), which Bobrick sells with built-in no-sightline privacy, are a separate category from traditional partitions.

## Fire ratings in plain terms

Most partition materials are rated with ASTM E84, which measures how fast flame spreads across the surface. Bobrick's materials guide summarizes the classes: Class A is a flame spread index of 25 or less, Class B is 26 to 75, and Class C is 76 to 200. The rating you need depends on the building's occupancy and on where the restroom sits, so confirm it with your architect or local code official. As noted above, HDPE and polypropylene partitions are tested to NFPA 286 instead.

## What comes with the panels

Whichever material you choose, a partition system is more than panels. Next Partitions quotes include panels, doors, pilasters, headrails, hinges, latches, keepers, strikes, brackets, pilaster shoes and mounting fasteners. Hardware is often a different material from the panels: ASI uses type 304 stainless steel for its leveling shoes and offers chrome plated Zamac slide latches.

How the partitions attach to the building is a separate choice from the material. ## How to choose

Start with the environment, then the budget:

- **Dry office or retail restroom, tight budget:** powder coated steel or plastic laminate.
- **Design-led restroom that has to match millwork:** plastic laminate, for the widest color and pattern choice.
- **Showers, pools, locker rooms, or restrooms that get hosed down:** phenolic or HDPE. Bobrick recommends its SCRC or solid phenolic for wet areas; ASI positions black core phenolic and HDPE for showers, pools and high humidity.
- **Graffiti and abuse:** phenolic and HDPE both have through-color or dense surfaces that resist marking; Bobrick's notes on xylene "ghosting" in HDPE are worth weighing in schools.
- **Premium, public-facing restroom:** stainless steel.
- **Fire-rated building:** confirm the rating first; with HDPE, specify a fire-rated grade tested to NFPA 286.

## FAQ

### What are bathroom stalls made of?
Commercial bathroom stalls are made of powder coated steel, stainless steel, plastic laminate, phenolic or solid plastic (HDPE). Steel partitions wrap a honeycomb core, plastic laminate is bonded to particleboard, phenolic is fused layers of resin-soaked kraft paper, and HDPE is one solid sheet of plastic.

### What is the best material for a wet restroom?
Phenolic or solid plastic (HDPE). Manufacturers position both for showers, pools and areas that are hosed down. Plastic laminate's particleboard core can swell with too much moisture, and painted steel can rust.

### Is phenolic the same as compact laminate?
Yes, in practice. Bobrick calls its phenolic material compact grade laminate (CGL): melamine faces and colored face sheets bonded to a black phenolic-resin core.

### Are HDPE partitions fire rated?
Not by default. The model codes require HDPE partitions to pass the NFPA 286 room-corner test, and untreated HDPE doesn't. Fire-rated grades are available, so tell us if your building needs one.

### How thick are bathroom stall partitions?
It depends on the material. Bobrick's plastic laminate doors, stiles and panels are 1" thick; Bobrick and Bradley phenolic panels are 1/2" with 3/4" doors and pilasters; Scranton's HDPE is 1".
