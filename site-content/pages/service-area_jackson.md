## The right material for Jackson's climate

Jackson gets about 57 inches of rain a year, on roughly 110 days, and relative humidity averages 90% in the morning and 57% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Jackson restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes everywhere. Mississippi has no separate modern state access code, and the model building rules Jackson has adopted bring their own access standard with them.
- **Fire safety:** the model building rules Jackson has adopted require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Jackson's building rules set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Mississippi extras:** the access standard in Jackson's building rules asks for one thing in a wheelchair stall that the federal standard doesn't: an upright grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal one. An older state law also sets stall sizes for buildings paid for with public money and has the State Board of Health review the plans. Ask the Building Permits Division which standard it enforces.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** under the model building rules Jackson has adopted, a one-person restroom is marked for use by either sex and counts toward the required toilets. Men and women need separate rooms, except in a building designed for 15 people or fewer, an office for 25 or fewer or a shop for 100 or fewer. That edition has no provision for a shared all-gender room with several stalls, so ask the Building Permits Division before planning one. Mississippi law requires public schools, community colleges and public universities to have restrooms designated for females and for males, or one-person rooms.
- **Permits:** Jackson requires a permit to construct, enlarge, repair, move or demolish a building. The model rules it has adopted exempt "painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, counter tops and similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high. They don't name toilet partitions, and Jackson adopted them with its own amendments. Ask the Building Permits Division whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Jackson's Building Permits Division or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Jackson restroom needs

Mississippi has no single statewide building code, so the model building rules Jackson has adopted decide. They count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and fractions round up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to two thirds of the toilets in a restaurant or school and up to half in an office, and each urinal needs a privacy screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

Jackson adopted these rules with its own amendments, so confirm the count with the Building Permits Division. These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the model rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or the Building Permits Division sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Jackson

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Jackson area earn a median of $23.72 an hour, about 19% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Mississippi has no state wage law for public jobs, so a set wage applies only on federally funded work. For a carpenter on building work in Hinds County the federal rate is $15.33 an hour plus $4.42 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 7% state. Jackson adds 1% on goods sold from a business located in the city, which makes 8% there. Goods delivered into Jackson by a seller outside the city don't carry the extra 1%. Mississippi has no county sales tax.
- **Tax on installation:** in Mississippi, installation labor sold together with the partitions is taxed. On a commercial construction or renovation contract above $10,000, the contractor instead pays a 3.5% contractor's tax on the whole contract price and buys the materials free of sales tax.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Jackson are taxable at the state rate of 7%, freight included. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Mississippi Department of Revenue.
