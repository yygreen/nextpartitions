# How to Install Bathroom Partitions

**This page is an overview, not installation instructions.** Follow the manufacturer's installation instructions for your product and the approved shop drawings, and use a qualified installer. Next Partitions sells partitions. We do not install them, and we are not responsible for installation. Details change by brand, series and material.

Knowing how to install bathroom partitions helps you plan the job, check the building and spot problems early. This guide covers what to check first, the general order of work for each mounting style, how the hardware goes on, and what to do when a door won't latch or a pilaster won't stand plumb. Every step here comes from manufacturer literature and specifications. Your product's own guide always wins. To find it, see [bathroom partition installation instructions](/guides/installation-instructions).

Not sure which brand you have? Start with [our brands](/brands).

## Before you start

Most installation problems begin before anyone picks up a drill. Check these four things first.

### Tools and parts

Read the manufacturer's guide all the way through and lay out every part before you begin. The guide for your series lists the tools and fasteners. Anchors and fasteners differ by floor type and wall type. Use the ones the manufacturer specifies for your series. Do not substitute.

### Wall blocking and backing

Panels and pilasters attach to the walls with brackets. The walls need backing behind those brackets. Bobrick notes that wall backing is required to secure the mounting brackets. Bradley's installation guide includes a warning to confirm wall and floor backing before mounting. Partitions are heavy. If a wall is hollow drywall with nothing behind it, the brackets have nowhere to hold.

### Slab and ceiling structure check

The floor and ceiling carry the load, and each mounting style asks for something different.

| Mounting style | Floor | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| [Overhead braced](/mounting-styles/overhead-braced) | Floor that accepts anchors; NY OGS spec calls for at least 1-3/4" into the structural floor | Nothing above the ceiling needed |
| [Floor mounted](/mounting-styles/floor-mounted) | Structural concrete; Bobrick anchors need 2" minimum into concrete at least 3" thick | Nothing needed |
| [Ceiling hung](/mounting-styles/ceiling-hung) | Floor stays clear | Structural steel above the finished ceiling, by others |
| [Floor to ceiling](/mounting-styles/floor-to-ceiling) | Concrete floor | Structural members at the ceiling; Bobrick does not furnish them |

![The four bathroom partition mounting styles side by side](/images/diagrams/mounting-styles-compare.svg)

1. **Overhead braced:** headrail across the pilaster tops.
2. **Floor mounted:** pilasters anchored in the floor only.
3. **Ceiling hung:** pilasters hang from steel above the ceiling, floor clear.
4. **Floor to ceiling:** pilasters anchored at floor and ceiling.

The support system is shown in orange. Have your contractor or structural engineer confirm the structure at each pilaster location before partitions ship.

### Layout from approved shop drawings

Set out the job from the approved shop drawings, not from memory or from the old stalls. Measure the room and compare it with the drawings before you start. If a wall or column is not where the drawing says, stop and resolve it with the architect. For new work, see the room measurements we ask for in [how to measure a restroom](/guides/restroom-layout).

![Plan of an empty restroom with lettered measurements](/images/diagrams/measure-new-restroom.svg)

1. **A:** room width along the stall wall.
2. **B:** room depth.
3. **C:** ceiling height.
4. **D:** entry door location and swing.
5. **E:** toilet centerlines from the side wall.
6. **F:** floor and ceiling construction.

## The general sequence for overhead-braced and floor-mounted

The order below is the typical one across manufacturers. Your guide may differ in detail.

![Overhead braced toilet partitions, front view](/images/diagrams/mounting-overhead-braced.svg)

1. **Headrail:** extruded anodized aluminum with an anti-grip profile, fixed to both walls with brackets.
2. **Pilaster shoe** (4" high, stainless steel) over the floor anchor.
3. **Pilaster.** Doors and panels are 58" high, 12" above the floor.

1. **Layout lines.** Mark the pilaster, panel and wall bracket positions on the floor and walls from the shop drawings. Bradley's ceiling-hung guide uses a level line 12" above the floor, which matches the 12" floor clearance in Hadrian's standard series.
2. **Pilaster anchors and leveling.** Each pilaster sits on a floor bracket or leveling device. Bobrick's plastic laminate line uses a 12-gauge plated steel bracket with a leveling bolt, a floor L-bracket and stainless steel floor screws. For floor-mounted stalls, Bobrick uses a steel leveling bar with threaded rods and expansion shields in the floor. Set each pilaster plumb and at the right height, then fit the stainless steel shoe over the hardware.
3. **Panel brackets.** Fasten the brackets to the walls and pilasters at the marked positions, into the backing.
4. **Panels.** Hang the panels between the pilasters and the walls on the brackets.
5. **Headrail (overhead braced only).** The headrail clamps over the pilaster tops and fastens to the walls at each end. The NY OGS master spec says to fasten it to each pilaster with at least two fasteners. Floor-mounted stalls have no headrail, which is why their anchors are deeper.
6. **Doors and hinge adjustment.** Hang the doors and adjust them. The OGS spec says to adjust doors so their tops are parallel with the overhead brace. See the hinge setting below.
7. **Latch and keeper alignment.** Fit the latch, then the keeper. See below.

## Ceiling hung

![Ceiling hung toilet partitions, front view](/images/diagrams/mounting-ceiling-hung.svg)

1. **Structural steel above the finished ceiling**, supplied by the building and engineered for the load.
2. **Hanger** from the steel down through the ceiling into each pilaster. Bobrick's pilasters are 83" for an 8' ceiling.
3. **The floor stays completely clear.**

Ceiling hung is the style where the building matters most. If you are asking how to install ceiling hung toilet partitions, the first answer is that the support steel must exist before the partitions arrive.

- **Structural steel above the ceiling, per manufacturer.** Bobrick's TB-32 bulletin shows I-beam, channel and angle supports. Bradley's ceiling-hung guide says a "C" channel is required and is not included. The architect or structural engineer designs the support, and others furnish it.
- **Load.** Bobrick says the ceiling structure must support at least 295 lb per plastic laminate partition and 275 lb per compact grade partition, and must be rigid enough to withstand door swing and impact.
- **Steel, not wood.** Bobrick does not recommend wood beams for ceiling-hung installation.
- **The finished ceiling carries nothing.** The NY OGS spec requires anchoring assemblies to connect to structure above the ceiling without transmitting load to the finished ceiling.
- **Hanger and leveling.** Bobrick's ceiling anchor assembly is a threaded rod, hex nuts, washers, a shoe retainer and an expansion anchor. The pilaster hangs from the rod and is leveled with the nuts. A 4" stainless steel shoe covers the hanger at the ceiling.
- **Ceiling height.** Bobrick's standard 83" pilaster fits an 8' ceiling and is made to length up to 10'.

The panel, door, hinge and latch steps then follow the same pattern as above.

## Floor to ceiling

Floor-to-ceiling pilasters are anchored at both ends. The NY OGS spec calls for anchoring assemblies with leveling adjustment nuts at the tops and bottoms of the pilasters, with a shoe at the floor and a sleeve at the ceiling. Bobrick makes its floor-to-ceiling stiles to length for ceilings up to 10'. Unlike ceiling hung, the OGS spec notes wood may provide adequate overhead support for floor-and-ceiling-anchored compartments. Consult the manufacturer. Doors and panels hang level and aligned with each other.

## Hardware

### Latch, keeper and bumper

![Bathroom stall latch on the door and keeper on the pilaster](/images/diagrams/latch-keeper.svg)

1. **Latch**, mounted on the door; the bolt slides into the keeper.
2. **Keeper (strike)**, mounted on the pilaster.
3. **Occupancy indicator** on an indicator latch.

For a slide latch install, ASI Global's guide aligns the keeper with the latch bar while the door is closed and the latch engaged, then drills the pilaster through the keeper's holes. Close the door and check the fit before drilling. Fit the bumper so it strikes the wall before any other part of the door (ASI). Latches and strikes are not interchangeable across systems. Scranton states its latches and strikes cannot be mixed.

### Hinge setting

![Gravity (cam), wrap-around and continuous bathroom stall hinges](/images/diagrams/hinge-types.svg)

1. **Top pin** of a gravity hinge.
2. **Bottom cam:** sets where the door rests.
3. **Wrap-around flanges:** one wraps the pilaster end, one the door edge.
4. **Continuous hinge** running the full height of the door.

The bottom cam sets the rest position. On ASI metal partitions, the lowest point of the cam pintle sets it. Scranton's wrap-around hinges adjust in 30-degree steps. Accessible doors must be self-closing under the 2010 ADA Standards (604.8.1.2). Bobrick says to lubricate hinges with a PTFE-based lubricant. See [hinges](/hardware/hinges) for detail.

### Grab bars

Grab bar sizes and positions are set by the 2010 ADA Standards (see sections 604 and 609 of the Standards and our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance)). Do not assume a partition panel can carry a grab bar. Ask the manufacturer in writing whether your partition is rated for it, and have the bar fixed to backing provided by the building.

### Heights

Accessible compartment hardware (pulls and latches) must be mounted 34" minimum to 48" maximum above the floor (404.2.7). A coat hook in an accessible compartment must be within a reach range in section 308. For an unobstructed forward or side reach, that is 48" maximum and 15" minimum (604.8.3, 308.2.1, 308.3.1).

## Problems

Most toilet partition installation problems come down to level, backing or hinge and keeper settings. Check these first, and follow your manufacturer's guide for the fix.

| Problem | First checks |
|---|---|
| Door won't latch | The keeper may have moved or the door has dropped. Adjust the hinge first, then re-align the keeper with the latch bar. See [locks and latches](/hardware/locks-latches). |
| Door sags or rests in the wrong position | Adjust the bottom hinge cam, then lubricate it. Replace a worn cam or missing nylon pintle. |
| Uneven gaps | Check that the pilasters are plumb and at the same height, and that door tops are parallel with the headrail (OGS). |
| Pilaster not plumb | Re-set the leveling device or leveling nuts at the foot, or the top on ceiling styles. |
| Loose panels | Check the wall backing behind the brackets and the bracket fasteners. |

If a problem is not on this list, call the manufacturer.

## FAQ

### How do you install bathroom partitions?
In general: confirm the wall, floor and ceiling structure, mark the layout from the approved shop drawings, set and level the pilasters, fit the brackets and panels, add the headrail where the style has one, then hang and adjust the doors and align the latch and keeper. Your manufacturer's guide gives the exact steps.

### What do ceiling hung toilet partitions need?
Structural steel above the finished ceiling at every pilaster, designed by the architect or structural engineer and supplied by others. The finished ceiling itself carries no load.

### Do I need blocking in the walls?
Manufacturers call for backing behind the wall brackets. Bobrick states wall backing is required, and Bradley warns to confirm wall and floor backing before mounting.

### Why won't my stall door latch?
Often the door has dropped or the keeper has moved. Adjust the hinge cam first, then re-align the keeper with the latch bar.

### Does Next Partitions install the partitions?
No. We sell partitions and do not install them. Use a qualified installer and follow the manufacturer's instructions and your approved drawings.
