# How to Install Bathroom Partitions

**This page is an overview, not installation instructions.** Follow the manufacturer's installation instructions for your product and the approved shop drawings, and use a qualified installer. Next Partitions sells partitions. We do not install them, and we are not responsible for installation. Details change by brand, series and material.

Knowing how to install bathroom partitions helps you plan the job, check the building and spot problems early. This guide covers what to check first, the general order of work for each mounting style, how the hardware goes on, and what to do when a door won't latch or a pilaster won't stand plumb. Every step here comes from manufacturer literature and specifications. Your product's own guide always wins. To find it, see [bathroom partition installation instructions](/guides/installation-instructions).

Not sure which brand you have? Start with [our brands](/brands).

## Before you start

Most installation problems begin before anyone picks up a drill. Check these four things first.

### Tools and parts

Read the manufacturer's guide all the way through and lay out every part before you begin. The guide for your series lists the tools and fasteners. Anchors and fasteners differ by floor type and wall type. Use the ones the manufacturer specifies for your series. Do not substitute.

### Wall blocking and backing

Panels and pilasters hang on the walls by brackets, and the brackets are only as strong as what is behind them. **Before anything is drilled, confirm that the walls and their backing can carry the partitions.** Manufacturers open their installation guides with this warning, and a hollow drywall with nothing behind it gives the brackets nothing to hold.

The drawing cuts through the wall at one bracket, seen from above. The bracket screws go through the wall finish into anchors set in the backing. Use the anchors and fasteners your manufacturer's guide specifies; the sizes shown are typical for phenolic and steel lines.

![Wall cut through at a partition bracket, seen from above: wall finish, backing behind it, and the bracket screwed into anchors](/images/diagrams/wall-backing.svg)

1. Wall finish
2. Backing, at every bracket
3. Plastic anchor, 5/16" hole, 2" min deep
4. Screw, #14 x 2"
5. Wall bracket
6. Panel, with its gap to the wall

The wall finish alone, tile, board or plaster, never holds a bracket. Drill through it into the backing, and use the bracket itself as the template for its holes.

### Handling and site conditions

Manufacturers ask for care before the first hole is drilled.

- **Lay material flat.** Bradley says to lay panels flat and not lean them against a wall or stack them unevenly, to prevent warping. Hadrian says the same for solid plastic and asks that it stay at about 68 degrees F for 24 hours before and after installation. Bradley's Bradmar plastic guide asks for 24 hours to adjust to the site, a climate-controlled room and shelter from direct sunlight.
- **Keep protective masking on** stainless steel (and Bradmar plastic) until installation is finished, per Bradley.
- **Support the material when drilling.** Bradley's phenolic guides say to use a support block when drilling through the material to minimize break-out. Its steel guides say to take extra care where a hole goes through one face only, to prevent dimpling.
- **Clean, smooth walls and floors.** Bradley asks you to remove protruding nails and other debris first.

### Slab and ceiling structure check

The floor and ceiling carry the load, and each mounting style asks for something different.

| Mounting style | Floor | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| [Overhead braced](/mounting-styles/overhead-braced) | Floor that accepts anchors; NY OGS spec calls for at least 1-3/4" into the structural floor | Nothing above the ceiling needed |
| [Floor mounted](/mounting-styles/floor-mounted) | Structural concrete; Bobrick anchors need 2" minimum into concrete at least 3" thick | Nothing needed |
| [Ceiling hung](/mounting-styles/ceiling-hung) | Floor stays clear | Structural steel above the finished ceiling, by others |
| [Floor to ceiling](/mounting-styles/floor-to-ceiling) | Concrete floor | Structural members at the ceiling; Bobrick does not furnish them |

![The four bathroom partition mounting styles side by side](/images/diagrams/mounting-styles-compare.svg)

1. **Overhead braced:** headrail across the pilaster tops.
2. **Floor mounted:** pilasters anchored in the floor only.
3. **Ceiling hung:** pilasters hang from steel above the ceiling, floor clear.
4. **Floor to ceiling:** pilasters anchored at floor and ceiling.

The support system is shown in orange. Have your contractor or structural engineer confirm the structure at each pilaster location before partitions ship.

### Layout from approved shop drawings

Set out the job from the approved shop drawings, not from memory or from the old stalls. Measure the room and compare it with the drawings before you start. If a wall or column is not where the drawing says, stop and resolve it with the architect. What to measure in an empty room is in [how to measure a restroom](/guides/restroom-layout).

## The general sequence for overhead-braced and floor-mounted

Most lines go in the same order. The two strips below are the whole job at a glance: each frame shows the same two stalls from above and from the front, with that step in orange. The steps after them explain each one. Your shop drawings and the manufacturer's instructions have the final word.

![Installation sequence, steps 1 to 4: layout, wall brackets, wall pilasters, panels](/images/diagrams/install-sequence-1.svg)

![Installation sequence, steps 5 to 7: headrail, doors, hinges and latches](/images/diagrams/install-sequence-2.svg)

### 1. Mark the layout

Everything else hangs off these lines, so mark them once and check them twice. Snap the pilaster line on the floor, then draw a plumb line on the walls at every pilaster and panel centerline. Bracket heights are measured from the highest point of the floor and leveled across to every plumb line.

![Layout marks: plumb lines on the side and back walls with bracket marks at 19 and 63 inches, and the pilaster line on the floor](/images/diagrams/install-layout-marks.svg)

1. **Pilaster plumb line** on the side wall, where the pilaster line meets the wall.
2. **Panel plumb lines** on the back wall, one at each panel centerline.
3. **Bracket marks** at 19" and 63" above the floor on many lines.
4. **Third bracket** (optional) at 43".
5. **Continuous brackets** (optional): one mark at 12-1/2".
6. **Pilaster line** on the floor: the back-wall-to-face dimension from the shop drawings, less a small set-back (3/8" on some lines, 5/8" on others).

Use the drawing dimension for the pilaster line, not the room depth, and make any change before you drill.

### 2. Fix the wall brackets

![Stirrup brackets: one-ear and two-ear, with the opening for a pilaster or a panel](/images/diagrams/bracket-types-m1.svg "side")

Fix each bracket at its mark, into solid backing, using the bracket itself as the drilling template. Holes for the wall anchors are typically 5/16" and at least 2" deep. Pilaster brackets have a wider opening than panel brackets (3/4" and 1/2" on phenolic lines).

### 3. Stand and level the pilasters

![The pilaster foot: leveling bar, bolts and anchors under a stainless steel shoe](/images/diagrams/mounting-overhead-braced-detail-anchor.svg "side")

Each pilaster stands on a floor bracket or leveling device: a bar or bracket with leveling bolts, anchored into the floor. Slide the shoe up out of the way, set the pilaster plumb and at height, and level the pilaster tops from one compartment to the next. Lower the shoe over its clip last.

### 4. Hang the panels

Rest each panel on a spacer block, about 12" high on most lines (14" on some solid plastic lines), while it slides into its wall brackets. Leave the gap at the wall that the drawing shows, and wedge a shim at the top rear to hold the panel while you fix it to the pilaster. Panel and pilaster then alternate to the far end of the run.

### 5. Fit the headrail (overhead braced only)

![The headrail clamped over the top of a pilaster](/images/diagrams/mounting-overhead-braced-detail-headrail.svg "side")

The headrail clamps over the pilaster tops and fastens to the walls at both ends, with at least two fasteners into each pilaster. Floor-mounted stalls have no headrail, which is why their floor anchors go deeper.

### 6. Hang the doors and set the gaps

Hang each door on its spacer, then set the gaps along its edges before you tighten the hinge screws. The drawing shows typical gaps, drawn wider than scale so they show. Your shop drawings set the actual sizes.

![Gaps between a door and its pilasters, a wall pilaster and the wall, and a panel and the back wall, with the 12-inch spacer](/images/diagrams/install-gaps.svg)

1. **Hinge side:** 1/8" between the door and the pilaster.
2. **Latch side:** 1/8", set with spacers at the top and bottom of the door.
3. **Wall pilaster to wall:** 1/2" to 1-1/4", as the drawing shows.
4. **12" spacer** under the door while the hinges go on.
5. **Panel to back wall:** 1" standard.
6. **Panel to pilaster:** 1/2" standard.

Adjust the doors so their tops run parallel with the headrail.

### 7. Set the hinges and latches

Set the rest position at the hinge, then fit the keeper in line with the latch. Both are covered under [hardware](#hardware) below.

## Ceiling hung

Ceiling hung is the style where the building matters most: the support steel must exist before the partitions arrive. The front view, side view and details are on the [ceiling hung](/mounting-styles/ceiling-hung) page.

- **Structural steel above the ceiling.** A steel member, usually a channel, sits above the finished ceiling at every pilaster. The architect or structural engineer designs it, and another trade supplies, installs and drills it. It is never included with the partitions.
- **Load.** The structure must carry the partitions and stay rigid when doors swing and get pushed. One manufacturer asks for at least 275 to 295 lb per partition, depending on the material.
- **Steel, not wood.** Wood construction doesn't give adequate support, and the finished ceiling itself must carry nothing.
- **Hanger and leveling.** A threaded rod hangs from the steel through the ceiling into each pilaster, with nuts above and below the steel. The pilaster is leveled on the nuts, and a stainless steel shoe covers the hanger at the ceiling.
- **Bracing.** Tall ceilings need it: lines call for a stabilizer bar across the pilaster backs or cross bracing at ceilings of 9 feet and above.
- **Ceiling height.** A standard ceiling-hung pilaster suits an 8' ceiling, and most lines make them to length up to about 10'.
- **Order on site.** Leave the shoe down until the pilasters are level and the panels are fastened, then raise it to the ceiling. Slotted mounting bars allow a little side-to-side adjustment to set the door openings.

The panel, door, hinge and latch steps then follow the same pattern as above.

## Floor to ceiling

Floor-to-ceiling pilasters are anchored at both ends. The NY OGS spec calls for anchoring assemblies with leveling adjustment nuts at the tops and bottoms of the pilasters, with a shoe at the floor and a sleeve at the ceiling. Bobrick makes its floor-to-ceiling stiles to length for ceilings up to 10'. Consult the manufacturer for what the ceiling structure must be. Doors and panels hang level and aligned with each other.

## Hardware

### Latch, keeper and bumper

![Bathroom stall latch on the door and keeper on the pilaster](/images/diagrams/latch-keeper.svg)

1. **Latch**, mounted on the door; the bolt slides into the keeper.
2. **Keeper (strike)**, mounted on the pilaster.
3. **Occupancy indicator** on an indicator latch.

For a slide latch install, ASI Global's guide aligns the keeper with the latch bar while the door is closed and the latch engaged, then drills the pilaster through the keeper's holes. Close the door and check the fit before drilling. Fit the bumper so it strikes the wall before any other part of the door (ASI). Latches and strikes are not interchangeable across systems. Scranton states its latches and strikes cannot be mixed.

### Hinge setting

![Gravity (cam), wrap-around and continuous bathroom stall hinges](/images/diagrams/hinge-types.svg)

1. **Top pin** of a gravity hinge.
2. **Bottom cam:** sets where the door rests.
3. **Wrap-around flanges:** one wraps the pilaster end, one the door edge.
4. **Continuous hinge** running the full height of the door.

Do not assemble a two-part latch before it goes through the door: some use pressure-sensitive parts that cannot be taken apart again (Ammco). Tap hinge pins in gently and do not overtighten the cam nut, which can damage the pintle. A surface-mounted continuous hinge on a solid plastic door is set closed for doors 30" and wider, leaves about 1/8" between door and pilaster, and is not field adjustable (Ammco).

The bottom cam sets the rest position. On ASI metal partitions, the lowest point of the cam pintle sets it. Scranton's wrap-around hinges adjust in 30-degree steps. Accessible doors must be self-closing under the 2010 ADA Standards (604.8.1.2). Bobrick says to lubricate hinges with a PTFE-based lubricant. See [hinges](/hardware/hinges) for detail.

### Grab bars

Grab bar sizes and positions are set by the 2010 ADA Standards (sections 604 and 609; see also our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance)). In a wheelchair accessible compartment, grab bars go on the side wall closest to the toilet and on the rear wall (604.5).

- **Side wall bar:** 42" long minimum, no more than 12" from the rear wall, and extending at least 54" from the rear wall (604.5.1).
- **Rear wall bar:** 36" long minimum, extending 12" minimum on one side of the toilet centerline and 24" minimum on the other (604.5.2).
- **Height:** horizontal, 33" minimum to 36" maximum above the floor, measured to the top of the gripping surface (609.4).
- **Spacing:** 1-1/2" between the wall and the bar (609.3). Round bars are 1-1/4" to 2" in outside diameter (609.2.1).
- **Strength:** the bar, its fasteners and the supporting structure must withstand a 250 lb force without exceeding allowable stresses (609.8).
- **Ambulatory compartments:** a side wall bar is required on both sides (604.8.2.3).

The drawing puts these positions in three views of one wheelchair accessible compartment: the rear wall, the side wall closest to the toilet, and the inside of the door near the latch.

![Rear wall, side wall and door of a wheelchair accessible compartment with grab bar, seat, dispenser, latch and coat hook heights](/images/diagrams/accessible-stall-heights.svg)

1. **Side wall grab bar** (604.5.1): 42" long minimum, starting no more than 12" from the rear wall and reaching at least 54" from it.
2. **Rear wall grab bar** (604.5.2): 36" long minimum, reaching at least 12" from the toilet centerline towards the side wall and 24" towards the open side. Both bars are 33" to 36" above the floor to the top of the gripping surface (609.4).
3. **Toilet seat** 17" to 19" above the floor to the top of the seat (604.4), with the toilet centerline 16" to 18" from the side wall (604.2).
4. **Toilet paper dispenser** (604.7): its centerline 7" to 9" in front of the toilet, the outlet 15" minimum and 48" maximum above the floor, and not behind a grab bar.
5. **Latch and pulls:** operable parts 34" to 48" above the floor (404.2.7), with a pull on both sides of the door near the latch (604.8.1.2).
6. **Coat hook** within a reach range in section 308: 15" to 48" above the floor for an unobstructed reach (604.8.3).

Do not assume a partition panel can carry a grab bar. Bradley's Series 400 steel guide refers to "grab bar reinforcement" in some panels and tells the installer to turn the panel so the reinforcement hole or holes sit on the bottom edge, but it does not state a load rating. Ask the manufacturer in writing whether your partition is rated for it, and have the bar fixed to backing provided by the building.

### Heights

Accessible compartment hardware (pulls and latches) must be mounted 34" minimum to 48" maximum above the floor (404.2.7). A coat hook in an accessible compartment must be within a reach range in section 308. For an unobstructed forward or side reach, that is 48" maximum and 15" minimum (604.8.3, 308.2.1, 308.3.1). A shelf in the compartment must be 40" minimum to 48" maximum above the floor (604.8.3).

*Disclaimer: ADA dimensions and accessibility guidance shown here are provided for general informational purposes only. Codes and their interpretation vary by jurisdiction and project. We accept no responsibility or liability for compliance — always verify requirements with your architect, engineer, or local code official before ordering.*

Manufacturers also give their own mounting heights. Bradley's Series 400 phenolic guide puts the coat hook 36" above the finished floor on the inside face of the door, the slide latch at 41" above the floor, and the door pulls on 34" to 36" doors at 42-3/4" above the floor (on a 58" door, 30-3/4" up from the bottom edge). Bradley tells installers to check local codes before fixing the pulls and hook. Its 36" hook height sits inside the 15" to 48" reach range above, but confirm your accessible stall against the code that applies.

## Problems

Most toilet partition installation problems come down to level, backing or hinge and keeper settings. Check these first, and follow your manufacturer's guide for the fix.

The three drawings below show the usual reasons a door won't latch, seen from inside the stall with the hinge pilaster on the left. The angles are exaggerated so they show.

![Three causes of a door that won't latch: pilaster out of plumb, door dropped at the latch side, keeper out of line](/images/diagrams/door-latch-problems.svg)

- **A** Pilaster out of plumb: the gap at the latch edge is wider at the top than at the bottom. Check the pilaster with a level and re-set it at its leveling screw or nuts and its brackets. Manufacturers say to check that the pilasters are plumb before the hinges go on.
- **B** Door dropped at the latch side: the top of the door slopes and the latch sits below the keeper. Check that the hinges are plumb and their screws tight; reset the door with 1/8" spacers at the top and bottom so the gaps match, then re-tighten.
- **C** Keeper out of line with the latch: the door hangs square but the latch misses the keeper. With the door closed, move the keeper so the latch slides freely into it, and drill through the keeper's own holes.

| Problem | First checks |
|---|---|
| Door won't latch | The keeper may have moved or the door has dropped. Adjust the hinge first, then re-align the keeper with the latch bar. See [locks and latches](/hardware/locks-latches). |
| Door sags or rests in the wrong position | Adjust the bottom hinge cam (loosen the nut, set the door, re-tighten without forcing), then lubricate it. Continuous hinges on solid plastic doors are not adjustable; check the door height and gap instead. Replace a worn cam or missing nylon pintle. |
| Uneven gaps | Check that the pilasters are plumb and at the same height, and that door tops are parallel with the headrail (OGS). |
| Pilaster not plumb | Re-set the leveling device or leveling nuts at the foot, or the top on ceiling styles. If the pilaster shifted off the door opening, loosen the nut, slide it along the slotted mounting bar and re-tighten. |
| Loose panels | Check the wall backing behind the brackets and the bracket fasteners. |

If a problem is not on this list, call the manufacturer.

## FAQ

### How do you install bathroom partitions?
In general: confirm the wall, floor and ceiling structure, mark the layout from the approved shop drawings, set and level the pilasters, fit the brackets and panels, add the headrail where the style has one, then hang and adjust the doors and align the latch and keeper. Your manufacturer's guide gives the exact steps.

### What do ceiling hung toilet partitions need?
Structural steel above the finished ceiling at every pilaster, designed by the architect or structural engineer and supplied by others. The finished ceiling itself carries no load.

### Do I need blocking in the walls?
Manufacturers call for backing behind the wall brackets. Bobrick states wall backing is required, and Bradley warns to confirm wall and floor backing before mounting.

### Why won't my stall door latch?
Often the door has dropped or the keeper has moved. Adjust the hinge cam first, then re-align the keeper with the latch bar.

### Does Next Partitions install the partitions?
No. We sell partitions and do not install them. Use a qualified installer and follow the manufacturer's instructions and your approved drawings.
