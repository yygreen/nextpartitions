## The right material for Louisville's climate

Louisville gets about 48 inches of rain a year, on roughly 124 days, and relative humidity averages 79% in the morning and 56% in the afternoon. In most Louisville restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Louisville restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Kentucky's building rules set the sizes. Kentucky has no separate access registration: the plans are checked for access when the building permit is reviewed, by the local building department or, for larger buildings and all schools, by the state.
- **Fire safety:** Kentucky's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Kentucky's plumbing rules set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. They are the same statewide, Louisville can't change them, and state plumbing inspectors enforce them here. The table below shows what that means.
- **Kentucky extras:** Kentucky's rules add an 18 inch vertical grab bar on the side wall of each accessible stall, which the federal rules don't ask for. A restroom with six or more toilets and urinals also needs a narrower stall with grab bars on both sides for people who walk with aids, as under federal law. Restaurants, other assembly buildings and stores that need six or more toilets in total must add a family or assisted-use restroom.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** Kentucky requires men's, women's and unisex toilet rooms to be clearly marked, and has no rule that a one-person restroom be open to everyone. Its rules count toilets separately for men and women, and name unisex rooms in place of separate ones only for restaurants holding up to 15 people and listed historic buildings holding 100 or fewer. They don't say whether a shared all-gender room is allowed elsewhere, so ask the Kentucky Division of Plumbing. In public schools, state law says students may not use restrooms reserved for the other biological sex, and names single-stall restrooms as an accommodation.
- **Permits:** Louisville Metro Construction Review issues building permits in Louisville under Kentucky's statewide building rules, which require a permit to alter or repair a building unless the work is exempt. The exempt list covers "painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, counter tops, and similar finish work" and movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches that aren't fixed in place. It doesn't name toilet partitions, so ask Construction Review (502-574-3321) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Louisville Metro Construction Review or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Louisville restroom needs

Kentucky counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women unless the plans show otherwise. Every toilet needs a stall. In restaurants and schools, Kentucky's rules set a number of urinals for the men's room on top of the men's toilets, and each urinal needs a privacy screen. For offices the rules give one total for the building, urinals are optional, and a workplace with more than five employees needs separate facilities for each sex.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: =3 | 1/1 | 2/1+1
100: =5 | 1/1+1 | 3/2+2
200: =7-8 | 1/1+1 | 6/2+4
400: =12-13 | 2/1+1 | 8/3+6
:::

Kentucky's office figures are totals for the building. Its rules don't say how they divide between the women's and men's rooms, or whether part of the next 40 people adds a toilet, so confirm both with the Kentucky Division of Plumbing. Where the men's room needs three or more toilets, up to a third may be swapped for extra urinals. These are minimums, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, Kentucky's building rules count an office at one person per 100 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Louisville

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Louisville/Jefferson County area earn a median of $27.17 an hour, about 7% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Kentucky repealed its wage law for public construction in 2017, so a set wage applies only on federally funded jobs, under the federal Davis-Bacon Act. For a carpenter on building work in Jefferson County that rate is $30.80 an hour plus $22.34 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 6% in Louisville, all of it Kentucky's state tax.
- **Tax on installation:** in Kentucky, a contractor's labor to improve or repair a building is not taxed. A contractor who supplies and installs the partitions pays the 6% tax on the materials and does not add sales tax to the contract.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Louisville are taxed at Kentucky's 6%, freight included. Kentucky has no city or county sales tax. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Kentucky Department of Revenue.
