## The right material for Providence's climate

Providence gets about 48 inches of rain a year, on roughly 125 days, and relative humidity averages 75% in the morning and 55% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Providence restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Rhode Island's building rules both set the sizes, and the local building official checks them with the building permit. No separate state review step was found.
- **Fire safety:** Rhode Island's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene partitions to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Rhode Island's plumbing rules set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. State law doesn't let cities change them, so Providence applies them as written. The table below shows what that means.
- **Rhode Island extras:** the state's rules add to the federal ones in a few places. The accessible stall needs a vertical grab bar, at least 18 inches long, beside the horizontal one on the side wall. Where a partition must leave toe space underneath, it is 12 inches high, against 9 inches federally. In a large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible. A restaurant or shop that needs six or more toilets in total must also have a single-user family restroom.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** Rhode Island law makes every one-person restroom in a public building or a business open to the public available to any gender. A sign is optional, and if there is one it must say restroom without naming one gender. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view.
- **Permits:** Rhode Island law requires a permit to alter a building but not for ordinary repairs. Providence's list of work that needs no permit covers painting, tiling, cabinets, counter tops "and similar finish work", and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high. It doesn't name toilet partitions, so ask the Providence Department of Inspection and Standards whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Providence Department of Inspection and Standards or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Providence restroom needs

Rhode Island's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in each men's room they may replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a privacy screen. The table shows all the men's fixtures as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and the same table applies in every Rhode Island city and town. An office holding 25 people or fewer, or any space holding 15 or fewer, may use one restroom for everyone, and a restaurant's seasonal outdoor seating counts toward its capacity. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Providence

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Providence–Warwick area earn a median of $29.76 an hour, about 2% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on state, city, town and public school projects over $1,000, Rhode Island law requires at least the prevailing wage, which the state Department of Labor and Training takes from the federal wage decisions and updates each July 1. For a carpenter on building work in Providence County that is $47.88 an hour plus $30.50 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 7% in Providence, which is Rhode Island's state rate. No city or county sales tax on goods was found.
- **Tax on installation:** in Rhode Island, a charge for installing what you bought is not taxed when it is listed separately on the bill, and the seller must list it separately if you ask. On a construction contract, the contractor pays the tax on the materials when buying them. Ask the Rhode Island Division of Taxation which rule covers a partition supply-and-install job.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Providence are taxable at 7%, freight included. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Rhode Island Division of Taxation.
