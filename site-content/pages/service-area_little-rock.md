## The right material for Little Rock's climate

Little Rock gets about 50 inches of rain a year, on roughly 105 days, and relative humidity averages 84% in the morning and 57% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Little Rock restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and the access standard in Arkansas's building rules set the sizes. No separate state registration or review step for access was found, so plans go through the local building department.
- **Fire safety:** Arkansas's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Little Rock enforces Arkansas's plumbing rules, which set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means. On commercial plumbing work the city asks for an approval letter from the Arkansas Department of Health, which checks the count.
- **Arkansas extras:** the access standard in Arkansas's building rules asks for two things the federal rules don't. The accessible stall needs an 18 inch vertical grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal one. The gap under the front and one side partition must be 12 inches high, against 9 inches federally, unless the stall is larger than the minimum.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom is marked for use by either sex. Arkansas's plumbing rules call for separate men's and women's rooms once a space holds more than 25 people, and they don't provide for a shared all-gender room with several stalls, so ask the Arkansas Department of Health before planning one. Arkansas law requires shared restrooms in buildings occupied by state or local government, and in public schools, to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** Little Rock requires a permit for any work that costs more than $5,000, material and labor together, or that needs an inspection. Painting, floor coverings and cosmetic changes are excluded. The state building rules the city enforces also exempt finish work such as painting, tiling and cabinets, and movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches. Neither list names toilet partitions, so ask the Building Codes Division permit desk (501-371-4832) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Little Rock Building Codes Division or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Little Rock restroom needs

Arkansas's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and fractions round up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to two thirds of the toilets in a restaurant or school and up to half in an office, and each urinal needs a privacy screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

A space that holds 25 people or fewer, staff and customers together, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. Banks and credit unions don't have to provide public restrooms. These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, Arkansas's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.
