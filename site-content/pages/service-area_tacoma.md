## The right material for Tacoma's climate

The nearest long-term weather station, at Seattle, records about 39 inches of rain a year, on roughly 156 days, and relative humidity averages 82% in the morning and 63% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month there is November (about 6.3 inches) and the driest is July (about 0.6 inches). What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Tacoma restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Washington's own access rules set the sizes.
- **Fire safety:** Washington's building rules, which Tacoma enforces, require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Washington sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building, and Tacoma applies the state count without changes. The table below shows what that means. Tacoma Planning and Development Services issues the plumbing permits as well as the building permits.
- **Washington extras:** stalls here need a second, upright grab bar on the side wall, and a wheelchair stall needs more toe room under its partitions (12 inches high, against 9 under the federal rules). In a large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, not just one. Large stores and venues also need a family restroom.
- **Rule update planned for May 3, 2027:** Washington's code council plans to bring in the next edition of the state building and plumbing rules on that date, and Tacoma applies state updates on the day they take effect.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** in Tacoma, a one-person restroom in a business open to the public, a city building or another public agency's building must be open to anyone and signed that way. That covers existing restrooms as well as new ones. Under state rules, a shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet and urinal is in its own compartment with full-height walls and a door that locks from the inside.
- **Permits:** Tacoma requires a permit for most construction work unless it is on the city's exempt list. The list covers "similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high, without naming toilet partitions, so ask Tacoma Planning and Development Services (253-591-5030) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Tacoma Planning and Development Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Tacoma restroom needs

Tacoma uses Washington's count, which goes by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Washington sets its own count for schools, with more toilets for girls than for boys. Urinals are optional and may stand in for most of the men's toilets.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 2/2
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 4/3
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 8/6
:::

Small businesses may be allowed one shared restroom: up to 25 people in an office and up to 30 in a restaurant. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. Confirm your project with Tacoma Planning and Development Services.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor and a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet. A school is counted at one student per 100 square feet of the whole building. Your architect or Tacoma Planning and Development Services sets the final number.
