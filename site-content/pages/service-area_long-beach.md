## The right material for Long Beach's climate

Long Beach gets about 12 inches of rain a year, on roughly 34 days, and relative humidity averages 75% in the morning and 54% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is February (about 3.0 inches) and the driest is August (almost none). The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Long Beach restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and California's own access rules both set the sizes, and California's are stricter in places. A remodel can also oblige you to bring the restrooms serving that area up to today's rules; on projects under $209,208 (the 2026 figure) that duty is limited.
- **Fire safety:** building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Long Beach uses California's rules, which set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **California extras:** California asks more of a stall than the federal rules. In a large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, not just one. The accessible stall needs more open floor in front of the toilet, so it's deeper, and the aisle outside its door must be 48 inches wide, against 42. Stall doors need a privacy latch, and every restroom door carries California's circle or triangle symbol. Give your layout to your architect before you order.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be signed as all-gender. A shared all-gender room is allowed in place of separate men's and women's rooms if every toilet is in a floor-to-ceiling privacy compartment whose door locks from the inside and shows when it's occupied.
- **Permits:** Long Beach requires a building permit to alter, repair or remodel any part of a building unless the work is on its exempt list. The list covers work valued at $1,000 or less, unless it affects fire safety, the structure or a required accessible route, and "nonfixed and movable" partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high. It doesn't name toilet partitions, so ask the Building and Safety Bureau (562-570-7648) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Long Beach Building and Safety Bureau or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Long Beach restroom needs

Long Beach counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and California's rules require urinals in the men's room. Every toilet needs a stall, and urinals need screens between them. The women's room must have at least as many toilets as the men's room has toilets and urinals together.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 2/1+1 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
100: 3/1+1 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
200: 4/2+1 | 3/2+1 | 4/2+1
400: 8/3+2 | 4/3+1 | 7/4+2
:::

These are minimums for new buildings, additions and changes of use that bring in more people. An office or shop with 50 people or fewer may have a single one-person restroom. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, California's plumbing rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, and a restaurant dining room or a classroom at one per 30 square feet. Your architect or the Long Beach Building and Safety Bureau sets the final number.
