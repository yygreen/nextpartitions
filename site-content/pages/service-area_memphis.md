## The right material for the Memphis climate

Memphis gets about 55 inches of rain a year, on roughly 109 days, and relative humidity averages 80% in the morning and 56% in the afternoon. In most Memphis restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Memphis restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Tennessee law uses the federal access standards as its own for public buildings. Memphis and Shelby County's building rules also apply a national accessibility standard, in its 2017 edition, and the plans are approved by the office that reviews the project.
- **Fire safety:** the building rules Memphis enforces require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Memphis sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Tennessee and federal stall rules match:** Tennessee adopted the federal standards word for word, so a stall, door or grab bar that meets one meets the other. The 2017 standard in Memphis and Shelby County's building rules is a separate document, so ask Construction Enforcement which sizes it checks on your project.
- **Urinals:** Memphis and Shelby County don't allow waterless, trough or floor-mounted urinals.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom is marked as open to everyone. Memphis and Shelby County removed from their plumbing rules the allowance for a shared all-gender room and the allowance for a building served only by one-person rooms, so those rules call for separate men's and women's rooms except in the small spaces noted under the table. The building rules still carry both allowances, so ask Construction Enforcement before you plan either layout. Since July 2026, state law requires shared restrooms in public colleges and universities, state prisons, juvenile detention facilities and domestic violence shelters to be designated for females, males or families. A 2021 law covers shared restrooms in public schools.
- **Permits:** Memphis and Shelby County require a permit to alter or repair a building. Their list of work that needs no permit includes painting, tiling, cabinets, counter tops and similar finish work, and movable partitions not over 5 feet 9 inches high. It doesn't name toilet partitions. Ordinary repairs need no application as long as no wall or partition is cut away. Ask Construction Enforcement (901-222-8300) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Memphis and Shelby County Construction Enforcement or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Memphis restroom needs

Memphis counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and every toilet needs a stall with a door. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, each with a privacy screen. Where a men's room needs three fixtures, two of them must be toilets. The table shows toilets only.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. An office for 25 people or fewer, or any space for 15 or fewer, may use one shared room. Memphis and Shelby County add rules of their own. In an office building with several tenants, every floor needs shared men's and women's rooms sized for that floor, and toilets inside a tenant's own suite don't count toward them. Toilets must be on the same floor as the space they serve and no more than 300 feet away. A restaurant or nightclub in a covered mall needs its own toilets inside its space.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Memphis

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Memphis area earn a median of $24.34 an hour, about 16% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Tennessee's wage law for public work covers state highway projects only, so on a school or city building a set wage applies only where federal money brings in the federal wage rules. The federal rate for a carpenter on building work in Shelby County is $30.20 an hour plus $14.74 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 9.75% in Memphis: 7% state and 2.75% local. The local part applies only to the first $1,600 of the price of each single item, and the state adds 2.75% on the part of an item's price between $1,600 and $3,200.
- **Tax on installation:** in Tennessee, labor to install something that becomes part of the building is not taxed when it is billed separately. A contractor who supplies and installs pays the tax on the materials and builds it into the price, rather than adding it to your invoice.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Memphis are taxable, freight included when the seller bills it. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Tennessee Department of Revenue.
