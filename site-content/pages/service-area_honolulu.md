## The right material for Honolulu's climate

Honolulu gets about 16 inches of rain a year, on roughly 92 days, and relative humidity averages 74% in the morning and 56% in the afternoon. The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Honolulu restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Hawaii applies the federal access rules. With a building permit application, the owner or designer states in writing that the access rules will be met. Plans for state and county buildings, and for buildings on state or county land, also go to the state's Disability and Communication Access Board for review before construction.
- **Fire safety:** Hawaii's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Honolulu sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building, and lets urinals replace up to half of the men's toilets. The table below shows what that means.
- **Hawaii and federal stall rules match:** Hawaii uses the federal rules for stalls, doors and grab bars, so a stall that meets one meets the other.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** under the building rules Honolulu uses, a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. Where toilets are required, the rules call for separate men's and women's rooms and don't cover a shared all-gender room with several stalls, so ask the Department of Planning and Permitting before planning one. Honolulu adds no rule of its own. State law bars places open to the public from discriminating on the basis of gender identity or expression. Buildings open to the public that are designed after July 31, 2027 must include an adult changing station in a public restroom; public schools are exempt.
- **Permits:** Honolulu requires a building permit to alter or repair a building and lists the work that needs none. The list covers like-for-like repairs for maintenance worth up to $10,000 in any 12 months, and interior remodeling that leaves rooms, walls and windows where they are, such as painting, floor covering, cabinets, countertops and replacing existing fixtures. Both apply only where no electrical, plumbing or mechanical work is involved. It also covers movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches. It doesn't name toilet partitions fixed to the floor or wall, so ask the Department of Planning and Permitting's Building Division (808-768-8232) whether your replacement needs one. Work done for a government agency needs a permit only if the agency asks for one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Honolulu Department of Planning and Permitting or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Honolulu restroom needs

Honolulu's building rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to half of the required toilets, so the table shows the men's side as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

Honolulu applies these counts to new construction. A men's room that needs only one toilet can't swap it for a urinal. An office for up to 25 people, or any space for up to 15, may use one shared restroom. A restaurant's seasonal outdoor seating counts toward the number of people. In a new entertainment center, movie theater, sports arena or similar building, and in alterations costing more than $500,000 to an existing one, there must be at least twice as many toilets for women visitors as for men. Ask the Department of Planning and Permitting how the counts apply to your project. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20 square feet.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Honolulu

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Urban Honolulu area earn a median of $45.34 an hour, about 56% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Hawaii requires state-set wages on public construction contracts over $2,000, and the state labor department publishes the rates each February and September. One schedule covers the whole state. For a journeyman carpenter the current rate is $56.75 an hour plus $30.49 in benefits.
- **General excise tax:** Hawaii has no sales tax. Its general excise tax is charged to the business, not the buyer: 4% for the state plus 0.5% for the City and County of Honolulu, 4.5% in all. A business may pass the tax on, and in Honolulu the most it may add to an invoice is 4.712%.
- **Tax on installation:** an installer owes the general excise tax on the full installation price, labor included, and may add it to the invoice.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Honolulu are taxed in one of two ways. A seller with enough Hawaii sales (at least $100,000 or 200 sales a year) owes the general excise tax and may pass it on. If the seller isn't licensed in Hawaii, the buyer owes use tax at the same rate on the price plus shipping.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Hawaii Department of Taxation.
