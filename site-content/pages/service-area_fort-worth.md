## The right material for Fort Worth's climate

The nearest long-term weather station, at Dallas, records about 38 inches of rain a year, on roughly 79 days, and relative humidity averages 78% in the morning and 54% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month there is October (about 4.8 inches) and the driest is July (about 1.7 inches). In most Fort Worth restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Fort Worth restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Texas's own access rules set the sizes. On projects of $50,000 or more, the state reviews the plans; smaller projects must still follow the rules.
- **Fire safety:** Fort Worth's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Fort Worth sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Texas and federal stall rules match:** Texas's own access rules for stalls, doors and grab bars read the same as the federal ones, so a stall that meets one meets the other.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom doesn't have to be designated for men or women. For larger restrooms, Fort Worth's plumbing rules call for separate men's and women's rooms and list no exception for a shared all-gender room, so ask Fort Worth Development Services before you plan one. Since December 2025, Texas law requires shared restrooms in city, county, school district and public college buildings to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** Fort Worth requires a permit to alter or repair a building unless the work is exempt. Its exempt list covers "similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high, without naming toilet partitions, so ask Fort Worth Development Services whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Fort Worth Development Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Fort Worth restroom needs

Fort Worth counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional and may stand in for some of the men's toilets. Outside schools, the city may approve fewer toilets if the applicant asks in writing and gives reasons.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. Where a building or tenant space holds 50 people or fewer, staff and customers together, Fort Worth doesn't require separate men's and women's rooms. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or Fort Worth Development Services sets the final number.
