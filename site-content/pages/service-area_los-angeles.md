## The right material for the Los Angeles climate

Los Angeles is dry: about 14 inches of rain a year, on roughly 35 days, with relative humidity averaging 58% at 4 a.m. and 54% at 4 p.m. On average the wettest month is February (about 3.6 inches) and the driest is August (almost none). The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool and beach facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Los Angeles restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and California's own access rules both set the sizes, and California's are stricter in places. A remodel can also oblige you to bring the restrooms serving that area up to today's rules; on projects under $209,208 (the 2026 figure) that duty is limited.
- **Fire safety:** building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Los Angeles sets the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **California extras:** California asks more of a stall than the federal rules. In a large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, not just one. The accessible stall needs more open floor in front of the toilet, so it's deeper, and the aisle outside its door must be 48 inches wide, against 42. Stall doors need a privacy latch, and every restroom door carries California's circle or triangle symbol. Give your layout to your architect before you order.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be signed as all-gender. A shared all-gender room is allowed in place of separate men's and women's rooms if every toilet is in a floor-to-ceiling privacy compartment whose door locks from the inside and shows when it's occupied.
- **Permits:** Los Angeles needs no permit for work worth $2,500 or less, or for "partitions, not over 5 feet 9 inches high", but the city doesn't say whether toilet partitions count. Ask the Department of Building and Safety (LADBS) before a replacement. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety (LADBS) or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Los Angeles restroom needs

Los Angeles counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and it requires urinals in the men's room. Every toilet needs a stall, and urinals need screens between them. The women's room must have at least as many toilets as the men's room has toilets and urinals together.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 2/1+1 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
100: 3/1+1 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
200: 4/2+1 | 3/2+1 | 4/2+1
400: 8/3+2 | 4/3+1 | 7/4+2
:::

These are minimums for new buildings, additions and changes of use that bring in more people; a remodel that doesn't add people can usually keep the toilets it has. A business with 50 people or fewer may be allowed a single one-person restroom, so ask LADBS. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, Los Angeles counts an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, and a restaurant dining room or a classroom at one per 30 square feet. Your architect or LADBS sets the final number.

## Installation in Los Angeles

Next Partitions supplies the partitions and the shop drawings; a contractor you hire installs them. Our directory of [partition installers in Los Angeles](/installers/california/los-angeles) lists local installers, and you can ask for their prices in your quote request.

California requires a CSLB contractor license, usually the C-61/D-34 Prefabricated Equipment classification, for partition installs of $1,000 or more; jobs needing workers or permits require a license at any price. Check a contractor's license on the CSLB website, and check local contractor licensing or registration with LADBS before work starts. You don't need a license or a permit to buy partitions.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Los Angeles

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Los Angeles–Long Beach–Anaheim area earn a median of $36.41 an hour, about 25% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** California requires the state-set wage on public jobs over $1,000, including city and school district work. For a carpenter in Los Angeles County that is $54.41 an hour plus $29.91 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 10.25% in Los Angeles since October 1, 2026: 7.25% statewide plus 3% in county district taxes.
- **Tax on installation:** in California, installation labor billed separately isn't taxed.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Los Angeles are taxable. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration.

### How many stalls does a Los Angeles restroom need?
For a building that holds 100 people, an office needs at least 3 women's stalls, 1 men's stall and 1 urinal, a restaurant needs at least 2 women's stalls, 1 men's stall and 1 urinal and a school needs at least 2 women's stalls, 1 men's stall and 1 urinal. The table above covers other sizes. Confirm the number for your building with the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety (LADBS).
