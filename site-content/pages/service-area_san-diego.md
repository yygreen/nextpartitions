## The right material for San Diego's climate

San Diego gets about 10 inches of rain a year, on roughly 41 days, and relative humidity averages 74% in the morning and 63% in the afternoon. The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a San Diego restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and California's own access rules both set the sizes, and California's are stricter in places. A remodel can also oblige you to bring the restrooms serving that area up to today's rules; on projects under $209,208 (the 2026 figure) that duty is limited.
- **Fire safety:** building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** San Diego uses California's rules, which set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **California extras:** California asks more of a stall than the federal rules. In a large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, not just one. The accessible stall needs more open floor in front of the toilet, so it's deeper, and the aisle outside its door must be 48 inches wide, against 42. Stall doors need a privacy latch, and every restroom door carries California's circle or triangle symbol. Give your layout to your architect before you order.
- **Privacy:** San Diego requires privacy partitions for toilets and urinals used by the public or by employees.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be signed as all-gender. A shared all-gender room is allowed in place of separate men's and women's rooms if every toilet is in a floor-to-ceiling privacy compartment whose door locks from the inside and shows when it's occupied.
- **Permits:** San Diego requires a building permit to alter, repair or improve a building unless the work is exempt. Its exempt list covers "partitions not over 5 feet 9 inches in height", "painting, papering, and similar finish work" and like-for-like repairs worth $1,000 or less, without naming toilet partitions, so ask the Development Services Department whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Development Services Department or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a San Diego restroom needs

San Diego counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and California's rules require urinals in the men's room. Every toilet needs a stall, and urinals need screens between them. The women's room must have at least as many toilets as the men's room has toilets and urinals together.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 2/1+1 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
100: 3/1+1 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
200: 4/2+1 | 3/2+1 | 4/2+1
400: 8/3+2 | 4/3+1 | 7/4+2
:::

These are minimums for new buildings, additions and changes of use that bring in more people. An office or shop with 50 people or fewer, or a restaurant with fewer than 50, may have a single one-person restroom. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, San Diego counts an office at one person per 200 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 30 square feet and a school at one per 50. Your architect or the Development Services Department sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in San Diego

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the San Diego–Chula Vista–Carlsbad area earn a median of $35.19 an hour, about 21% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** California requires the state-set wage on public jobs over $1,000, including city and school district work. For a carpenter on building work in San Diego County that is $51.11 an hour plus $29.60 in benefits. The City of San Diego enforces prevailing wages on the public works projects it awards.
- **Sales tax:** 7.75% in San Diego: 7.25% statewide plus a 0.5% county transportation tax. The city adds no tax of its own.
- **Tax on installation:** in California, installation labor billed separately isn't taxed.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to San Diego are taxable. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration.
