# Commercial Bathroom Partition FAQ

Answers to common questions about commercial bathroom partitions: what they cost, how big they are, what they are made of, how they ship and who installs them. Can't find yours? [Contact us](/contact) or [request a quote](/get-a-quote).

## Cost and quotes

### How much do commercial bathroom partitions cost?
Price depends on material, mounting style, color, dimensions and stall count. We don't publish a per-stall price because two stalls with the same material can differ in mounting, hardware and freight. Use the [configurator](/get-a-quote) to request a quote. Standard configurations are quoted within 2 business hours; custom projects within 24–48 hours.

### How does a quote work?
You choose material, mounting style, color, stall count and dimensions in the [configurator](/get-a-quote) and send the request. We quote it. Custom projects come with shop drawings. When you approve the quote and drawings, the partitions ship. The configurator is a quote request, not a purchase.

## Sizes and layout

### What is the standard bathroom stall size?
A standard adult stall is 36 inches wide by 60 inches deep, with panels 58 inches high mounted 12 inches above the floor. A narrower 30-inch-wide stall is common where space is tight, such as elementary schools. See our [standard sizes](/guides/standard-sizes) and [stall dimensions](/guides/bathroom-stall-dimensions) guides.

### What size is a wheelchair-accessible stall?
Under the 2010 ADA Standards, the compartment is at least 60 inches wide. Its minimum depth is 56 inches with a wall-hung toilet and 59 inches with a floor-mounted toilet (604.8.1.1). The door must give at least 32 inches of clear opening (404.2.3). Read our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance) for the full picture.

*Disclaimer: ADA dimensions and accessibility guidance shown here are provided for general informational purposes only. Codes and their interpretation vary by jurisdiction and project. We accept no responsibility or liability for compliance — always verify requirements with your architect, engineer, or local code official before ordering.*

### Why do bathroom stalls have gaps?
A traditional partition is a modular kit assembled between walls that are never perfectly square. The door needs room to swing, the floor gap lets staff clean and spot an occupied stall, and the system is inexpensive to make and ship. The standard floor gap is 12 inches. Manufacturers also sell no-sightline doors and taller panels that close most gaps. See [why bathroom stalls have gaps](/blog/why-do-bathroom-stalls-have-gaps).

### Can I use a lower floor gap in an accessible stall?
Be careful. Wheelchair-accessible compartments need 9 inches of toe clearance under the front partition and at least one side partition (12 inches in children's compartments), unless the compartment is large enough to qualify for an exception (604.8.1.4). Confirm the details with your architect or code official.

## Materials and mounting

### What materials are commercial bathroom partitions made of?
Five: powder coated steel, solid plastic (HDPE), phenolic, plastic laminate and stainless steel. Steel types wrap a honeycomb core, plastic laminate is bonded to particleboard, phenolic is fused layers of resin-soaked paper, and HDPE is one solid sheet of plastic. Compare them on the [partitions page](/partitions).

### What is the best material for a wet restroom?
Phenolic or solid plastic (HDPE). Manufacturers position both for showers, pools and areas that get hosed down. Plastic laminate's particleboard core can swell with too much moisture, and painted steel can rust. See [what bathroom partitions are made of](/blog/what-are-bathroom-partitions-made-of).

### Are HDPE partitions fire rated?
Not by default. Model codes based on the International Building Code require HDPE partitions to pass the NFPA 286 room-corner test, and untreated HDPE does not. Fire-rated grades exist. If your building needs one, say so in your quote request and ask for the test documentation. Confirm the requirement with your code official.

### What are the mounting styles?
Overhead braced (floor anchors plus a headrail), floor mounted (floor anchors only), ceiling hung (supported from steel above the ceiling) and floor to ceiling (anchored at both ends). Overhead braced is the general-purpose choice because it needs no ceiling structure and works on floors that accept standard anchors. Compare all four on [mounting styles](/mounting-styles).

## Shipping and installation

### Do you ship commercial bathroom partitions nationwide?
Yes. We ship to all 50 states and Washington, D.C. by freight carrier. Shipments are crated, palletized and tracked. Curbside and liftgate delivery are available on request. See our [service area](/service-area).

### Do you install the partitions?
No. Next Partitions supplies partitions and hardware. Follow the manufacturer's instructions and the approved drawings, and use a qualified installer.

### What is included with the partitions?
Panels, doors, pilasters, hinges, slide latches, keepers, strikes, brackets, pilaster shoes and mounting fasteners, plus headrails where the mounting style uses them. Confirm the exact list on your quote.

### How thick are bathroom stall partitions?
Typically 1 inch for steel, laminate and HDPE panels, and 1/2 inch for phenolic panels. Pilasters are usually 1-1/4 inches on powder coated steel, 1 inch on HDPE and 3/4 inch on phenolic. Measure yours, because older and custom systems vary. See [replacement parts](/hardware/parts).

### What should I measure before asking for a quote?
Room width along the stall wall, room depth, ceiling height, the entry door location and swing, and the toilet centerlines. Photos of the floor, walls and ceiling help. See [Contact](/contact) for the full list.

### Can I replace one door instead of the whole stall?
Yes, if the pilaster and hinges it hangs on are sound. Send the door dimensions, material, brand if known, hinge type and photos. See [replacement stall doors](/hardware/doors).

## Accessible stalls

### Can I get ADA-compliant stalls?
Yes. The configurator has an ADA option that adjusts stall dimensions, door width and grab bar requirements. Compliance depends on your whole restroom, so confirm it with your architect or code official.
