## The right material for New York City's climate

New York City gets rain on roughly 121 days a year, and relative humidity averages 70% in the morning and 54% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a New York City restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and New York City's building rules both set the sizes. A room with six or more toilets and urinals in total also needs a second accessible stall, 36 inches wide with grab bars on both sides. The city's access rules apply to alterations of older buildings too, minor ones included, but not to ordinary repairs.
- **Fire safety:** New York City has its own building rules. They treat toilet partitions as an interior wall finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene partitions to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** New York City has its own plumbing rules, separate from the state's. They set the number of toilets by how many people use the building, and every toilet used by staff or the public needs a stall with a door. The table further down shows what that means.
- **New York City extras:** the city adds a vertical grab bar, 18 inches or longer, on the side wall beside each accessible toilet. The wheelchair turning space in the room can't be inside a stall.
- **New rules on July 17, 2027:** New York City's first building rules written for work in existing buildings take effect, and the 1968 rules that owners of older buildings may still choose today are repealed. For a project filed after that date, confirm which rules apply with the NYC Department of Buildings.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** in New York City every one-person restroom must be open to people of any sex, with a sign near the door that says so. That covers existing rooms, and the sign is the only change required. A one-person restroom is a lockable room with no more than one toilet, one urinal and one sink. Where a building needs more toilets than that, the city's plumbing rules call for separate men's and women's rooms unless the space serves 30 people or fewer. They have no rule for a shared all-gender room with several stalls, so ask the NYC Department of Buildings before you plan one.
- **Permits:** New York City requires a permit for most construction and alteration work. Minor alterations and ordinary repairs need none, but work that affects exits, fire safety or the building's accessibility requirements doesn't count as minor. The city's examples of work without a permit are painting, plastering, new cabinets, replacing a plumbing fixture and resurfacing floors. Toilet partitions aren't named in any of its lists, so ask the NYC Department of Buildings (212-566-5000) or your architect or engineer whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the NYC Department of Buildings or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a New York City restroom needs

New York City counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. In an office building the count is made floor by floor. Every toilet needs its own stall with a door. Urinals are optional: in each men's room they can replace up to half of the required toilets. Each urinal needs a privacy screen that starts no more than 12 inches above the floor and reaches at least 5 feet.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 3/3 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 4/4 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 7/7 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums. The office figures are for one floor that holds that many people; the restaurant and school figures are for the whole building. A space for 30 people or fewer doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. A restaurant's count includes seasonal outdoor seating, and a restaurant with fewer than 20 seats and under 10,000 square feet doesn't have to offer its toilets to the public. Neither does an office or store under 10,000 square feet, though staff toilets are still required. Bars and nightclubs need more women's toilets than restaurants: one for every 40 women. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, New York City's building rules count an office at one person per 100 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room with tables and chairs at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in New York City

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the New York–Newark–Jersey City area earn a median of $35.72 an hour, about 23% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on public work, such as school, city and state projects, New York law requires at least the prevailing wage for the trade, with no minimum contract size. In New York City the City Comptroller sets the rates each July. For a carpenter on commercial building work that is $60.05 an hour plus $50.83 in benefits, through June 30, 2027.
- **Sales tax:** 8.875% in New York City: 4% state, 4.5% city and 0.375% for the regional transit district.
- **Tax on installation:** in New York, installation that counts as a permanent improvement to the building is not taxed, and the owner gives the contractor a capital improvement certificate (Form ST-124). Repair, maintenance and other installation labor is taxed, even when billed separately. Work done for a tenant is treated as temporary, and so taxed, unless the lease shows it stays with the building. The state's list of examples doesn't name toilet partitions.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to New York City are taxable, freight included when the seller bills it. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state. Materials are taxed even when they go into a permanent improvement.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance.
