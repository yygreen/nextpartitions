## The right material for Savannah's climate

Savannah gets about 48 inches of rain a year, on roughly 110 days, and relative humidity averages 86% in the morning and 54% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is June (about 6.7 inches) and the driest is November (about 2.4 inches). What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Savannah restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Georgia's own access rules adopt the federal standards, and the State Fire Marshal or the local building department enforces them. A stall that is replaced, not just repaired, must meet the current rules.
- **Fire safety:** Georgia's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version. In new buildings the State Fire Marshal's rules govern wall finishes, so confirm with your local fire marshal.
- **How many stalls:** Georgia's plumbing rules set the number of toilets by how many people use the building, and Savannah enforces them without changing the counts. The table below shows what that means.
- **Georgia and federal stall rules match:** Georgia uses the federal standards for stalls, doors and grab bars as written, so a stall that meets one meets the other.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. Georgia's rules call for separate men's and women's rooms for the toilets a building must have, and in 2026 the state removed the option that lets a shared all-gender room take their place. Savannah's city code still carries its own version of that option, written for an earlier edition of the plumbing rules: a shared room where every toilet sits in a compartment with floor-to-ceiling walls and a lockable door, urinals are in stalls or screened from view, and the sink area is not closed off by a door. Ask Savannah Development Services which rule applies before planning one. State law requires public schools, and private schools that compete against them, to provide separate shared restrooms for each sex.
- **Permits:** Savannah requires a permit to build, alter or repair a building, and lets ordinary minor repairs go ahead without one. The city code doesn't say which work counts as a minor repair and doesn't name toilet partitions, so ask Savannah Development Services whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Savannah Development Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Savannah restroom needs

Georgia's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in the men's room they may take the place of up to half the toilets in an office, and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a screen on both sides.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are state minimums for the whole building, and Savannah's city code does not change them. An office that holds 25 people or fewer, or any space that holds 15 or fewer, may use one restroom for everyone. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or Savannah Development Services sets the final number.

- **Public jobs:** Georgia has no state wage law for public construction, so a set wage applies only on federally funded projects, under federal law. For a carpenter on building work in Chatham County that rate is $19.13 an hour plus $0.30 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 7% in Savannah: 4% state, 1% local option, 1% schools and 1% special purpose tax, all three collected across Chatham County.
