## The right material for Columbia's climate

Columbia gets about 45 inches of rain a year, on roughly 109 days, and relative humidity averages 84% in the morning and 50% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Columbia restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and South Carolina's own access standard set the sizes. There is no separate state registration: the local building department reviews the plans, and the state reviews plans for public schools and state buildings.
- **Fire safety:** South Carolina's building rules treat toilet partitions as an interior finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) used that way to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** South Carolina's building rules, which Columbia enforces without changes of its own, set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **South Carolina extras:** the state's access standard asks for more than the federal one in a few places. In the edition used until September 2026, the accessible stall needs a vertical grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal bars, and the gap under its partitions must be 12 inches high where the federal rule asks for 9. A room with six or more toilets and urinals also needs a narrower stall with grab bars on both sides, as under federal law. Columbia says a newer edition, published in September 2026, now applies to projects, so confirm the stall details with the City's commercial plan review (803-545-3427).
- **New rules on January 1, 2027:** Columbia moves to the next edition of South Carolina's building and plumbing rules on that date. A permit request submitted to the City after December 31, 2026 has to follow the new edition. The toilet counts in the table below, the rule that every toilet needs its own stall with a door and the fire test for solid plastic are the same in the new edition.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. South Carolina's building rules require separate men's and women's rooms, and the state removed the option of meeting that with a shared all-gender room with stalls. The rules don't say whether one may be added on top of the required rooms, so ask your building department. Since May 2026, state law requires shared restrooms in public schools and public colleges to be designated for one sex, and each school must offer a single-user restroom or an accommodation.
- **Permits:** Columbia requires a permit for all regulated work. The City says painting, flooring and other cosmetic work doesn't need a building permit and points to the building rules' list of exempt work. That list covers painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, countertops and similar finish work, and movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches. It doesn't name toilet partitions fixed to the floor or wall, so ask the City of Columbia Development Center (803-545-3483) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the City of Columbia Development Center or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Columbia restroom needs

South Carolina counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs its own stall with a door. Urinals are optional: they can take the place of up to half of the men's toilets in an office, and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, so the table shows the men's side as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. An office that holds 25 people or fewer, and any building or tenant space that holds 15 or fewer, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. A restaurant counts its seasonal outdoor seating in its capacity.

To find your building's capacity, South Carolina's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Columbia

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Columbia area earn a median of $23.11 an hour, about 21% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** South Carolina has no state wage law for public construction, so a set wage applies only on federally funded jobs, under the federal Davis-Bacon Act. For a carpenter on building work in Richland County that is $16.32 an hour plus $3.83 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 8% in Columbia: 6% state, 1% Richland County local option tax and 1% Richland County transportation tax. The part of Columbia in Lexington County is taxed at 7%.
- **Tax on installation:** in South Carolina, a seller who also installs doesn't charge tax on installation labor when it is shown separately on the bill and is reasonable. A contractor who supplies and installs under a construction contract pays the tax when buying the partitions and doesn't add sales tax to the contract.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Columbia are taxable. Freight is taxed when the seller delivers to your site and isn't taxed when the goods ship by common carrier with the sale made at the shipping point. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the South Carolina Department of Revenue.
