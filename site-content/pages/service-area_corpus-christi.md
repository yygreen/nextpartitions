## The right material for Corpus Christi's climate

Corpus Christi gets about 32 inches of rain a year, on roughly 76 days, and relative humidity averages 89% in the morning and 61% in the afternoon. In most Corpus Christi restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Corpus Christi restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Texas's own access rules set the sizes. On projects of $50,000 or more, the state reviews the plans; smaller projects must still follow the rules.
- **Fire safety:** Corpus Christi's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Corpus Christi sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Texas and federal stall rules match:** Texas's own access rules for stalls, doors and grab bars read the same as the federal ones, so a stall that meets one meets the other.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. In a private building, a shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. Since December 2025, Texas law requires shared restrooms in city, county, school district and public college buildings to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** Corpus Christi requires a permit to alter or repair a building unless the work is exempt. The exempt list covers "similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high, without naming toilet partitions, so ask Corpus Christi Development Services whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Corpus Christi Development Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Corpus Christi restroom needs

Corpus Christi counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional. They may stand in for up to half of the men's toilets in an office, and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. An office that holds 25 people or fewer, a store that holds 100 or fewer, or any other space that holds 15 or fewer, staff and customers together, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or Corpus Christi Development Services sets the final number.
