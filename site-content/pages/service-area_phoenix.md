## The right material for Phoenix's climate

Phoenix gets about 7 inches of rain a year, on roughly 35 days, and relative humidity averages 46% in the morning and 23% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is August (about 0.9 inches) and the driest is June (almost none). The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Phoenix restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Arizona's disability law uses the federal sizes, and state law writes them into every local building code, so the city or county building department checks them.
- **Fire safety:** Phoenix's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Phoenix sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building, and caps how many of the men's toilets may be urinals. The table below shows what that means.
- **Arizona and federal stall rules match:** Arizona adopts the federal standards for stalls, doors and grab bars, and state law says its rules may not go beyond them. A city may set stricter rules of its own.
- **Phoenix access rules:** Phoenix names two accessibility standards for buildings: the federal one and a national standard, in its 2017 edition, that the city's building rules apply.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** under Phoenix's plumbing rules a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room may take the place of separate men's and women's rooms if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and every urinal has its own partitioned area. A measure Arizona voters approved in November 2026 says that from January 1, 2027, a school or athletic association may not authorize anyone to use a restroom, locker room or shower room that is part of its athletics facilities and is not designated for that person's sex.
- **Permits:** Phoenix requires a permit to alter a building unless the work is on its exempt list. The list covers painting, carpeting and similar finish work, and "fixtures, cases, racks, counters and partitions not over 5 feet 9 inches" high, but it doesn't say whether that includes toilet partitions. Ask Phoenix Planning and Development whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Phoenix Planning and Development or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Phoenix restroom needs

Arizona has no statewide building rules, so Phoenix's own rules decide. Phoenix counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in Phoenix they may take the place of up to half of the men's toilets in any kind of building, so the table shows men's toilets as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. An office or shop designed for 50 people or fewer, and any building designed for 15 or fewer, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. Confirm the count with Phoenix Planning and Development.

To find your building's capacity, Phoenix's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or Phoenix Planning and Development sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Phoenix

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Phoenix–Mesa–Chandler area earn a median of $29.00 an hour, about the same as the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Arizona has no wage law for public jobs, and state law bars cities, counties and school districts from requiring prevailing wages in their contracts. On federally funded work the federal rate applies: $33.19 an hour plus $19.20 in benefits for a carpenter on building work in Maricopa County.
- **Sales tax:** 9.1% in Phoenix: 5.6% state, 0.7% Maricopa County and 2.8% city.
- **Tax on installation:** in Arizona, a contractor's labor to repair, replace or alter part of an existing building is not taxed. The contractor pays tax on the materials and adds no tax line to the invoice. New construction, and alterations to a commercial building under a contract above $750,000, are taxed as contracting on 65% of the contract price.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Phoenix are taxable. A delivery charge listed separately on the invoice is not. An out-of-state seller with more than $100,000 a year in Arizona sales collects the tax. If the seller doesn't collect it, the buyer owes use tax to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Arizona Department of Revenue.
