## The right material for Springfield's climate

Springfield gets about 45 inches of rain a year, on roughly 109 days, and relative humidity averages 83% in the morning and 58% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is May (about 5.6 inches) and the driest is February (about 2.4 inches). What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Springfield restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes, and the city's building code adds a national accessibility standard on top. Missouri has no state review of plans for access: the city checks it with the building permit.
- **Fire safety:** Springfield's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Springfield sets the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Missouri extras:** the national standard that Missouri cities apply through their building codes asks for an 18 inch vertical grab bar on the side wall next to the toilet, which the federal rules do not. On buildings paid for with state, city, county or school district money, Missouri law requires the latest edition of that standard and the wheelchair symbol at the main entrance.
- **New rules on January 1, 2027:** Springfield switches to newer editions of its building and plumbing rules that day. The toilet counts in the table and the fire test for solid plastic stay the same. The rules on restroom signs and shared restrooms change.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** Missouri has no state law on single-user or all-gender restrooms, and none on who may use which restroom. Under the building rules Springfield applies until December 31, 2026, a one-person or family restroom is marked for use by either sex, and larger buildings need separate men's and women's rooms. From January 1, 2027 a one-person restroom is signed as open to everyone, and a shared restroom for all users is allowed where every toilet has its own stall with a door and every urinal has a screen.
- **Permits:** Springfield requires a building permit to construct, enlarge or alter a building and says some remodeling and repairs are exempt. The exempt list in the rules that start on January 1, 2027 includes painting, tiling, cabinets, counter tops and similar finish work, and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high. It doesn't name toilet partitions. Ask Building Development Services at 417-864-1585 whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Springfield's Building Development Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Springfield restroom needs

Springfield counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and they are the same under the rules that start on January 1, 2027. Separate men's and women's rooms aren't required in a space for 15 people or fewer, in an office for 25 or fewer or in a store for 100 or fewer. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, Springfield's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or Building Development Services sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Springfield

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Springfield area earn a median of $25.00 an hour, about 14% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on state, city, county and school district projects with a total cost above $75,000, Missouri law requires at least the wage the state sets each year for each trade in each county. For a carpenter on building work in Greene County that is $52.16 an hour, benefits included.
- **Sales tax:** 8.1% at most Springfield addresses: 4.225% state, 1.75% Greene County and 2.125% city. Addresses inside the downtown improvement district pay 8.6%, and other shopping and transportation districts add their own tax. Partitions shipped in from a seller outside Missouri fall under the use tax instead: 6.35% at most addresses and 6.85% in the downtown district.
- **Tax on installation:** in Missouri, installation labor is not taxed. A contractor who supplies and installs partitions pays the tax when buying them and does not add tax to the contract.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Springfield are taxable. Usual delivery charges listed separately on the invoice are not taxed. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Missouri Department of Revenue.
