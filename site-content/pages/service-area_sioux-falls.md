## The right material for the Sioux Falls climate

Sioux Falls gets about 28 inches of rain a year, on roughly 97 days, and relative humidity averages 82% in the morning and 60% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Sioux Falls restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use, under federal law. South Dakota has no access rules of its own for private buildings and no separate state review: access is checked with the building plans by the city or county. Buildings paid for with state or local public money must meet federal access guidelines under state law.
- **Fire safety:** Sioux Falls's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Sioux Falls sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Sioux Falls extras:** the city applies a national accessibility standard that goes further than the federal rules in two details: an accessible stall needs an 18 inch vertical grab bar beside the toilet as well as the horizontal one, and the partitions of a wheelchair stall need 12 inches of toe room above the floor instead of 9. The plumbing rules also name a security standard for toilet partitions and urinal screens, so ask your plan reviewer whether it applies to your project.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. In a private building, a shared all-gender room may take the place of separate men's and women's rooms if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and every urinal has its own partitioned area. In public schools and in buildings owned, leased or occupied by the state, a county or a city, South Dakota law requires restrooms for more than one person to be designated for females only or males only.
- **Permits:** Sioux Falls requires a permit to alter a building unless the work is on its exempt list, and the building official may exempt minor work. The list covers "painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, countertops, and similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high, but it doesn't name toilet partitions. Ask Sioux Falls Building Services at 605-367-8670 whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Sioux Falls Building Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Sioux Falls restroom needs

Sioux Falls counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. The city's plumbing ordinance takes the numbers from the table in its building rules. Urinals are optional: they may take the place of up to 67 percent of the toilets a men's room needs, each with a screen on both sides, so the table shows men's toilets as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. An office or warehouse designed for 25 people or fewer, a shop designed for 100 or fewer and any building designed for 15 or fewer doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. South Dakota's statewide plumbing rules use a different table, with more women's toilets and required urinals, and state law says a city's plumbing rules must meet the state's minimum. Ask Sioux Falls Building Services which numbers your plans are reviewed against.

To find your building's capacity, Sioux Falls's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 25. Your architect or Sioux Falls Building Services sets the final number.

- **Public jobs:** South Dakota has no state wage law for public jobs, so a set wage applies only on work paid for with federal money, where federal law sets it by county. For a carpenter on building work in Minnehaha County that is $17.61 an hour plus $8.53 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 6.2% in Sioux Falls: 4.2% state and 2% city.
- **Tax on installation:** South Dakota taxes building work with a separate 2 percent tax on the contractor's whole bill, labor and materials together, in place of sales tax on the labor. The contractor pays sales tax on the materials when buying them and may show both taxes as lines on the invoice. A seller with more than $100,000 a year in South Dakota sales must collect it. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.
