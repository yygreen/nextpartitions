## The right material for the Indianapolis climate

Indianapolis gets about 44 inches of rain a year, on roughly 127 days, and relative humidity averages 82% in the morning and 60% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is June (about 5.0 inches) and the driest is February (about 2.4 inches). What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules an Indianapolis restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use, and a room with six or more toilets and urinals also needs a narrower stall with grab bars on both sides. Federal law and Indiana's building rules set the sizes. Indiana has no separate access sign-off: for buildings used by the public or employees the state reviews construction plans as a whole, and it exempts some small remodeling work, so ask the state's Building Plan Review section whether yours needs filing.
- **Fire safety:** Indiana's building rules treat toilet partitions as an interior finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene partitions to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Indiana's building rules set the number of toilets by how many people use the building, and Indianapolis applies them unchanged. The table below shows what that means.
- **Indiana extras:** Indiana goes further than the federal rules in two places. The accessible stalls need an 18 inch vertical grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal one. And every toilet stall, not only the accessible one, must be at least 30 inches wide and 60 inches deep.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** Indiana's rules call for separate men's and women's rooms, each with a sign, once a space holds more than 15 people. A one-toilet room can serve everyone, and two such rooms can stand in for the men's and women's rooms where each needs only one toilet. The rules make no provision for a shared all-gender room with several stalls, so ask your building department before planning one. No Indiana law was found that restricts who may use which restroom.
- **Permits:** Indianapolis requires a permit for construction work in a commercial building unless the work is on the city's exempt list. The list covers ordinary maintenance and repair, painting and similar finish work, and movable cases, counters and partitions not over 69 inches high. It doesn't name toilet partitions, so ask the Indianapolis Department of Business and Neighborhood Services whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Indianapolis Department of Business and Neighborhood Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls an Indianapolis restroom needs

Indiana's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in each men's room they may take the place of up to half of the required toilets in an office, and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and the men's figures show toilets only, before any are swapped for urinals. A space that holds 15 people or fewer can use one shared restroom, and Indiana lets the count rest on the number of people the space is actually designed for where that is lower than the floor area figure. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 100 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or the Indianapolis Department of Business and Neighborhood Services sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Indianapolis

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Indianapolis–Carmel–Greenwood area earn a median of $31.28 an hour, about 7% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Indiana repealed its wage law for public construction in 2015 and state law now stops public agencies from setting their own wage scales, so a required wage applies only on federally funded projects. The federal wage list for building work in Marion County has no rate for carpenters. A rate for a trade that isn't listed is added after the contract is awarded, so ask the agency paying for the project.
- **Sales tax:** 7% in Indianapolis. Indiana's sales tax is one rate for the whole state.
- **Tax on installation:** in Indiana, an installation charge listed separately on the invoice is not taxed when the work is done after delivery. A contractor who quotes one lump sum to supply and install work that becomes part of the building doesn't charge sales tax on the job, and pays the tax on the materials instead. Where the contractor lists materials and labor separately, the materials are taxed and the labor is not.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Indianapolis are taxable, and so is freight charged by the seller. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Indiana Department of Revenue.
