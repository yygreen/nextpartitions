## The right material for Hartford's climate

Hartford gets about 47 inches of rain a year, on roughly 128 days, and relative humidity averages 76% in the morning and 53% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Hartford restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use, and a room with six or more toilets and urinals also needs a narrower stall with grab bars on both sides. Federal law and Connecticut's building rules set the sizes. Connecticut has no separate state sign-off for access: your local building official checks it with the permit, and only the State Building Inspector can approve an exception to an access rule.
- **Fire safety:** Connecticut's building rules treat toilet partitions as an interior finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene partitions to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Connecticut's building rules, which Hartford enforces unchanged, set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Connecticut extras:** Connecticut goes further than the federal rules in four places. The accessible stall needs an 18 inch vertical grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal one. Its partitions need 12 inches of toe space above the floor, where the federal rule asks for 9. An accessible stall door that swings out needs an extra pull handle on the inside, 6 inches from the hinge side, unless the door closes and latches by itself. And in a very large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, which means a second one from 21 stalls up.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone, and where several sit side by side, one needs a baby changing station. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. Connecticut's public accommodations law bars discrimination based on sex or gender identity or expression, and it allows separate bathrooms based on sex.
- **Permits:** Hartford follows Connecticut's statewide permit rules. They require a permit to alter or repair a building and list the work that needs none. The list covers painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, countertops and similar finish work that involves no new construction or alterations, and movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches. It doesn't name toilet partitions fixed to the floor or wall, so ask Hartford's Licenses and Inspections division (860-757-9200) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Hartford's Licenses and Inspections division or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Hartford restroom needs

Connecticut's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in each men's room they may take the place of up to half of the required toilets in an office, and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and the men's figures show toilets only, before any are swapped for urinals. An office with 25 people or fewer, or any space with 15 or fewer, can use one shared restroom. The same rules apply in every Connecticut town, because towns cannot change the state's building rules. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

- **Public jobs:** on state, city, town and school projects costing $100,000 or more for remodeling or repair, or $1 million or more for new construction, Connecticut law requires at least the wage the state Labor Commissioner sets for each trade in each town. For a carpenter on building work in Hartford that is $44.48 an hour plus $29.74 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 6.35% in Hartford. That is Connecticut's state rate.
- **Tax on installation:** in Connecticut, labor on an existing commercial building is taxed, even when it's billed separately from the partitions. Labor on new construction is not. A contractor who supplies and installs pays tax on the materials and charges tax on the taxable job.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Hartford are taxable at 6.35%, freight included, and Connecticut has no city or county sales tax on top. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Connecticut Department of Revenue Services.
