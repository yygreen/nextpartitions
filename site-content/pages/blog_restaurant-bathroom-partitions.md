# The Best Restaurant Bathroom Partitions: How to Choose

Restaurant bathroom partitions have two jobs that pull in different directions. Guests judge the kitchen by the restroom, so the stalls should look like they belong to the dining room. And the same stalls have to survive mop water, cleaning chemicals and a closing routine that runs every night. The best choice comes from one question first: how is the room cleaned?

## Start with how you clean

The five partition materials are built very differently, and water is what separates them:

![Five partition doors cut through and seen edge on: phenolic, solid plastic, plastic laminate, powder coated steel, stainless steel](/images/diagrams/material-sections-compare.svg)

- **If the floor is mopped and the room stays dry,** every material is open to you. Choose on looks and budget.
- **If the floor is hosed down, or the room stays damp,** choose [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic) or [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

The reason is in the cores. [Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and hosing it down can void its warranty. Painted steel can rust where the coating is damaged, and that usually starts low, where mop water sits.

## Then choose the look

## Mounting: overhead braced suits most restaurants

Most restaurant restrooms are small, and they sit in buildings of every kind: strip malls, old storefronts, hotels. Overhead braced mounting is the usual answer because it asks the least of the building. Pilasters are anchored to the floor and tied together by a headrail, and it works with any ceiling type.

![Overhead braced toilet partitions, front view](/images/diagrams/mounting-overhead-braced-front.svg)

If you want the floor clear for cleaning, [ceiling hung](/mounting-styles/ceiling-hung) partitions leave nothing standing on it, but they need structural steel above the finished ceiling at every pilaster, which most restaurant spaces don't have. [Floor to ceiling](/mounting-styles/floor-to-ceiling) gives the most rigid stalls and suits taller, more private doors. Our [mounting style comparison](/blog/ceiling-hung-vs-floor-mounted-vs-floor-to-ceiling) explains what each one needs.

## Fitting stalls into a small room

In a small restroom, the accessible stall and its door decide the layout:

![Plan of a wheelchair accessible toilet compartment at minimum size, with the minimum area, toilet centerline, grab bars, door clear width and door location](/images/diagrams/ada-wheelchair-stall.svg)

- **Standard stalls** are usually 36" wide.
- **A wheelchair accessible compartment** must be at least 60" wide, and at least 56" deep with a wall-hung toilet or 59" deep with a floor-mounted one.
- **Its door** is self-closing, needs at least 32" of clear opening and can't swing into the minimum compartment area. At the minimum size, that means an outswing door, so check the door doesn't open across the entry or the sink.
- **How many:** where a restroom has toilet compartments, at least one must be wheelchair accessible. Six or more compartments, or six or more toilets and urinals combined, also need an ambulatory accessible compartment.

A restroom with a single toilet in its own lockable room has no stalls to buy. Partitions come in once a room has two or more toilets, or a urinal that needs a screen:

![Plan of urinals showing the 15 inch side clearance and 30 inch center spacing](/images/diagrams/urinal-spacing.svg)

Urinal screens give privacy between urinals or between a urinal and the sink. Confirm the screen size and where it mounts before you set the layout. The method for a whole room is in our [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout), and the accessible stall in full is in the [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance).

## Privacy for an upscale room

Standard doors and panels are 55" or 58" high and sit about 12" to 14" above the floor, depending on the manufacturer. For a more private, upscale feel, look at:

- **Taller doors and panels,** mounted closer to the floor. Some lines offer doors up to 72" high.
- **No-sightline doors,** which overlap the pilaster so there's no gap at the door edges.
- **Full-height enclosures,** which run from floor to ceiling for no gaps at all.

Two limits: taller doors aren't offered in every mounting style, and the accessible stall must keep its 9" toe clearance under the front and one side partition unless it's larger than the exception sizes. The options are compared in [why do bathroom stalls have gaps](/blog/why-do-bathroom-stalls-have-gaps).

## Plan the change-over

A restaurant can't close its restrooms for long. Three things keep the downtime short:

1. **Measure before you ask.** A quote built on real measurements doesn't change when the shop drawings come back. See [how to measure for toilet partitions](/blog/how-to-measure-for-toilet-partitions).
2. **Approve the shop drawings carefully.** The partitions are made from them.
3. **Ask the installer how long the room will be closed,** and whether the price includes removing the old stalls. If you have two restrooms, do one at a time.

## FAQ

### What is the best material for restaurant bathroom partitions?
It depends on cleaning. In a dry, mopped room, plastic laminate or stainless steel gives the most choice of finish. If the floor is hosed down, choose phenolic or solid plastic (HDPE).

### Are plastic laminate partitions OK in a restaurant?
Yes, where the restroom stays dry. Laminate has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, so it isn't for rooms that are hosed down.

### Which mounting style is best for a restaurant?
Overhead braced. It works with any ceiling type and asks the least of the building.

### Does a small restaurant restroom need an ADA stall?
Where a restroom has toilet compartments, at least one must be wheelchair accessible: at least 60" wide, with a door that gives 32" of clear opening. Confirm your project with your architect or code official.

### Do I need partitions in a single-toilet restroom?
No. A single toilet in its own lockable room has no stalls. You may still want a urinal screen if the room has a urinal.

### How do I make restaurant bathroom stalls more private?
Taller doors mounted closer to the floor, no-sightline doors that overlap the pilaster, or full-height enclosures. The accessible stall still needs its toe clearance.
