## The right material for Boston's climate

Boston gets about 44 inches of rain a year, on roughly 127 days, and relative humidity averages 72% in the morning and 57% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Boston restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Massachusetts's own access rules both apply, and the local building inspector enforces the state rules. On a remodel costing $100,000 or more, the building must also have an accessible toilet room and public entrance. An owner who can't meet a rule can ask the state's Architectural Access Board for a variance.
- **Fire safety:** Massachusetts's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Massachusetts sets the number of toilets by how many people a building is set up for, and the same state table applies in Boston. The table below shows what that means.
- **Massachusetts extras:** the state's wheelchair stall is deeper than the federal one, at least 60 inches wide and 72 inches deep. It needs two 42-inch grab bars with a roughened, non-rusting finish, nothing mounted above them, a door that swings out and closes itself with a pull on both sides, and a coat hook. A room with six or more stalls also needs a second, 36-inch-wide stall with grab bars on both sides. In schools through sixth grade, toilet and grab bar heights are set by grade.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** Massachusetts lets men's and women's toilets be replaced by single-user rooms open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in a lockable compartment with partitions from floor to ceiling and a single-user room open to everyone is on the same floor within 300 feet. When an existing room is converted, partitions that lock may stay, but new ones must be full height. Where restrooms are separated by sex, state law lets each person use the one that matches their gender identity.
- **Permits:** in Boston, as everywhere in Massachusetts, a permit is needed to alter or repair a building, and the state's building rules list the work that needs none. The list covers painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, countertops and similar finish work, movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches, and ordinary repairs. It doesn't name toilet partitions fixed to the floor or wall. Boston issues a Short-Form Permit for minor alterations, repairs and replacements that don't change a building's structure or use, so ask the Inspectional Services Department (617-635-5300) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Boston's Inspectional Services Department or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Boston restroom needs

Massachusetts's rules count toilets by the people a building is set up for: seats plus staff in a restaurant, staff plus the usual number of visitors in an office, and student seats in a school. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet in a shared restroom needs its own stall. Urinals are optional: they may replace up to half of the men's toilets, and each needs a side screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 2/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 3/2 | 2/1 | 2/2
200: 5/4 | 4/2 | 4/4
400: 7/6 | 8/4 | 6/6
:::

School figures are for students; staff toilets are counted separately, one for every 20 women and one for every 25 men on staff. A business with no more than 20 staff and customers and no more than 2,000 square feet does not need separate men's and women's rooms. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

Massachusetts's toilet counts do not start from floor area: a restaurant counts its seats and the most staff on duty, an office counts the most staff on duty and its usual number of visitors, and a school counts student seats, so ask your plumbing inspector to confirm the number for your building.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Boston

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Boston–Cambridge–Newton area earn a median of $36.16 an hour, about 24% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on state, county, city, town and school district construction, Massachusetts law requires at least the wage rates the state Department of Labor Standards sets for that project, with no minimum project size in the law. The state issues the rates project by project. On a schedule it issued for a Boston school job in April 2026, a carpenter's rate from September 1, 2026 is $63.69 an hour plus $30.55 in benefits, rising to $65.19 an hour on March 1, 2027.
- **Sales tax:** 6.25% in Boston. That is the Massachusetts state rate, set by state law for sales of goods across the state.
- **Tax on installation:** in Massachusetts, a charge for labor to install the partitions is not taxed. A contractor who supplies and installs them as part of the building generally pays the tax on the materials and does not add sales tax to the contract, so ask your contractor how the quote treats it.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Boston are taxable at 6.25%. Freight shown as a separate charge for delivery after the sale is not taxed. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Massachusetts Department of Revenue.
