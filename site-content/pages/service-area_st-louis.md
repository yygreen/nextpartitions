## The right material for the St. Louis climate

St. Louis gets about 42 inches of rain a year, on roughly 111 days, and relative humidity averages 81% in the morning and 58% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is May (about 4.8 inches) and the driest is February (about 2.2 inches). In most St. Louis restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a St. Louis restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes, and the city's building code adds a national accessibility standard on top. Missouri has no state review of plans for access: the city checks it with the building permit.
- **Fire safety:** St. Louis's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** St. Louis sets the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Missouri extras:** the national standard that Missouri cities apply through their building codes asks for an 18 inch vertical grab bar on the side wall next to the toilet, which the federal rules do not. On buildings paid for with state, city, county or school district money, Missouri law requires the latest edition of that standard and the wheelchair symbol at the main entrance.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** Missouri has no state law on single-user or all-gender restrooms, and none on who may use which restroom. Under St. Louis's plumbing rules a one-person or family restroom carries a sign showing that either sex may use it. Larger buildings need separate men's and women's rooms, and the rules have no provision for a shared all-gender room with stalls. Where each sex needs only one toilet, two family restrooms may take the place of the separate rooms.
- **Permits:** St. Louis's list of work that needs no building permit includes movable cases, counters and partitions, cabinet installation and similar finish work, and non-fixed partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high. It doesn't name toilet partitions. Ask the Building Division's Permit Section at (314) 622-3313 whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the City of St. Louis Building Division or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a St. Louis restroom needs

Missouri has no statewide building code, so the City of St. Louis's own rules decide. The city counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and women get more toilets than men. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are required in men's rooms, each with a screen, and the women's room must have at least as many toilets as the men's room has toilets and urinals together. The table shows women's stalls / men's stalls + urinals.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 2/1+1 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
100: 3/1+1 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
200: 4/2+1 | 3/2+1 | 4/2+1
400: 8/3+2 | 4/3+1 | 7/4+2
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. A restaurant designed for 100 people or more also needs separate staff restrooms, which the table doesn't count. An office or shop designed for 50 people or fewer, or any building designed for 10 or fewer, may have one single-user restroom for everyone instead of separate rooms. A men's room may swap a toilet for each urinal added beyond the required number, as long as two thirds of its required toilets remain. Confirm the count for your building with the City of St. Louis Building Division.

To find your building's capacity, St. Louis's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor and a classroom at one per 20. The city's plumbing rules count a restaurant at one person per 15 square feet of dining and waiting area, outdoor seating included. Your architect or the Building Division sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in St. Louis

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the St. Louis area earn a median of $34.13 an hour, about 17% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on state, city, county and school district projects with a total cost above $75,000, Missouri law requires at least the wage the state sets each year for each trade in each county. The City of St. Louis has its own rate, separate from St. Louis County's. For a carpenter on building work in the city that is $69.16 an hour, benefits included.
- **Sales tax:** 9.679% at most addresses in the City of St. Louis: 4.225% state and 5.454% city. The city is not part of St. Louis County, so no county tax applies. Many small business and transportation districts, several of them downtown, add 1% or 2%. Partitions shipped in from a seller outside Missouri fall under the use tax instead, 9.013% at most city addresses.
- **Tax on installation:** in Missouri, installation labor is not taxed. A contractor who supplies and installs partitions pays the tax when buying them and does not add tax to the contract.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to St. Louis are taxable. Usual delivery charges listed separately on the invoice are not taxed. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Missouri Department of Revenue.
