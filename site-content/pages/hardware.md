# Bathroom Partition Hardware & Replacement Parts

Bathroom partition hardware is the set of metal parts that holds a toilet compartment together and makes the door work: hinges, latches, keepers, door pulls, coat hooks, brackets, pilaster shoes and headrails. When one piece fails, you can usually replace that piece instead of the whole stall. ## What commercial bathroom stall hardware is made of

Hardware material and finish vary by manufacturer and by partition material. Common options in manufacturers' current literature:

- **Type 304 stainless steel, satin finish.** Bobrick builds the hinges, latch, keepers, coat hooks, brackets and door handles on its partitions from 18-8, Type-304 stainless steel with a satin finish.
- **Chrome-plated zamac (die-cast zinc alloy).** Standard on many powder coated steel partitions. Metpar describes its standard hinges, latch and brackets as zamac die cast; ASI Global lists chrome-plated zamac wrap-around hinges, latches and stirrup brackets.
- **Extruded or cast aluminum.** Usual on solid plastic (HDPE) partitions. Scranton Products specifies 8-inch extruded aluminum wrap-around hinges with a bright dip anodized finish for Hiny Hiders.
- **Stainless upgrades.** Metpar and ASI Global both offer stainless steel hardware as an option on powder coated partitions.

Partition hardware is not universal. Hinge and latch hole patterns, pilaster thickness and fastener types differ between brands and materials. To match a part, we need to know:

1. **Brand**, if you know it (Bobrick, ASI Global, Scranton Products, Bradley, Metpar, Ammco or another maker).
2. **Partition material**: powder coated steel, stainless steel, solid plastic (HDPE), phenolic or plastic laminate.
3. **Door and pilaster thickness.** Measure with a tape at the door edge (see the table below).
4. **Door swing and hand**: inswing or outswing, and which side the hinges are on, viewed from outside the stall.
5. **Photos**: the whole stall, the hinge, the latch and keeper, and the bracket or shoe you need, with a tape measure in the shot.

## Hardware that ADA-accessible stalls need

Under the 2010 ADA Standards, an accessible toilet compartment door, including its hardware, must comply with section 404 (604.8.1.2 and 604.8.2.2). In practice that means:

- Handles, pulls, latches and locks must work with one hand, without tight grasping, pinching or twisting of the wrist, at 5 pounds of force maximum (309.4, 404.2.7).
- Those operable parts must be 34" minimum and 48" maximum above the finish floor (404.2.7).
- The door must be self-closing, with a door pull on both sides near the latch (604.8.1.2).
- Coat hooks must be within the reach ranges in section 308 (604.8.3).

## FAQ

### Is bathroom partition hardware universal?
No. Hole patterns, hinge styles and fasteners vary by brand and by partition material. Send the brand (if known), material, door thickness and photos so the part can be matched.

### What is restroom partition hardware usually made of?
Common materials are Type 304 stainless steel (satin finish), chrome-plated zamac (a die-cast zinc alloy) and anodized aluminum. The material usually follows the partition: zamac or stainless on steel and laminate partitions, aluminum or stainless on HDPE.

### What hardware does an ADA stall door need?
Under the 2010 ADA Standards, the door must be self-closing, have a door pull on both sides near the latch, and have hardware that works with one hand without tight grasping, pinching or twisting, mounted 34–48" above the floor (604.8.1.2, 404.2.7, 309.4).

### Does Next Partitions install hardware?
No. We supply partitions and parts. Installation is arranged separately with your own contractor.
