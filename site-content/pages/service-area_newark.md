## The right material for Newark's climate

Newark gets about 47 inches of rain a year, on roughly 122 days, and relative humidity averages 72% in the morning and 53% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Newark restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and New Jersey's own access rules set the sizes. In an existing building that has to be accessible, installing or moving partitions means creating a wheelchair-accessible stall where that can be done without moving the toilets. There is no separate state review: the local construction office checks access with the construction permit.
- **Fire safety:** New Jersey's building rules treat toilet partitions as an interior wall finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene partitions to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** New Jersey's plumbing rules set the number of toilets by how many people a restroom serves. They are the same in every New Jersey town, and Newark applies them unchanged. The table below shows what that means.
- **New Jersey extras:** stall sizes match the federal ones, but New Jersey adds a vertical grab bar, 18 inches or longer, on the side wall beside each accessible toilet. The open space under the wheelchair stall's front and side partitions must be 12 inches high and reach 8 inches past the partition, where federal rules ask for 9 and 6. The state's plumbing rules also name a partition standard that stalls and urinal screens must meet, with a higher privacy level for all-gender rooms. A restaurant or store that needs six or more toilets in total must also have a family restroom.
- **New editions in 2026:** New Jersey adopted new editions of its building and plumbing rules on August 17, 2026. For six months after a revision takes effect, a complete permit application can still be reviewed under the previous edition, so ask the Newark construction office which one applies to your project.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** New Jersey's statewide building rules set no sign requirement for one-person restrooms, so ask the Newark construction office what it expects. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in a privacy compartment and urinals are screened from the rest of the room or placed in compartments of their own. State civil rights law requires that people be allowed to use the restroom that matches their gender identity.
- **Permits:** New Jersey's statewide permit rules apply in Newark. Their list of ordinary maintenance that needs no permit covers attached furnishings, cabinets and bathroom accessories, and doesn't name toilet partitions. The rules also treat replacing a non-structural part "such as a partition" without changing the layout of the space as minor work, where you tell the construction office before work starts and file a permit application within five business days. They don't say which of the two a toilet partition is, so ask the Newark construction office (973-733-5632) about your replacement. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Newark construction office or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Newark restroom needs

New Jersey's rules count toilets by the number of people a restroom serves, a figure the building's designer sets. Where it is taken from the building's capacity (the most people it is designed to hold, worked out from its floor area), the rules allow two thirds of that capacity to be used. Half are counted as men and half as women, and part of a group counts as a whole one. Every toilet needs its own stall with a door. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they can replace up to half of the toilets, and each urinal needs a privacy screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 2/2
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 4/4
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 6/6
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, for the number of people served. The school column is for grades 1 to 6: grades 7 to 12 need 3 stalls for each sex at 200 students and 5 at 400, and kindergartens and day care need more. A space serving 15 people or fewer, or an office of 1,500 square feet or less, can use one single-user restroom for everyone. A restaurant that seats more than 100 customers needs separate toilets for staff. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find a building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room with tables and chairs at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20, and a restaurant is never counted below its number of seats.
