## The right material for Orlando's climate

Orlando gets about 51 inches of rain a year, on roughly 117 days, and relative humidity averages 88% in the morning and 55% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is June (about 8.1 inches) and the driest is November (about 1.8 inches). What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules an Orlando restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Florida's own access rules set the sizes.
- **Fire safety:** building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Florida's plumbing rules set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building, and Orlando applies them unchanged. The table below shows what that means.
- **New rules on December 31, 2026:** Florida replaces its building rules with a new edition that day. Orlando reviews permit applications submitted on or before December 30, 2026 under the current rules, and applications submitted from December 31 under the new ones.
- **Florida extras, in new buildings:** the wheelchair-accessible stall needs its own sink inside it, which makes the stall larger than in most states, and the toilet must sit in the corner diagonally across from the stall door.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** one-person and family restrooms must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. A 2023 Florida law sets separate rules for schools, colleges, jails and government buildings; private businesses aren't covered by it.
- **Permits:** Orlando says Florida law requires a permit for most projects, even small ones. The building rules' list of work that needs no permit covers minor gas, mechanical and plumbing repairs, and doesn't name toilet partitions. Ask Orlando Permitting Services whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Orlando Permitting Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls an Orlando restroom needs

Florida counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: they may stand in for up to half of the men's toilets in an office, and up to two-thirds in a restaurant or school.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3;More women's stalls may apply | 4/4
:::

In restaurants and other gathering places, Florida can require three women's toilets for every two men's toilets and urinals once the men's room has more than two. Licensed restaurants may be exempt, so confirm with Orlando Permitting Services. Outdoor seating counts toward the number of people. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or Orlando Permitting Services sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Orlando

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Orlando–Kissimmee–Sanford area earn a median of $23.92 an hour, about 18% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Florida has no state wage law for public construction and doesn't let cities or school districts set one. On federally funded work the federal rate applies: $28.65 an hour plus $13.80 in benefits for a carpenter on building work in Orange County.
- **Sales tax:** 6.5% in Orlando: 6% state and 0.5% Orange County. The county's 0.5% applies only to the first $5,000 of each item.
- **Tax on installation:** on a supply-and-install contract for work that becomes part of the building, the contractor pays the tax on the materials and charges you none on the contract.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Orlando are taxable. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Florida Department of Revenue.
