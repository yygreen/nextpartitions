Every bathroom partition is held up one of four ways: from the floor with a headrail across the top, from the floor alone, from steel above the ceiling, or from both the floor and the ceiling. These four bathroom partition mounting styles decide what your building has to provide, how the stalls clean, and how well they stand up to abuse. Pick the style first, then the [material](/partitions).

### Overhead braced
Pilasters (the posts at the front of each stall) are fastened to the floor, and an anti-grip aluminum headrail ties their tops to the walls. Manufacturers describe it as economical and sturdy, and it installs almost anywhere. [Overhead braced partitions →](/mounting-styles/overhead-braced)

### Floor mounted (floor anchored)
Pilasters are bolted into a structural concrete floor with no headrail, so the restroom looks more open. [Floor mounted partitions →](/mounting-styles/floor-mounted)

### Ceiling hung
Pilasters hang from structural steel above the ceiling and never touch the floor, which leaves the whole floor open for cleaning. [Ceiling hung partitions →](/mounting-styles/ceiling-hung)

### Floor to ceiling
Pilasters are anchored into the concrete floor and into structural support at the ceiling. Manufacturers describe it as very stable and recommend it for high-traffic areas such as schools. [Floor to ceiling partitions →](/mounting-styles/floor-to-ceiling)

## How to choose

- **No structure above the ceiling?** Overhead braced or floor mounted. Manufacturers recommend these two when adequate ceiling support can't be provided.
- **Floor can't take anchors?** Ceiling hung is the only style with nothing in the floor.
- **High ceiling or open look?** Floor mounted has no headrail.
- **Heavy use or abuse?** Floor to ceiling anchors every pilaster at both ends.

## FAQ

### What is the most common toilet partition mounting style?
Overhead braced, meaning floor-anchored pilasters tied together by a headrail, is the general-purpose style. Manufacturers describe it as economical and sturdy and say it installs almost anywhere. It needs no ceiling structure and works on floors that accept standard anchors, so it suits most restrooms.

### Are there wall mounted toilet partitions?
Not for full stalls. Every style attaches panels and pilasters to the walls with brackets, but stalls still need support from the floor, the ceiling or both. Wall-hung mounting is offered for urinal screens only, for example in some compact laminate lines.

### Which mounting style is easiest to clean, and which is strongest?
Ceiling hung is easiest to clean, because nothing touches the floor. Floor to ceiling is the most stable, with every pilaster anchored at both ends. Manufacturers describe it as very stable and durable.
