Floor mounted toilet partitions stand on their own. Each pilaster is bolted into the floor and there is no headrail, so nothing runs above the doors. Manufacturers also call the style floor anchored or floor braced. ASI says its "simplified construction permits ease of installation anywhere," with one firm condition: "For concrete floors only: 2" minimum penetration into floor required."

## How it's built

![Floor mounted toilet partitions, front view](/images/diagrams/mounting-floor-mounted.svg)

1. **Floor anchors** into structural concrete carry the whole load.
2. **No headrail:** the pilaster tops are free. Floor-anchored pilasters are 69" high.

Without a headrail, each pilaster has to stay rigid on its own anchors.

- **Anchoring device:** in Bobrick's lines, a steel leveling bar bolted to the bottom of the pilaster, with threaded rods, hex nuts, washers and an expansion shield set in the floor.
- **Anchor depth:** Bobrick's expansion shields need at least 2" of penetration into structural concrete at least 3" thick. The New York State OGS master spec likewise sets floor-anchored units at no less than 2" into the structural floor, compared with 1-3/4" for overhead-braced units.
- **Wider pilasters:** Bobrick recommends a pilaster (stile) at least 8" wide for floor-anchored compartments.
- **Shoe:** a stainless steel shoe covers the anchor and leveling hardware. Bobrick's is 4" high.
- **Shorter pilasters:** with no headrail to reach, Bobrick's floor-anchored stile is 69" high, compared with 83" for overhead braced. Doors are hung so their tops are level with the tops of the pilasters.

Panels and doors attach to the pilasters and to the walls with brackets, as in every style. The walls need backing behind those brackets. Bobrick notes that wall backing is required to secure the mounting brackets.

## Why specifiers choose floor mounted

- **Open look.** Hadrian says floor-mounted partitions "lend an air of spaciousness" and that the "absence of top structure makes them the ideal choice for high ceiling areas."
- **No ceiling work.** As with [overhead braced](/mounting-styles/overhead-braced), the ceiling carries nothing, and no structural steel is needed above it.
- **Nothing overhead.** With no headrail, there's no rail across the stalls to grab or hang from.
- **Fallback when the ceiling can't carry a load.** Bobrick recommends floor-anchored or overhead-braced compartments where adequate ceiling support can't be provided.

## Limits to plan for

- **Concrete floor required.** Floor-anchored units depend entirely on their anchors. A floor without structural concrete of adequate thickness isn't suitable. ASI states "concrete floors only."
- **The floor has to be sound where every pilaster lands.** Ask your contractor or structural engineer to confirm that anchors can go in at each pilaster location, especially over in-slab services.
- **Not usually offered in solid plastic.** The NY OGS spec notes that several manufacturers don't recommend floor-anchored mounting for solid-plastic units. Scranton's Hiny Hiders HDPE line lists only floor mounted overhead-braced, ceiling hung and floor to ceiling. For HDPE, choose [overhead braced](/mounting-styles/overhead-braced) or [floor to ceiling](/mounting-styles/floor-to-ceiling).
- **Floor cleaning.** Pilaster shoes stay on the floor, as with overhead braced. For a clear floor, see [ceiling hung](/mounting-styles/ceiling-hung).

## Where it works well

Floor mounted suits restrooms on a concrete slab where an open, uncluttered look matters: high-ceiling spaces, design-led [offices](/industries/offices) and [restaurants](/industries/restaurants), and smaller restrooms where a headrail would look out of place. Hadrian also points to its floor-mounted phenolic partitions for all-gender and unisex restrooms, using doors and pilasters that overlap to eliminate sightlines. That's a feature of the door and pilaster design, which some lines offer in other mounting styles too.

## Floor mounted vs overhead braced

The two are easy to confuse because both anchor into the floor.

| | Floor mounted | Overhead braced |
|---|---|---|
| Headrail | No | Yes, across the pilaster tops and into the walls |
| Pilaster height (Bobrick) | 69" | 83" |
| Floor anchoring (NY OGS) | 2" min. into structural floor | 1-3/4" min. into structural floor |
| Floor | Structural concrete | Most floors that accept anchors |
| Solid plastic | Generally not recommended | Offered |

## FAQ

### What is the difference between floor mounted and overhead braced partitions?
Both anchor pilasters into the floor. Overhead braced adds a continuous headrail that ties the pilaster tops to each other and to the walls. Floor mounted has no headrail, so each pilaster relies on deeper anchors in a structural concrete floor.

### What floor do floor anchored toilet partitions need?
Structural concrete. Bobrick's anchors need at least 2" of penetration into concrete at least 3" thick, and ASI states its floor-anchored style is "for concrete floors only."

### Are floor mounted partitions less stable without a headrail?
They're designed to stand without one, using wider pilasters (Bobrick recommends at least 8") and deeper anchors. The floor has to be structural concrete for that to work. For the most rigid setup, choose [floor to ceiling](/mounting-styles/floor-to-ceiling).

### Can I get floor mounted partitions in solid plastic (HDPE)?
Usually not. Several manufacturers don't recommend floor-anchored mounting for solid plastic. Choose overhead braced or floor to ceiling for HDPE.
