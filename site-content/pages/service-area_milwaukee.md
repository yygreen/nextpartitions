## The right material for Milwaukee's climate

Milwaukee gets about 35 inches of rain a year, on roughly 125 days, and relative humidity averages 79% in the morning and 63% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Milwaukee restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Wisconsin's building rules set the sizes. Plans for commercial buildings of 25,000 cubic feet or more, and for schools of any size, are reviewed by the state or by a city acting for it.
- **Fire safety:** Wisconsin's building rules count toilet partitions as interior finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene finishes to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Wisconsin's building rules set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. Milwaukee adopts them as the state writes them. The table below shows what that means.
- **Wisconsin extras:** Wisconsin's rules ask for more than the federal ones in three places. The front and one side partition of the accessible stall must clear the floor by 12 inches, not 9, unless the stall is built larger than the minimum. Accessible stalls need an 18-inch vertical grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal bars. A restaurant or store that needs six or more toilets must add a family or assisted-use toilet room.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. The number of toilets is still worked out half for men and half for women.
- **Permits:** Milwaukee requires a permit from the Department of Neighborhood Services to alter or repair a building or install equipment in it, unless the work is on the city's exempt list. The list covers "painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinet or countertop installation, and similar finish work" and repairs the department's commissioner judges minor, without naming toilet partitions. Ask the Department of Neighborhood Services whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the City of Milwaukee Department of Neighborhood Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Milwaukee restroom needs

Wisconsin counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional under Wisconsin's rules: where the men's room needs more than one toilet, urinals may replace up to half of them in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, each with a privacy screen. The table shows the men's side as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. A restaurant that needs six or more toilets in total, as at 400 people, must also have a family or assisted-use toilet room, which counts toward the total. Wisconsin's rules don't say how an odd number splits between stalls and urinals, so confirm that with plan review. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20, and Wisconsin may accept the real number of occupants instead where the owner can justify it.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Milwaukee

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Milwaukee–Waukesha area earn a median of $30.73 an hour, about 6% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Wisconsin repealed its own wage law for public jobs in 2017, so set wage rates apply only on federally funded work, under the federal Davis-Bacon Act. For a carpenter on building work in Milwaukee County that rate is $45.46 an hour plus $31.52 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 7.9% in Milwaukee: 5% state, 0.9% Milwaukee County and 2% city.
- **Tax on installation:** in Wisconsin, a contractor's charge to install partitions fixed to the building is not taxed, for labor or materials. A contractor who supplies the partitions pays the tax when buying them and doesn't add sales tax to your bill.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Milwaukee are taxable, and so is the seller's delivery charge. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state. Schools and government bodies can buy tax free with an exemption certificate.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Wisconsin Department of Revenue.
