## The right material for the Colorado Springs climate

Colorado Springs gets about 16 inches of rain a year, on roughly 89 days, and relative humidity averages 58% in the morning and 40% in the afternoon. The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Colorado Springs restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes, and Colorado's plumbing rules and the local building rules add their own access standard. Colorado has no separate state review step: the city or county building department checks access with the permit, and the state reviews public school projects.
- **Fire safety:** the building rules used in Colorado Springs require solid plastic (HDPE) used as an interior finish to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Colorado's plumbing rules, which Colorado Springs uses without changes, set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Colorado Springs extras:** in very large restrooms, the building rules used in Colorado Springs ask for 5% of the stalls to be wheelchair accessible, where federal law asks for at least one.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and the urinals are screened from the rest of the room. Colorado Springs adds no rule of its own. In buildings owned by the state, a state college, a county or a city, a new building or a renovated restroom must include an all-gender restroom; K-12 schools are outside that law. Colorado's civil rights rules allow separate men's and women's rooms and require that people may use the one that matches their gender identity.
- **Permits:** Colorado Springs requires a permit to alter or repair a building and lists the work that needs none. The list covers painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, countertops and similar finish work, and movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches. It doesn't name toilet partitions fixed to the floor or wall, so ask the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department (719-327-2880) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Colorado Springs restroom needs

Colorado's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in each men's room they may replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal then needs a screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are the minimums for the whole building. Colorado Springs uses the state's counts as written and adds none of its own. An office that holds 25 people or fewer, or any space that holds 15 or fewer, does not need separate men's and women's rooms. A shared all-gender room is counted on everyone in the building instead of half. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules used in Colorado Springs count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department sets the final number.

- **Public jobs:** Colorado's wage law for public jobs covers state agency projects of $500,000 or more without federal money, at rates the state takes from the federal wage tables for the county; it does not cover city, county or school district projects. - **Sales tax:** 8.2% in Colorado Springs: 3.07% city, 2.9% state, 1.23% El Paso County and 1% Pikes Peak Rural Transportation Authority. Colorado Springs collects its own 3.07% under its own tax rules.
- **Tax on installation:** installation labor listed separately on the bid, invoice or contract isn't taxed. Colorado Springs taxes an installation charge that is included in the price and not shown separately. A contractor owes the city's 3.07% on construction materials unless a tax-exempt organization buys them directly, and the state's exemption certificate for contractors covers only the taxes the state collects.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Colorado Springs are taxable. Colorado Springs counts freight and delivery charges as part of the taxed price; the state taxes freight unless it is an optional charge shown on its own line. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it as use tax.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant, the City of Colorado Springs sales tax office (719-385-5903) or the Colorado Department of Revenue.
