## The right material for Akron's climate

Akron gets about 42 inches of rain a year, on roughly 156 days, and relative humidity averages 81% in the morning and 60% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is June (about 4.4 inches) and the driest is February (about 2.4 inches). What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules an Akron restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use, and a room with six or more toilets and urinals also needs a narrower stall with grab bars on both sides. Federal law and Ohio's building rules set the sizes. Ohio has no separate state sign-off for access: your local building department checks it when it approves the plans.
- **Fire safety:** Ohio's building rules treat toilet partitions as an interior finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene partitions to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Ohio's statewide building rules set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building, and Summit County enforces them in Akron. The table below shows what that means.
- **Ohio extras:** in new buildings and additions, Ohio goes further than the federal rules in three places. The accessible stall needs an 18 inch vertical grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal one. Its partitions need 12 inches of toe space above the floor, where the federal rule asks for 9. And in a very large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, which means a second one from 21 stalls up.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed outside schools and colleges if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door, the compartments meet a set privacy rating, and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. Since February 2025, Ohio law requires shared student restrooms in schools and colleges to be designated for one sex; one-toilet family restrooms are still allowed there.
- **Permits:** building permits for Akron come from the Summit County Department of Building Standards, and the City of Akron reviews the same project separately before the county can issue one. The county requires a permit for alterations unless the work is on Ohio's exempt list. That list covers decorating, maintenance, minor repairs and finishes the building rules don't regulate, and doesn't name toilet partitions. Ask Summit County Building Standards at (330) 630-7280 whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Summit County Department of Building Standards or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls an Akron restroom needs

Ohio's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in each men's room they may take the place of up to half of the required toilets in an office, and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and the men's figures show toilets only, before any are swapped for urinals. An office with 25 people or fewer, or any space with 15 or fewer, can use one shared restroom, and the building official may set a different capacity where the real number of people is very different. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Akron

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Akron area earn a median of $29.36 an hour, about the same as the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on state, county and city projects over $250,000 for new construction or $75,000 for remodeling, Ohio law requires at least the wage the state sets for each trade in each county; school district projects are exempt from that law. The state publishes its Summit County rates on a portal that needs a login. On federally funded building work in Summit County, the federal rate for a carpenter is $36.29 an hour plus $23.79 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 6.75% in Akron: 5.75% state, 0.5% Summit County and 0.5% transit authority.
- **Tax on installation:** in Ohio, a contractor who supplies and installs items that become part of the building doesn't charge sales tax on the job, and pays the tax on the materials instead. Where the installed item stays the occupant's own property rather than part of the building, the installation is taxed along with the item. The state's published guidance doesn't say which applies to toilet partitions, or to an installer fitting partitions you bought yourself, so ask.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Akron are taxable, and so is freight charged by the seller. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Ohio Department of Taxation.
