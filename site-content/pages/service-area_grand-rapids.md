## The right material for the Grand Rapids climate

Grand Rapids gets about 39 inches of rain a year, on roughly 147 days, and relative humidity averages 82% in the morning and 62% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Grand Rapids restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Michigan's barrier free design rules set the sizes. The building department checks them with the permit, and only the state's Barrier Free Design Board can grant an exception.
- **Fire safety:** Michigan's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Michigan's plumbing rules set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. They apply across the state, Grand Rapids included. The table below shows what that means.
- **Michigan extras:** the stall sizes match the federal ones, but Michigan's rules add an 18-inch vertical grab bar on the side wall of accessible stalls, and ask for more toe space under the wheelchair stall's partitions: 12 inches high and 8 inches deep, against 9 and 6 under the federal rules.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. Michigan's rules provide for shared all-gender rooms: the toilet count is based on everyone in the building, every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door, and every urinal must be in a stall. Michigan's rule on separate men's and women's rooms doesn't list these rooms among its exceptions, so confirm the layout with your building department. No Michigan law in force sets who may use which restroom.
- **Permits:** Grand Rapids says a renovation of a commercial building needs a commercial building permit. Michigan's building rules, which the city enforces, list the work that needs no building permit. The list covers painting, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, countertops and similar finish work, and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high that aren't fixed in place. It doesn't name toilet partitions. Ask the Grand Rapids Development Center (616-456-4100) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Grand Rapids Development Center or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Grand Rapids restroom needs

Michigan counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional under Michigan's rules: in a men's room they may replace up to half of the toilets in an office, and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a screen on both sides.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. A space that holds 15 people or fewer in total doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms, and Michigan lets an owner base the count on the actual number of occupants with a signed affidavit.

To find your building's capacity, Michigan's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Grand Rapids

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Grand Rapids–Wyoming–Kentwood area earn a median of $28.36 an hour, about 3% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on schools and other public buildings paid for in whole or in part by the state, Michigan law requires at least the prevailing local wage and benefits, and contractors on those jobs must register with the state. The Kent County schedule dated November 2025 lists a journeyman carpenter at $53.30 an hour at straight time. The state issues new rates every November 1.
- **Sales tax:** 6% in Grand Rapids. Michigan has one statewide rate and no city or county sales tax.
- **Tax on installation:** in Michigan, an installation charge isn't taxed when it is listed separately on the invoice and the seller's records back it up. A contractor who supplies and installs partitions pays the 6% tax on the materials and can't add sales tax to your bill, though the tax may be built into the price.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Grand Rapids are taxed at 6%. Freight isn't taxed when it is listed separately on the invoice. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state as use tax.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Michigan Department of Treasury.
