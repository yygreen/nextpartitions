## The right material for Denver's climate

Denver gets about 15 inches of rain a year, on roughly 79 days, and relative humidity averages 50% in the morning and 43% in the afternoon. The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Denver restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes, and Colorado's plumbing rules and the local building rules add their own access standard. Colorado has no separate state review step: the city or county building department checks access with the permit, and the state reviews public school projects.
- **Fire safety:** Denver's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) used as an interior finish to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Denver's building rules set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Denver extras:** in very large restrooms, Denver's building rules ask for 5% of the stalls to be wheelchair accessible, where federal law asks for at least one. The city's rules also state that meeting them does not assure that a design meets federal access law.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. Denver allows a shared all-gender room and sets its own stall rule for it: every toilet sits in a compartment with a lockable door and no sightlines, running from the floor to the ceiling, or starting no more than 4.5 inches above the floor and reaching at least 8 feet. The gap under the door can be half an inch at most. A urinal is allowed in such a room only inside a toilet compartment, and it doesn't count toward the required number. In buildings owned by the state, a state college, a county or a city, a new building or a renovated restroom must include an all-gender restroom; K-12 schools are outside that law. Colorado's civil rights rules allow separate men's and women's rooms and require that people may use the one that matches their gender identity.
- **Permits:** Denver requires a permit to alter or remodel a building and lists the work that needs none. The list covers repairs, painting, tiling, floor coverings, countertops and cabinets, and movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches. It doesn't name toilet partitions fixed to the floor or wall, so ask Denver Community Planning and Development (720-865-2700) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Denver Community Planning and Development or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Denver restroom needs

Denver's building rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in each men's room they may replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal then needs a screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are Denver's minimums for the whole building. An office that holds 25 people or fewer, or any space that holds 15 or fewer, does not need separate men's and women's rooms. A shared all-gender room is counted on everyone in the building instead of half. A restaurant's seasonal outdoor seating counts toward the number of people. Public school projects are reviewed and permitted by the state, not the city, and the state's counts are the same. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, Denver's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of the whole floor or one per 100 square feet of the office space itself, whichever gives more people, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or Denver Community Planning and Development sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Denver

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Denver–Aurora–Centennial area earn a median of $31.21 an hour, about 7% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Colorado's wage law for public jobs covers state agency projects of $500,000 or more without federal money, at rates the state takes from the federal wage tables for the county. For a carpenter on building work in Denver County the federal rate is $21.09 an hour plus $6.31 in benefits. Denver has its own wage rule for city contracts of $2,000 or more: the city's schedule sets $23.09 an hour plus $6.31 in benefits for a carpenter.
- **Sales tax:** 9.15% in Denver: 5.15% city, 2.9% state, 1% transit district and 0.1% cultural district. Denver collects its own 5.15% under its own tax rules.
- **Tax on installation:** installation labor listed separately on the bid, invoice or contract isn't taxed. A contractor who supplies and installs building materials pays Denver's tax on those materials instead of charging it to the customer, and Denver gives no exemption for materials on government, religious or charity projects.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Denver are taxable. Denver taxes freight that the seller charges or arranges as part of the price; the state taxes it unless it is an optional charge shown on its own line. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it as use tax.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant, the Denver Treasury Division or the Colorado Department of Revenue.
