## The right material for Spokane's climate

Spokane gets about 16 inches of rain a year, on roughly 111 days, and relative humidity averages 74% in the morning and 52% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is December (about 2.3 inches) and the driest is July (about 0.4 inches). The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Spokane restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Washington's own access rules set the sizes.
- **Fire safety:** Washington's building rules, which Spokane enforces, require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Washington sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building, and Spokane applies the state count without changes. The table below shows what that means.
- **Washington extras:** stalls here need a second, upright grab bar on the side wall, and a wheelchair stall needs more toe room under its partitions (12 inches high, against 9 under the federal rules). In a large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, not just one. Large stores and venues also need a family restroom.
- **Rule update planned for May 3, 2027:** Washington's code council plans to bring in the next edition of the state building and plumbing rules on that date. Spokane's code adopts whichever edition the state has in force, so the update applies in Spokane the same day.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** under Washington's building rules, which Spokane enforces, a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet and urinal is in its own compartment with full-height walls and a door that locks from the inside.
- **Permits:** Spokane requires a building permit to alter or repair a building unless the work is on the exempt list in the state building rules. The list covers "similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high, without naming toilet partitions, so ask the Spokane Development Services Center (509-625-6300) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Spokane Development Services Center or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Spokane restroom needs

Spokane uses Washington's count, which goes by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Washington sets its own count for schools, with more toilets for girls than for boys. Urinals are optional and may stand in for most of the men's toilets.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 2/2
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 4/3
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 8/6
:::

Small businesses may be allowed one shared restroom: up to 25 people in an office and up to 30 in a restaurant. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. Confirm your project with the Spokane Development Services Center.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor and a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet. A school is counted at one student per 100 square feet of the whole building. Your architect or the Spokane Development Services Center sets the final number.

- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Spokane are taxable, freight included. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Washington Department of Revenue.
