## The right material for Long Island's climate

The nearest long-term weather station, at Islip, records about 46 inches of rain a year, on roughly 116 days, and relative humidity averages 76% in the morning and 58% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Long Island restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and New York's building rules both set the sizes. New York has no separate state review for access: your local building department checks it with the building permit.
- **Fire safety:** New York's building rules treat toilet partitions as an interior wall finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene partitions to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** New York's building rules set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The same state rules apply in every town, village and city in Nassau and Suffolk counties. The table below shows what that means.
- **New York extras:** stall sizes match the federal ones, but New York adds a vertical grab bar, 18 inches or longer, on the side wall beside each accessible toilet. The open space under the wheelchair stall's front and side partitions must be 12 inches high, where federal rules ask for 9. The wheelchair turning space in the room can't be inside a stall.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** New York law requires every one-person restroom in a public place, school or state building to be marked gender neutral, with the sign on or near the door. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own stall with a door and each urinal is screened; it is not signed by sex, and its toilets are counted from the building's whole capacity.
- **Permits:** on Long Island the building department of the town, village or city where the building stands issues permits. The Town of Hempstead, Town of Oyster Bay, Town of Brookhaven and Town of Islip each have their own, and so do the incorporated villages. State rules require a permit for work the building rules cover and let each local government exempt a short list of work. That list includes installing partitions or movable cases under 5 feet 9 inches high, finish work such as painting and tiling, and repairs that don't affect fire and life safety. It doesn't name toilet partitions, and each town or village chooses which exemptions it adopts, so ask the local building department whether your replacement needs a permit. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the building department of the town, village or city where the building stands or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Long Island restroom needs

New York's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs its own stall with a door. Urinals are optional: in each men's room they can replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a privacy screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. An office for 25 people or fewer, or any space for 15 or fewer, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. A restaurant's count includes seasonal outdoor seating. These figures apply outside New York City, which has its own building rules. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room with tables and chairs at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Long Island

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the New York–Newark–Jersey City area earn a median of $35.72 an hour, about 23% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on public work, such as school, town, village, county and state projects, New York law requires at least the prevailing wage for the trade, with no minimum contract size; the state Department of Labor publishes the rates by county each July. For a carpenter on building work in Suffolk County and most of Nassau County that is $55.06 an hour plus $35.43 in benefits. The southwest corner of Nassau County, south of the Southern State Parkway and west of Seaford Creek, has a higher rate of $60.05 an hour.
- **Sales tax:** 8.625% in Nassau County: 4% state, 4.25% county and 0.375% for the regional transit district. 8.75% in Suffolk County: 4% state, 4.375% county and 0.375% for the transit district. The rate is the one where the partitions are delivered.
- **Tax on installation:** in New York, installation that counts as a permanent improvement to the building is not taxed, and the owner gives the contractor a capital improvement certificate (Form ST-124). Repair, maintenance and other installation labor is taxed, even when billed separately. Work done for a tenant is treated as temporary, and so taxed, unless the lease shows it stays with the building. The state's list of examples doesn't name toilet partitions.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Long Island are taxable, freight included when the seller bills it. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state. Materials are taxed even when they go into a permanent improvement.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance.
