## The right material for Jacksonville's climate

Jacksonville gets about 53 inches of rain a year, on roughly 115 days, and relative humidity averages 88% in the morning and 56% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Jacksonville restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Florida's own access rules set the sizes.
- **Fire safety:** building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Florida's plumbing rules set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building, and Jacksonville applies them unchanged. The table below shows what that means.
- **New rules on January 1, 2027:** Florida replaces its building rules with a new edition, and Jacksonville applies it from that day. The city takes permit applications under the current rules until December 31, 2026, and under the new edition from December 1, 2026.
- **Florida extras, in new buildings:** the wheelchair-accessible stall needs its own sink inside it, which makes the stall larger than in most states, and the toilet must sit in the corner diagonally across from the stall door.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** one-person and family restrooms must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. A 2023 Florida law sets separate rules for schools, colleges, jails and government buildings; private businesses aren't covered by it. Jacksonville adds a rule for theaters, restaurants, churches, arenas, stores and similar buildings with restrooms open to the public. If the building is new or substantially renovated and needs six or more toilets, it must have a family restroom with an adult changing table, and swapping toilets for urinals doesn't remove that duty. In an existing building the owner may fit an adult changing table in an accessible stall in each restroom instead. A renovation counts as substantial when it covers more than half the building's floor area and changes the structure of a public restroom.
- **Permits:** Jacksonville's list of work that needs no permit covers patio slabs, replacing plumbing fixtures and small plumbing and equipment repairs, and doesn't name toilet partitions. The city says a permit is needed for construction that physically changes a property, alterations included. Ask the Jacksonville Building Inspection Division at 904-255-8500 whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Jacksonville Building Inspection Division or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Jacksonville restroom needs

Florida counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: they may stand in for up to half of the men's toilets in an office, and up to two-thirds in a restaurant or school.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3;More women's stalls may apply | 4/4
:::

In restaurants and other gathering places, Florida can require three women's toilets for every two men's toilets and urinals once the men's room has more than two. Licensed restaurants may be exempt, so confirm with the Jacksonville Building Inspection Division. Outdoor seating counts toward the number of people. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or the Jacksonville Building Inspection Division sets the final number.

- **Public jobs:** Florida has no state wage law for public construction and doesn't let cities or school districts set one. On federally funded work the federal rate applies: $23.63 an hour plus $13.30 in benefits for a carpenter on building work in Duval County.
