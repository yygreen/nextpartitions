## The right material for Boise's climate

Boise gets about 12 inches of rain a year, on roughly 90 days, and relative humidity averages 64% in the morning and 42% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is December (about 1.5 inches) and the driest is August (about 0.2 inches). The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Boise restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Idaho's building rules both set the sizes. Idaho has no separate state review for access: your local building department checks it with the rest of the plans.
- **Fire safety:** Idaho's building rules treat toilet partitions as an interior finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene finishes to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Boise sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building, using the table in Idaho's building rules. The table below shows what that means.
- **Idaho extras:** the accessible stall needs an 18-inch vertical grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal one, which federal rules don't ask for. The narrower stall with grab bars on both sides, required once a room has six or more toilets and urinals, must be 36 inches wide. A room with more than 20 stalls needs a second wheelchair stall. Restaurants, other assembly buildings and stores that need six or more toilets in total must also have a family restroom.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to either sex. Idaho's building rules require separate men's and women's rooms and have no option for a shared all-gender room with stalls, apart from the smallest buildings, which may have a single room. State law requires shared restrooms in public schools, state colleges and correctional facilities to be designated for one sex. Since July 2026 it is also a crime in Idaho to knowingly enter a restroom designated for the opposite sex in a government-owned building or a business open to the public, with listed exceptions. A federal court has put part of that law on hold while a challenge is heard.
- **Permits:** Boise's list of work that needs no permit covers painting, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, counter tops and similar finish work, and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high. It doesn't name toilet partitions, which are fixed in place. The city handles interior alterations to commercial spaces as tenant improvement permits. Ask the Boise Building Division whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Boise Building Division or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Boise restroom needs

Boise counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and every toilet used by staff or the public needs its own stall with a door. Idaho has two tables for the count, one in its building rules and one in its plumbing rules. Boise's Building Division has a written policy that the building rules decide the number, and the city's own building code says the same. That table gives one number of toilets for each sex and does not require urinals, so the table below shows the men's side as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

Urinals are optional in Boise. The city's building code lets urinals replace up to two thirds of the men's toilets in a restaurant or school restroom and up to half in other buildings, so confirm the split with Boise plan review. These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or the Boise Building Division sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Boise

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Boise City area earn a median of $24.45 an hour, about 16% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Idaho has no wage law for public construction and bars the state, cities and school districts from setting wage rates on their projects, so a required wage applies only on federally funded work. For a carpenter on building work in Ada County that federal rate is $22.49 an hour plus $3.61 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 6% in Boise. That is the state rate: Boise and Ada County add no sales tax of their own.
- **Tax on installation:** in Idaho, a contractor who supplies and installs partitions that become part of the building pays the 6% tax on the materials and does not add sales tax to your bill. Installation labor is not taxed.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Boise are taxable at 6%. Freight to your address is not taxed when it is listed separately on the invoice. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Idaho State Tax Commission.
