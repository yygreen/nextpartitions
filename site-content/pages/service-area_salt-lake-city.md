## The right material for Salt Lake City's climate

Salt Lake City gets about 16 inches of rain a year, on roughly 92 days, and relative humidity averages 63% in the morning and 43% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is April (about 2.2 inches) and the driest is July (about 0.5 inches). The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Salt Lake City restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes, and Utah's building rules add their own access standard. Utah has no separate state review step: the local building department checks access with the permit.
- **Fire safety:** Utah's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Utah's plumbing rules set the number of toilets and urinals by how many people use the building, and Salt Lake City applies them unchanged. The table below shows what that means.
- **Utah extras:** Utah's access standard asks for a vertical grab bar, at least 18 inches long, on the side wall next to the toilet, in addition to the horizontal bars federal law requires. A restaurant or store that needs six or more toilets in total must also have a single-user family restroom. In a room with more than 20 stalls, 5% of them must be wheelchair accessible, where federal law asks for at least one.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed for up to half of a building's required toilets, with the rest in separate men's and women's rooms. In a shared all-gender room, Utah requires stall partitions that run from the floor to the ceiling, and urinals must be in a separate area or in floor-to-ceiling stalls. In public schools, state law limits each men's or women's student restroom to students of that sex, and a new government building with public restrooms must include a single-occupant restroom with floor-to-ceiling walls and a locking door.
- **Permits:** Salt Lake City says most construction projects need a permit. Its list of exceptions covers painting, flooring and similar finish work, and movable partitions not over five feet high. It doesn't name toilet partitions, so ask Salt Lake City Building Services whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Salt Lake City Building Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Salt Lake City restroom needs

Utah's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in each men's room they may replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal then needs a screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and Utah's rules are the same in every city. An office that holds 25 people or fewer, or any space that holds 15 or fewer, does not need separate men's and women's rooms. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, Utah's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Salt Lake City

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Salt Lake City–Murray area earn a median of $28.78 an hour, about the same as the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Utah has no state wage law for public construction, so set wage rates apply only on federally funded projects. For a carpenter on building work in Salt Lake County the federal rate is $20.66 an hour plus $7.47 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 8.45% in Salt Lake City: 4.85% state, 1% local, 0.25% county, 1.25% in transit and transportation taxes, 0.1% for botanical, cultural and zoo organizations, a 0.5% correctional facility tax and a 0.5% capital city revitalization tax.
- **Tax on installation:** Utah does not tax installation labor that is listed separately on the invoice. Under a furnish-and-install contract for work that becomes part of the building, the contractor pays the tax on the materials and charges the customer none.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Salt Lake City are taxable. Freight is not taxed if it is listed separately on the invoice. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state as use tax.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Utah State Tax Commission.
