## The right material for El Paso's climate

El Paso gets about 9 inches of rain a year, on roughly 48 days, and relative humidity averages 50% in the morning and 27% in the afternoon. The weather rarely decides the material here. The room does:

- **Most restrooms** (offices, restaurants, retail, clinics, schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Rooms that get wet from the inside** (pool facilities, gym locker rooms, restrooms that are hosed down): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules an El Paso restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Texas's own access rules set the sizes. On projects of $50,000 or more, the state reviews the plans; smaller projects must still follow the rules.
- **Fire safety:** El Paso's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** El Paso sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Texas and federal stall rules match:** Texas's own access rules for stalls, doors and grab bars read the same as the federal ones, so a stall that meets one meets the other.
- **Family restrooms and changing stations:** El Paso's building rules call for a family or assisted-use toilet room in stores and in buildings where people gather. Every new business must also have a baby changing station on each floor that has restrooms, one that women can reach and one that men can reach, or a single one open to both, with a sign showing where it is.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a lockable one-person restroom in a commercial building must carry a gender-neutral sign. El Paso has required this since January 2024, and the person in charge of the building must put the sign up and keep it there. In a private building, a shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. Since December 2025, Texas law requires shared restrooms in city, county, school district and public college buildings to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** El Paso requires a permit to alter or repair a building unless the work is on its exempt list. The list covers "similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high, without naming toilet partitions, so ask the One Stop Shop at El Paso Planning and Inspections whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with El Paso Planning and Inspections or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls an El Paso restroom needs

El Paso counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional. They may stand in for up to half of the men's toilets in an office, and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. An office that holds 25 people or fewer, a store that holds 100 or fewer, or any other space that holds 15 or fewer, staff and customers together, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or El Paso Planning and Inspections sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in El Paso

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the El Paso area earn a median of $20.40 an hour, about 30% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on city, county and school district projects, Texas law requires at least the locally set wage. The federal rate for a carpenter on building work in El Paso County is $13.51 an hour plus $3.29 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 8.25% in El Paso: 6.25% state, 1% city, 0.5% county and 0.5% transit.
- **Tax on installation:** in Texas, labor to remodel an existing commercial building is taxed, even when it's billed separately. Labor on new construction is not.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to El Paso are taxable, freight included. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Texas Comptroller.
