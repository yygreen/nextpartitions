## The right material for Birmingham's climate

Birmingham gets about 57 inches of rain a year, on roughly 117 days, and relative humidity averages 84% in the morning and 55% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is March (about 5.7 inches) and the driest is October (about 3.3 inches). What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Birmingham restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Alabama uses the federal access rules as its own, so one set of sizes applies. The state reviews plans for schools, hotels, movie theaters and state-funded buildings, and checks city and county owned projects for access.
- **Fire safety:** Birmingham's building rules, like Alabama's state rules, require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Birmingham sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Alabama and federal stall rules match:** Alabama adopted the federal access rules in place of a state version, so a stall that meets the federal rules meets the state's.
- **Birmingham access rules:** the list of rules Birmingham reviews plans against also names a national accessibility standard, in its 2017 edition.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** under Birmingham's plumbing rules, as under the state's, a one-person restroom is marked as open to everyone, and a shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. Alabama law requires shared restrooms in public K-12 schools and public colleges to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** Birmingham requires a permit to alter or repair a building, and the city's permit guide says all commercial construction work needs one, whatever the size of the project. The list of work that needs no permit covers "nonfixed and movable fixtures, cases, racks, and counters" and doesn't name toilet partitions. Ordinary minor repairs may be made without a permit if the city's building official approves. Ask the Department of Planning, Engineering and Permits (205-254-2211) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Birmingham's Department of Planning, Engineering and Permits or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Birmingham restroom needs

Alabama's state building rules cover schools, hotels, movie theaters and state-funded buildings. For other buildings, such as private offices and restaurants, Birmingham's own rules decide. Both use the same table, so the counts match. Birmingham counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and fractions round up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to two thirds of the toilets in a restaurant or school and up to half in an office, and each urinal needs a privacy screen. The table shows men's toilets as stalls.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. An office designed for 25 people or fewer, a shop designed for 100 or fewer, and any building designed for 15 or fewer, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. Confirm the count with Birmingham's Department of Planning, Engineering and Permits.

To find your building's capacity, Birmingham's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or the Department of Planning, Engineering and Permits sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Birmingham

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Birmingham area earn a median of $24.23 an hour, about 17% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Alabama has no state wage law for public jobs, so a set wage applies only on federally funded work. For a carpenter on building work in Jefferson County that is $18.43 an hour, with no separate benefits amount.
- **Sales tax:** 10% in Birmingham: 4% state, 2% Jefferson County and 4% city.
- **Tax on installation:** in Alabama, an installation charge shown separately on the bill is not taxed. A single price for partitions and installation is taxed in full when a seller installs what it sells. A contractor who supplies and installs under a construction contract pays the tax on the materials instead of charging it to you.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Birmingham are taxable. An out-of-state seller in Alabama's simplified program collects a flat 8% in place of state and local tax. Freight by common carrier is not taxed when it is billed as a separate line. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Alabama Department of Revenue.
