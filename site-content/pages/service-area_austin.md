## The right material for Austin's climate

Austin gets about 36 inches of rain a year, on roughly 84 days, and relative humidity averages 83% in the morning and 55% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is May (about 5.0 inches) and the driest is February (about 1.9 inches). In most Austin restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules an Austin restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Texas's own access rules set the sizes. On projects of $50,000 or more, the state reviews the plans; smaller projects must still follow the rules.
- **Fire safety:** Austin's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Austin sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Texas and federal stall rules match:** Texas's own access rules for stalls, doors and grab bars read the same as the federal ones, so a stall that meets one meets the other.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a lockable one-person restroom in a commercial building must carry a gender-neutral sign. Austin has required this since December 2014, and the person in charge of the building must put the sign up and keep it there. In a private building, a shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and every urinal sits between partitions. Since December 2025, Texas law requires shared restrooms in city, county, school district and public college buildings to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** Austin lists the work that needs no building permit. The list covers "similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high, without naming toilet partitions, so ask Austin Development Services whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Austin Development Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls an Austin restroom needs

Austin counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. The city's table counts toilets and requires no urinals. Ask Austin Development Services how many of the men's toilets may be urinals.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. Where a building or tenant space holds 15 people or fewer, staff and customers together, or an office holds 25 or fewer, Austin doesn't require separate men's and women's rooms. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or Austin Development Services sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Austin

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Austin–Round Rock–San Marcos area earn a median of $24.16 an hour, about 17% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on city, county and school district projects, Texas law requires at least the locally set wage. The federal rate for a carpenter on building work in Travis County is $31.80 an hour plus $9.56 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 8.25% in Austin: 6.25% state, 1% city and 1% transit authority.
- **Tax on installation:** in Texas, labor to remodel an existing commercial building is taxed, even when it's billed separately. Labor on new construction is not.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Austin are taxable, freight included. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Texas Comptroller.

## FAQ

Austin lists the work that needs no building permit. The list covers "similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high, without naming toilet partitions, so ask Austin Development Services whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

### How many stalls does an Austin restroom need?
For a building that holds 100 people, an office needs at least 2 women's and 2 men's stalls, a restaurant needs at least 1 women's and 1 men's stall and a school needs at least 1 women's and 1 men's stall. The table above covers other sizes. Confirm the number for your building with Austin Development Services.

### Who can install toilet partitions in Austin?

### Which partition material is best in Austin?
