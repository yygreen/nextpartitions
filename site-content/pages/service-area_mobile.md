## The right material for Mobile's climate

Mobile gets about 67 inches of rain a year, on roughly 120 days, and relative humidity averages 87% in the morning and 57% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is air-conditioned:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms open to outside air or without air conditioning** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Mobile restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Alabama uses the federal access rules as its own, so one set of sizes applies. The state reviews plans for schools, hotels, movie theaters and state-funded buildings, and checks city and county owned projects for access.
- **Fire safety:** Mobile's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Mobile sets the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **Alabama and federal stall rules match:** Alabama adopted the federal access rules in place of a state version, so a stall that meets the federal rules meets the state's.
- **Mobile access rules:** Mobile's plan form for commercial projects asks whether the design meets the access chapter of the city's building rules and a national accessibility standard in its 2017 edition. The federal rules apply as well.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** under Mobile's plumbing rules a one-person restroom is marked as open to everyone. Mobile took out of its plumbing rules the provision that lets a shared all-gender room take the place of separate men's and women's rooms, so ask Build Mobile before you plan one. Alabama law requires shared restrooms in public K-12 schools and public colleges to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** Mobile requires a permit to alter a building unless the work is on its exempt list. The list covers "painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, counter tops and similar finish work" and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high. It doesn't name toilet partitions, which are fixed in place. Ask Build Mobile, the city's permitting department, whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Build Mobile or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Mobile restroom needs

Alabama has no statewide building rules for private commercial buildings, so Mobile's own rules decide. Mobile counts toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and fractions round up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to two thirds of the toilets in a restaurant or school and up to half in an office, and each urinal needs a privacy screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. An office designed for 25 people or fewer, a shop designed for 100 or fewer, and any building designed for 15 or fewer, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. Schools, hotels and movie theaters also come under Alabama's state building rules, which use the same counts. Confirm the count with Build Mobile.

To find your building's capacity, Mobile's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Mobile's plan form for commercial projects asks for the figure.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Mobile

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Mobile area earn a median of $23.34 an hour, about 20% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Alabama has no state wage law for public jobs, so a set wage applies only on federally funded work. For a carpenter on building work in Mobile County the federal rate is $18.16 an hour, with no benefits amount set.
- **Sales tax:** 10% in Mobile: 4% state, 1% Mobile County and 5% city.
- **Tax on installation:** in Alabama, an installation charge shown separately on the bill is not taxed. A single price for partitions and installation is taxed in full when a seller installs what it sells. A contractor who supplies and installs under a construction contract pays the tax on the materials instead of charging it to you.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Mobile are taxable. An out-of-state seller in Alabama's simplified program collects a flat 8% in place of state and local tax. Freight by common carrier is not taxed when it is billed as a separate line. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Alabama Department of Revenue.
