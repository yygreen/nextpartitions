## The right material for Rochester's climate

Rochester gets about 35 inches of rain a year, on roughly 163 days, and relative humidity averages 80% in the morning and 61% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is July (about 3.6 inches) and the driest is February (about 2.1 inches). What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Rochester restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and New York's building rules both set the sizes. New York has no separate state review for access: your local building department checks it with the building permit.
- **Fire safety:** New York's building rules treat toilet partitions as an interior wall finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene partitions to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** New York's statewide building rules, which Rochester enforces, set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. The table below shows what that means.
- **New York extras:** stall sizes match the federal ones, but New York adds a vertical grab bar, 18 inches or longer, on the side wall beside each accessible toilet. The open space under the wheelchair stall's front and side partitions must be 12 inches high, where federal rules ask for 9. The wheelchair turning space in the room can't be inside a stall.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** New York law requires every one-person restroom in a public place, school or state building to be marked gender neutral, with the sign on or near the door. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own stall with a door and each urinal is screened; it is not signed by sex, and its toilets are counted from the building's whole capacity. A Rochester ordinance adds that a business or other place open to the public may not limit a one-person bathroom to one gender, require transgender people to use only a one-person bathroom, or refuse a patron the public bathroom designated for the gender that matches their gender identity. Separate men's and women's rooms remain allowed.
- **Permits:** Rochester requires a building permit for work that has to meet the building rules. New York lets each city exempt some small jobs, such as painting, tiling and similar finish work, and partitions or movable cases under 5 feet 9 inches tall. That list doesn't name toilet partitions, and Rochester keeps its own list, so ask the City of Rochester Permit Office (585-428-6526) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the City of Rochester Permit Office or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Rochester restroom needs

Rochester uses New York's statewide rules, which count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs its own stall with a door. Urinals are optional: in each men's room they can replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a privacy screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. An office for 25 people or fewer, or any space for 15 or fewer, doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. A restaurant's count includes seasonal outdoor seating. The state lists no stricter local standard for Rochester. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room with tables and chairs at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Rochester

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Rochester area earn a median of $28.60 an hour, about 2% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on public work, such as school, city, county and state projects, New York law requires at least the prevailing wage for the trade, with no minimum contract size; the state Department of Labor publishes the rates by county each July. For a carpenter on building work in Monroe County that is $37.03 an hour plus $25.07 in benefits, on the schedule for July 2026 through June 2027.
- **Sales tax:** 8% in Rochester: 4% state and 4% Monroe County. The city adds no sales tax of its own.
- **Tax on installation:** in New York, installation that counts as a permanent improvement to the building is not taxed, and the owner gives the contractor a capital improvement certificate (Form ST-124). Repair, maintenance and other installation labor is taxed, even when billed separately. Work done for a tenant is treated as temporary, and so taxed, unless the lease shows it stays with the building. The state's list of examples doesn't name toilet partitions.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Rochester are taxable, freight included when the seller bills it. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state. Materials are taxed even when they go into a permanent improvement.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance.
