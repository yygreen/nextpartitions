## The right material for Worcester's climate

Worcester gets about 48 inches of rain a year, on roughly 134 days, and relative humidity averages 75% in the morning and 57% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is October (about 4.8 inches) and the driest is February (about 3.3 inches). What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Worcester restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law and Massachusetts's own access rules both apply, and the local building inspector enforces the state rules. On a remodel costing $100,000 or more, the building must also have an accessible toilet room and public entrance. An owner who can't meet a rule can ask the state's Architectural Access Board for a variance.
- **Fire safety:** Massachusetts's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Massachusetts sets the number of toilets by the people a building is set up for, and Worcester applies the state's count without changes of its own. The table below shows what that means.
- **Massachusetts extras:** the state's wheelchair stall is deeper than the federal one, at least 60 inches wide and 72 inches deep. It needs two 42-inch grab bars with a roughened, non-rusting finish, nothing mounted above them, a door that swings out and closes itself with a pull on both sides, and a coat hook. A room with six or more stalls also needs a second, 36-inch-wide stall with grab bars on both sides. In schools through sixth grade, toilet and grab bar heights are set by grade.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** Massachusetts lets men's and women's toilets be replaced by single-user rooms open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in a lockable compartment with partitions from floor to ceiling and a single-user room open to everyone is on the same floor within 300 feet. When an existing room is converted, partitions that lock may stay, but new ones must be full height. Where restrooms are separated by sex, state law lets each person use the one that matches their gender identity. Worcester adds a rule for its own buildings only: under a 2020 order by the city manager, each city-owned building open to the public must have at least one single-user restroom open to everyone, with a sign at its entrance.
- **Permits:** Worcester enforces the Massachusetts building rules, which require a permit to alter or repair a building and list the work that needs none. The list covers painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, counter tops and similar finish work, and movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches. Ordinary repairs need no permit either. The list doesn't name toilet partitions fixed to the floor or wall, so ask Worcester's Building and Zoning Division (508-799-1198) whether your replacement needs one. Worcester fines work done without a required permit, $1,000 on a commercial property. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with Worcester's Building and Zoning Division at Inspectional Services or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Worcester restroom needs

Massachusetts's rules count toilets by the people a building is set up for: seats plus staff in a restaurant, staff plus the usual number of visitors in an office, and student seats in a school. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet in a shared restroom needs its own stall. Urinals are optional: they may replace up to half of the men's toilets, and each needs a side screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 2/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 3/2 | 2/1 | 2/2
200: 5/4 | 4/2 | 4/4
400: 7/6 | 8/4 | 6/6
:::

School figures are for students; staff toilets are counted separately, one for every 20 women and one for every 25 men on staff. A business with no more than 20 staff and customers and no more than 2,000 square feet does not need separate men's and women's rooms. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

Massachusetts's toilet counts do not start from floor area: a restaurant counts its seats and the most staff on duty, an office counts the most staff on duty and its usual number of visitors, and a school counts student seats, so ask your plumbing inspector to confirm the number for your building.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Worcester

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Worcester area earn a median of $35.10 an hour, about 21% above the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** on state, county, city, town and school district construction, Massachusetts law requires at least the wage rates the state Department of Labor Standards sets for that project, with no minimum project size in the law. The state issues the rates project by project, so there is no standing rate table. On a schedule issued in November 2026 for a City of Worcester project, a carpenter is listed at $52.10 an hour plus $31.05 in benefits, rising to $53.35 an hour on March 1, 2027. On city building contracts over $100,000, Worcester also requires every bidder and subcontractor to certify that it pays the prevailing wage, takes part in a state-approved apprentice program and keeps a daily sign-in log.
- **Sales tax:** 6.25% in Worcester. That is Massachusetts's state rate, and no city or county sales tax on goods was found.
- **Tax on installation:** in Massachusetts, a charge for labor to install the partitions is not taxed. A contractor who supplies and installs them as part of the building generally pays the tax on the materials and does not add sales tax to the contract, so ask your contractor how the quote treats it.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Worcester are taxable at 6.25%. Freight shown as a separate charge for delivery after the sale is not taxed. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Massachusetts Department of Revenue.
