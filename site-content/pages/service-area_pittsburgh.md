## The right material for Pittsburgh's climate

Pittsburgh gets about 40 inches of rain a year, on roughly 154 days, and relative humidity averages 79% in the morning and 57% in the afternoon. What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Pittsburgh restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use, and a room with six or more toilets and urinals also needs a narrower stall with grab bars on both sides. Federal law and Pennsylvania's building rules set the sizes. Access is checked with the building permit, by your local code office or, where it has no certified access inspector, by the state Department of Labor & Industry. Only the state can approve an exception.
- **Fire safety:** Pennsylvania's building rules treat toilet partitions as an interior finish and require solid plastic (HDPE) and polypropylene partitions to pass a room-corner fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** in Pittsburgh the number of toilets and urinals comes from the Allegheny County Health Department's plumbing rules, not from the state plumbing rules used in most of Pennsylvania. The county has its own table and requires urinals in men's rooms. The table below shows what that means.
- **Pennsylvania extras:** Pennsylvania goes further than the federal rules in three places. The accessible stall needs an 18 inch vertical grab bar on the side wall as well as the horizontal one. In a very large restroom, 5% of the stalls must be wheelchair accessible, which means a second one from 21 stalls up. And a restaurant or store that needs six or more toilets in total must add a one-toilet family restroom.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and urinals are in stalls or screened from view. Allegheny County's plumbing rules say the same. In a restaurant or bar the county also requires a urinal in a one-person restroom, unless it is a family restroom. We found no Pennsylvania law or Pittsburgh ordinance that limits who may use which restroom.
- **Permits:** the city's Department of Permits, Licenses and Inspections (PLI) issues building permits in Pittsburgh. Its list of work that needs no permit includes ordinary repairs, painting, tiling, cabinets, countertops and similar finish work, and movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches. Cutting away a wall or partition is not an ordinary repair. The list doesn't name toilet partitions fixed to the floor or wall, so ask PLI (412-255-2175) whether your replacement needs one. Plumbing is the county's: installing, moving or replacing a toilet or urinal needs a plumbing permit from the Allegheny County Health Department's Plumbing Program (412-578-8036). You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Pittsburgh Department of Permits, Licenses and Inspections and the Allegheny County Health Department's Plumbing Program or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Pittsburgh restroom needs

In Pittsburgh the counts come from Allegheny County's plumbing rules. They count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women, and any fraction rounds up. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are required: in the men's room there must be at least half as many urinals as the toilets the county's table asks for, and urinals may take the place of up to half of those toilets. Each urinal needs a screen. The table shows women's stalls, then men's stalls plus urinals.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | Elementary school
50: 2/1+1 | 1/1+1 | 1/1+1
100: 3/2+2 | 2/1+1 | 2/1+1
200: 5/3+3 | 3/2+2 | 4/2+2
400: 8/4+4 | 5/3+3 | 7/3+3
:::

These are minimums for the whole building. Where the county's table gives the men's side an odd number, half of it isn't a whole number. The table above rounds both the stalls and the urinals up, and the county's rules don't say how to round, so confirm the split with the Plumbing Program (412-578-8036). For secondary schools and universities the county counts one toilet per 50 men and one per 40 women. A space with 15 people or fewer in total doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. A restaurant or bar with eight or more toilets in total must also have a single-user restroom. A separate Pennsylvania law asks for two women's toilets for every men's toilet or urinal in new or largely rebuilt public middle and high schools, arenas, stadiums and convention halls, so ask your building code official whether it covers your project. At least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, the building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Pittsburgh

- **Public jobs:** on projects paid for in whole or in part with public money and costing more than $25,000, school district work included, Pennsylvania law requires at least the wage the state Department of Labor & Industry sets for each trade in the county; maintenance work is not covered. The state issues its rates project by project. On federally funded building work in Allegheny County the carpenter rate is $42.11 an hour plus $23.21 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 7% in Pittsburgh: 6% state and 1% Allegheny County.
- **Tax on installation:** in Pennsylvania, a contractor who supplies and installs partitions that are bolted or screwed to the building doesn't charge sales tax on the job, and pays the tax on the materials instead. State tax rules presume that partitions fixed this way become part of the building. They don't spell out the case of an installer fitting partitions you bought yourself, so ask.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Pittsburgh are taxable, and so is delivery charged by the seller. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Pennsylvania Department of Revenue.
