## The right material for the Des Moines climate

Des Moines gets about 37 inches of rain a year, on roughly 108 days, and relative humidity averages 81% in the morning and 60% in the afternoon. On average the wettest month is June (about 5.3 inches) and the driest is January (about 1.1 inches). What that means for partitions depends on whether the restroom is heated and ventilated:

- **Heated, ventilated restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Unheated restrooms or rooms open to outside air** (parks, stadiums, warehouses, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

Keep [plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) for dry rooms; [stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules a Des Moines restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Iowa applies the federal sizes statewide, or lets a project follow the building code's own access chapter instead. Iowa has no separate state review for access: it is checked with the building plans, by the city or, for state buildings, by the state.
- **Fire safety:** Des Moines' building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Iowa's plumbing rules, which Des Moines enforces, set the number of toilets by how many people use the building, and the city's own building rules carry the same numbers. The table below shows what that means.
- **Iowa and federal stall rules match:** Iowa's access rules adopt the federal ones, so a stall that meets the federal sizes meets Iowa's. A project designed to the building code's access chapter instead also needs an 18 inch vertical grab bar beside the toilet. Iowa's plumbing rules also name a security standard for toilet partitions and urinal screens, so ask your plan reviewer whether it applies to your project.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must carry a sign showing that either sex may use it. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet and every urinal sits in its own privacy compartment. In public and private schools, Iowa law requires restrooms for more than one person to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** Des Moines requires a building permit for work covered by its building rules unless the work is on the city's exempt list. The list covers movable partitions no taller than 5 feet 9 inches, painting, papering and similar finish work, and minor maintenance and repair that the building official judges not to affect structure, safety, fire resistance or sanitation. It doesn't name toilet partitions fixed to the floor or wall, so ask the Des Moines Permit and Development Center (515-283-4200) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the Des Moines Permit and Development Center or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls a Des Moines restroom needs

Iowa's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. It isn't the number of staff or of visitors in a day. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a screen.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

An office or shop designed for 50 people or fewer may have one single-user restroom for everyone instead of separate rooms. A restaurant's seasonal outdoor seating counts toward its capacity. These are minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible.

To find your building's capacity, Des Moines' building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20. Your architect or the Permit and Development Center sets the final number.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Des Moines

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Des Moines–West Des Moines area earn a median of $29.50 an hour, about the same as the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Iowa has no state wage law for public jobs, so a set wage applies only on work paid for with federal money, where federal law sets it by county. For a carpenter on building work in Polk County that is $24.93 an hour plus $11.17 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 7% in Des Moines: 6% state and a 1% local option tax. Ankeny and Waukee have no local option tax, so the rate there is 6%.
- **Tax on installation:** in Iowa, installation labor is not taxed when it is part of new construction or a remodel, and a contractor who supplies and installs on that kind of job pays the tax when buying the partitions and adds none to the bill. Labor on a repair or a stand-alone installation is taxed. The state's guidance does not say which side a partition replacement falls on.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Des Moines are taxable. Delivery charges listed separately on the invoice are not. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Iowa Department of Revenue.
