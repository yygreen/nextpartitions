## The right material for Oklahoma City's climate

Oklahoma City gets about 36 inches of rain a year, on roughly 82 days, and relative humidity averages 80% in the morning and 55% in the afternoon. In most Oklahoma City restrooms the room decides the material, not the weather:

- **Air-conditioned restrooms** (offices, restaurants, clinics, most schools): all five materials work. Choose on looks, cleaning routine and budget.
- **Restrooms without air conditioning, or that get wet from the inside** (warehouses, plants, parks, stadiums, pool and locker rooms): choose [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) or [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic). Both are solid all the way through and handle constant moisture.

[Plastic laminate](/partitions/plastic-laminate) has a particleboard core that may swell with excessive moisture, and [powder coated steel](/partitions/powder-coated-steel) can rust where the coating is damaged, so keep both for dry rooms. [Stainless steel](/partitions/stainless-steel) resists corrosion and suits premium public restrooms. Compare all five on the [partitions page](/partitions).

## The rules an Oklahoma City restroom has to meet

- **Wheelchair access:** a restroom with stalls needs at least one stall a wheelchair user can enter and use. Federal law sets the sizes, and Oklahoma's building rules add a national accessibility standard on top. The city checks access with the building permit. Where no city or county issues building permits, the State Fire Marshal does.
- **Fire safety:** Oklahoma's building rules require solid plastic (HDPE) partitions to pass a room fire test. Ask for the fire-rated version.
- **How many stalls:** Oklahoma's building rules set the number of toilets by how many people use the building. Oklahoma City's own amendments leave those numbers as the state writes them. The table below shows what that means.
- **Oklahoma extras:** Oklahoma's rules ask for three things the federal ones do not. The side wall next to the toilet needs an 18 inch vertical grab bar as well as the horizontal one. The front partition and one side partition of the wheelchair stall need 12 inches of clear space above the floor, where the federal rule asks for 9. A new building's restroom needs a 67 inch turning circle, where the federal rule asks for 60. Stall widths and depths are the same in both.
- **Which rules apply:** Oklahoma brought in new statewide building and plumbing rules on September 14, 2026. Oklahoma City's list of adopted codes still shows the earlier ones, with the city's own amendments. Ask the city's Development Center Plan Review (405-297-2525) which set your project is reviewed under.
- **Single-user and all-gender restrooms:** a one-person restroom must be marked as open to everyone. A shared all-gender room is allowed if every toilet sits in its own compartment with a door and every urinal has privacy partitions. Oklahoma law requires shared restrooms in public schools, from pre-kindergarten to 12th grade, and in state correctional facilities to be designated for one sex.
- **Permits:** Oklahoma City requires a permit to construct, alter or repair a structure. Its list of work that needs no building permit includes painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, counter tops and similar finish work, and movable partitions up to 5 feet 9 inches high that aren't fixed in place. The list doesn't name toilet partitions. Ask the city's Development Center Plan Review (405-297-2525) whether your replacement needs one. You don't need a permit to buy partitions.

Rules change, so confirm your project with the City of Oklahoma City Development Center or your architect. The accessible stall's sizes are in our [ADA compliance guide](/guides/ada-compliance), and fixture spacing is in the [restroom layout guide](/guides/restroom-layout).

## How many stalls an Oklahoma City restroom needs

Oklahoma's rules count toilets by a building's capacity: the most people it is designed to hold at one time, worked out from its floor area. Half are counted as men and half as women. Every toilet needs a stall. Urinals are optional: in a men's room they may replace up to half of the toilets in an office and up to two thirds in a restaurant or school, and each urinal needs a screen on each side.

:::stalls
head: Office | Restaurant | School
50: 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1
100: 2/2 | 1/1 | 1/1
200: 3/3 | 2/2 | 2/2
400: 5/5 | 3/3 | 4/4
:::

These are the minimums for the whole building, and at least one stall in each room must be wheelchair accessible. Oklahoma City's amendments to the earlier rules add one exception: a space that isn't used for assembly, holds 75 people or fewer, serves no food or drink on site and needs only one toilet doesn't need separate men's and women's rooms. The city's list of adopted codes shows no amendments to the new state rules, so ask Plan Review whether that exception applies to your project. Where a shared room is built for all genders, the count is worked out on everyone in the building instead of on each half.

Oklahoma's building rules count an office at one person per 150 square feet of floor, a restaurant dining room at one per 15 square feet and a classroom at one per 20.

## What installation labor and sales tax add in Oklahoma City

- **Local labor rates:** carpenters in the Oklahoma City area earn a median of $23.53 an hour, about 19% below the national median of $29.12. It's a guide to local labor rates, not a quote: installers set their own prices.
- **Public jobs:** Oklahoma has no state wage law for public jobs, so a set wage applies only on federally funded projects, under federal law. For a carpenter on building work in Oklahoma County that rate is $16.61 an hour plus $3.00 in benefits.
- **Sales tax:** 8.625% in Oklahoma City: 4.5% state and 4.125% city. Oklahoma County adds no sales tax. The parts of the city in Canadian and Cleveland counties also pay a county tax.
- **Tax on installation:** in Oklahoma, an installation charge is not taxed when it is shown separately from the partitions on the bill. A single price for partitions and installation is taxed in full. A contractor who supplies and installs partitions as part of the building pays the tax when buying them.
- **Buying without installation:** partitions shipped to Oklahoma City are taxable at the rate for the delivery address. Freight is not taxed when it is listed separately on the invoice. If the seller doesn't collect the tax, the buyer owes it to the state.

How tax applies depends on your contract, so confirm with your accountant or the Oklahoma Tax Commission.
