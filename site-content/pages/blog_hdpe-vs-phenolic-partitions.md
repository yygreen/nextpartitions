# HDPE vs Phenolic Partitions: Which Is Better?

HDPE vs phenolic partitions is the usual choice for a restroom that gets wet or takes a beating. Both are solid materials. Neither has a honeycomb or particleboard core that can rust or swell, and manufacturers position both for showers, pools, locker rooms and schools. So the decision comes down to the details: surface hardness, panel thickness, how each is fire tested, which mounting styles each comes in, and price.

Neither material is better everywhere. This guide compares HDPE vs phenolic point by point, using what manufacturers and the building code publish, and ends with a short guide to choosing.

## How each material is made

![Solid plastic (HDPE) and phenolic partition doors cut through and seen edge on at the same scale: HDPE one solid 1 inch sheet, phenolic a 3/4 inch layered core](/images/diagrams/material-compare-hdpe-phenolic.svg)

Seen edge on at the same scale: HDPE is one solid sheet, 1" thick. Phenolic is thinner, 3/4", made of fused layers with decorative faces. The two drawings below show each in detail.

![Cross-section of a solid plastic (HDPE) partition door edge: one homogeneous 1 inch sheet with a rounded edge](/images/diagrams/material-section-solid-plastic.svg)

1. **Core:** none. The whole 1" thickness is one homogeneous HDPE sheet.
2. **Faces:** the same HDPE with the color running through, so there is no separate finish layer.
3. **Edge:** the same sheet, with no edge band, eased or rounded.

**HDPE (solid plastic)** is high-density polyethylene. Each door, panel and pilaster is cut from one sheet, with the color running all the way through. There is no core, no skin and no edge band. Parts are typically 1" thick, and specifications ask for no less than 1". Edges are eased or rounded, often to a 1/4" radius.

**Phenolic** is made by stacking layers of kraft paper soaked in phenolic resin and fusing them under heat and pressure into one dense sheet, with melamine faces. You will also see it called compact laminate or compact grade laminate (CGL). It comes in two versions:

- **Black core:** decorative faces over a black core, so the cut edges are black.
- **Color-through:** the color runs through the core and matches the face.

![Cross-section of a phenolic partition door edge: fused layers of resin-soaked kraft paper with melamine faces and a machined edge](/images/diagrams/material-section-phenolic.svg)

1. **Core:** layers of kraft paper impregnated with phenolic resin, fused under heat and pressure. Black on black core phenolic; the color-through version carries the color through the whole thickness.
2. **Faces:** melamine decorative surface.
3. **Edge:** the cut edge of the fused sheet, machined smooth. It shows the core color: black on black core, the panel color on color-through.

Phenolic parts are thinner. Panels are typically 1/2" thick, with 3/4" doors and pilasters. Edges are machined, and polished, beveled or chamfered depending on the line. Because the thicknesses differ, the parts don't swap: one manufacturer sizes its headrail at 1" for solid plastic and 3/4" for phenolic.

## Water and humidity

Both materials handle water well, which is why they are the two usual picks for wet rooms.

- **HDPE** is highly resistant to moisture, mildew and common cleaning agents. It won't easily dent, rust or delaminate, and it can be hosed down.
- **Phenolic** has no core to swell. Manufacturers say black core phenolic holds up under severe moisture from showers, pools and saunas, and some allow hosing down.

The real difference is in the paperwork. Care rules vary by brand, so check the warranty before you plan to hose any partition down.

## Abuse, scratches and graffiti

This is where the two materials differ most.

**HDPE has the softest surface of the common partition materials, so it scratches easily.** The color runs through, so a scratch doesn't show a different layer underneath. Graffiti usually wipes off with a common, nonabrasive cleaner. Xylene-based markers are the exception: they can "ghost" into the plastic and leave a shadow. Some material guides say gouges can be sanded out of HDPE, but we haven't seen manufacturer instructions for it, so ask before you try.

**Phenolic is hard.** It resists dents, scratches and graffiti. In one manufacturer's lab comparison, compact laminate was the hardest material to dent (ASTM D2794 impact test) and among the most scratch-resistant (ASTM D2197) and graffiti-resistant (ASTM D6578) materials tested. The caveat is black core: a deep scratch can expose the black core. Color-through phenolic avoids that. Don't count on sanding to hide damage on either version.

## Fire ratings and the building code

This is the point most often missed. The International Building Code, the International Fire Code, NFPA 101 and NFPA 1 regulate toilet partitions as interior finish. That means a partition must meet the fire performance required for the building's occupancy and for where the restroom sits. Your architect or local code official sets that requirement.

**Phenolic** is tested to ASTM E84 and given a class by flame spread index: Class A is 25 or less, Class B is 26 to 75, and Class C is 76 to 200. Black core phenolic is usually Class B as standard, with Class A available on some lines. Color-through and some other lines are Class A. Some manufacturers rate each color separately.

**HDPE** follows a different rule. The IBC (Section 803.9 in the 2015 and 2018 editions) requires HDPE and polypropylene used as interior finish to pass the NFPA 286 room-corner fire test. The requirement dates from the 2009 IBC and IFC and the 2012 NFPA codes. Untreated HDPE tested as unclassified under ASTM E84 because of its smoke: in one manufacturer's test it scored a smoke-developed index of 705, well over the limit of 450.

Fire-rated HDPE exists, but not every color passes. Standard color collections may not meet NFPA 286, and fire-rated colors can come in a shorter list, at extra cost, sometimes slightly darker. Specifications treat HDPE that complies with NFPA 286 as meeting Class A.

What to do: tell us the class or test your building needs, ask for the test report for the exact color and thickness, and confirm with your code official.

## Weight and mounting styles

**Phenolic is offered in all four mounting styles** by several manufacturers: [overhead braced](/mounting-styles/overhead-braced), [floor mounted](/mounting-styles/floor-mounted), [ceiling hung](/mounting-styles/ceiling-hung) and [floor to ceiling](/mounting-styles/floor-to-ceiling). Ceiling-hung phenolic needs a ceiling structure that can carry it. One manufacturer gives an approximate weight of 275 lb per compact grade partition, and some lines aren't recommended for ceilings over eight feet. Where the ceiling can't carry the load, floor-anchored or overhead-braced mounting is the recommended fallback.

**HDPE is not offered in every style.** Typical lines come overhead braced and floor to ceiling, with ceiling hung on some lines. Several manufacturers don't recommend floor-anchored or ceiling-hung mounting for solid plastic. Manufacturers rarely publish HDPE weights, so ask for the figure if your structure is tight.

Heights differ too. Some phenolic lines offer doors and panels at 58" (12" above the floor), 69" (9") and 72" (6"). Some floor-to-ceiling HDPE lines use 55" doors at 14" above the floor, offered for schools.

## How the hardware is fixed

The two materials hold hardware in different ways.

- **HDPE:** hinges, latches and brackets are usually aluminum or stainless. One published specification calls for 8" extruded aluminum wrap-around hinges through-bolted to the doors and pilasters with stainless Torx-head sex bolts, and the same tamper-resistant bolts for strikes and brackets. Some lines offer an integral hinge cut from the door and pilaster material.
- **Phenolic:** threaded inserts are factory installed in the doors for the hinges and latch, and the hardware is fastened with theft-resistant stainless pin-in-head Torx screws.

Hardware often carries its own warranty, from 1 year up to lifetime on stainless steel hardware. Ask for tamper-resistant fasteners in schools and public restrooms. See [hinges](/hardware/hinges) and [replacement parts](/hardware/parts).

HDPE has color all the way through and soft, rounded edges. Its color range is more limited than laminate's, and fire-rated colors narrow it further. Some HDPE lines offer a shiplap door edge that removes the sightline between door and pilaster without extra hardware.

Phenolic offers a wider range of decorative faces. Black core shows a crisp black edge; color-through matches the edge to the face. Several phenolic lines offer rabbeted or overlapping door edges for privacy. For more on closing gaps, see [why bathroom stalls have gaps](/blog/why-do-bathroom-stalls-have-gaps).

## Warranty

Warranties are set by the brand and product line, not the material name.

- **HDPE:** published terms run from 15 to 25 years, and 25 is common. Coverage is usually limited to defects that result in breakage or delamination.
- **Phenolic:** published terms run from 3 to 25 years, and many lines carry 25. Coverage is some mix of breakage, corrosion, delamination, discoloration and defects in workmanship.

For both, vandalism is typically excluded, installation errors or improper care can void the warranty, and labor is often not covered. Your quote names the warranty for your line.

## Cost

Prices depend on brand, line, size and options, so use your quote, not a rule of thumb. One manufacturer's published price index puts untreated HDPE at 1.9 and compact laminate (phenolic) at 2.4, against 1.0 for painted metal. On that index, phenolic runs roughly a quarter more than untreated HDPE. The gap narrows when you need a fire-rated HDPE color, since those can cost extra.

## Cleaning

- **HDPE:** wipe with warm water and mild liquid soap, and hose down if needed. A mild commercial cleaner handles tougher stains. Remove the clear protective coating after installation. No abrasive pads or caustic solutions.
- **Phenolic:** wipe with warm water and very mild soap on a nonabrasive cloth, then rinse and dry. No brushes, steel wool or scouring pads, and keep acid, alkali and bleach-based bathroom cleaners off it, rinsing any splash at once.

Follow your manufacturer's care instructions for either.

## Best uses by building type

Both materials suit schools, universities, gyms, locker rooms, pool and shower areas, and public restrooms. One manufacturer's building-type guide rates compact laminate higher than HDPE for heavy-traffic buildings such as K-12 schools, malls, recreation centers and transit hubs, and rates both as good for standard-use buildings such as offices, healthcare and hospitality. It rates HDPE poor for prestige buildings, where phenolic's decorative faces fit better.

In practice:

- **Pools, showers and locker rooms:** either, with the fire rating confirmed. If you want ceiling-hung stalls, check that the HDPE line allows it.
- **Schools with heavy abuse:** phenolic for dent and scratch resistance; HDPE if the budget is tight and xylene markers aren't a big problem.
- **Design-led or public-facing restrooms:** phenolic, especially color-through.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose phenolic if:**

- You need ceiling-hung or floor-anchored mounting.
- Dents, scratches and graffiti are the main risk.
- You want Class A without a separate NFPA 286 test (pick a Class A line, usually color-through).
- You want a wider choice of decorative faces.

**Choose HDPE if:**

- Budget matters most and the line's fire-rated grade fits your code.
- The room is hosed down and you want color all the way through.
- Overhead-braced or floor-to-ceiling mounting suits your building.

**Before either:** confirm the fire requirement with your architect or code official, then compare both in one quote. See the [solid plastic (HDPE)](/partitions/solid-plastic-hdpe) and [phenolic](/partitions/phenolic) pages for specs.

## FAQ

### Is HDPE or phenolic better for bathroom partitions?
Neither is better everywhere. Phenolic is harder, comes in all four mounting styles and is tested to ASTM E84. HDPE costs less on one manufacturer's price index and has color all the way through, but it scratches more easily and must pass NFPA 286.

### Which lasts longer, HDPE or phenolic?
Both are solid materials that resist water. Published warranties run from 15 to 25 years for HDPE and from 3 to 25 years for phenolic, depending on the line. Phenolic resists dents and scratches better.

### Are HDPE partitions fire rated?
Not by default. The IBC requires HDPE partitions to pass the NFPA 286 room-corner test, and untreated HDPE doesn't. Fire-rated grades are available, so specify one and ask for the test report.

### Is phenolic more expensive than HDPE?
Usually. One manufacturer's price index puts phenolic at 2.4 and untreated HDPE at 1.9, relative to painted metal at 1.0. Fire-rated HDPE colors can cost extra, so compare both in your quote.

### Can HDPE partitions be ceiling hung?
Some lines offer it, but several manufacturers don't recommend ceiling-hung or floor-anchored mounting for solid plastic. Phenolic is offered in all four styles.

### Can graffiti be removed from HDPE and phenolic?
Usually, with a nonabrasive cleaner. Xylene-based markers can ghost into HDPE. Phenolic resists graffiti, but a deep scratch on black core phenolic can expose the black core.
