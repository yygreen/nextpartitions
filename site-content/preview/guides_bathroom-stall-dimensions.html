Bathroom Stall Dimensions: Standard and ADA Sizes

Bathroom stall dimensions come from three places: the plumbing code sets the smallest compartment, the 2010 ADA Standards set the accessible compartments, and manufacturers set everything else, from door width to the gap under the panels.

This guide puts all three in one place for commercial restrooms: standard bathroom stall size, ADA bathroom stall dimensions, panel and door heights, and how many accessible compartments a restroom needs.

Standard bathroom stall size

There is no single "standard" in a code. The usual commercial bathroom stall size is a compartment about 36" wide and 60" or slightly deeper, and it is common because it clears the plumbing minimums with room to spare.

  • Width. The IPC sets 30" as the narrowest compartment for any toilet (405.3.1). Bradley's example layout for its PH400 phenolic partitions uses two standard compartments of 36" each.
  • Depth. The IPC minimum is 60" for a floor-mounted toilet and 56" for a wall-hung one. Layouts often run a little deeper. In Bradley's PH400 example the compartments are 61-1/4" deep to the face of the door and panels.
  • Check what a published width means. Bradley's example calls the accessible compartment 60" clear and 60-1/2" overall. Ask your supplier whether a size is clear or overall.

You will also see a "narrow" 30" stall. That is the IPC minimum, not a separate product class. Treat it as a floor, and confirm door swing and clearances before specifying it.

A row of stalls is the sum of its compartments. Bradley's example is one 60-1/2" accessible compartment, one 36-1/2" ambulatory compartment and two 36" standard compartments: 169" along the wall in all.

How to read the plan, which is based on Bradley's PH400 data sheet:

  • Left to right: the accessible (ADA) compartment, 60" clear and 60-1/2" overall, with an outswing door and grab bars; the ambulatory compartment, 36" clear and 36-1/2" overall, with grab bars on both sides; then two standard compartments, 36" each, with inswing doors.
  • The 169" figure is the overall length of the run. 61-1/4" to face is the depth to the face of the doors and panels.
  • Door swings: the accessible and ambulatory doors swing out, the standard doors swing in, as in Bradley's example.
  • Grab bars follow 2010 ADA Standards 604.5 (the side bar is at least 42" long and starts no more than 12" from the rear wall; the rear bar is at least 36" long).

The drawing is one manufacturer's example layout, not a rule. Your sizes depend on the brand and the room.

The gaps in a standard stall

Standard partitions have four gaps (see the drawing above). On Bobrick's published tolerances the door-to-pilaster gap is 3/32" on the hinge side and 7/64" at most on the keeper side, and the panel or pilaster to wall gap is 1/2" to 1". Other manufacturers' gaps may be wider. Why they exist, and how no-sightline doors close them, is in why do bathroom stalls have gaps.

Bathroom stall door width

Stall door width is the measurement people get wrong most often.

  • Standard doors are sized by the manufacturer to suit the compartment. They come in specific widths, not round numbers. Metpar's standard powder coated doors run from 21-5/8" to 35-5/8", for example.
  • Accessible doors must give at least 32" of clear opening when the door is open at 90 degrees (2010 ADA Standards 404.2.3, applied by 604.8.1.2). The door leaf has to be wider than the clear opening it leaves, and a 36" door is a common product size.
  • Do not swing into the minimum area. The accessible compartment door may not swing into the minimum required compartment area (604.8.1.2). A minimum-size compartment therefore takes an outswing door. A larger compartment can take an inswing door as long as the minimum area stays clear.

To replace a door, measure the door itself, not the opening. See replacement doors.

ADA bathroom stall dimensions

This section follows the 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design, section 604. State codes that adopt the Standards (or ICC A117.1) may differ in places, so check the version your project is built under.

Wheelchair accessible compartment (604.8.1)

  • Size (604.8.1.1). At least 60" wide, measured at right angles to the side wall. At least 56" deep with a wall-hung toilet, or 59" deep with a floor-mounted toilet, measured at right angles to the rear wall.
  • Toilet centerline (604.2). 16" to 18" from the side wall or partition.
  • Door (604.8.1.2). Self-closing, with a pull on both sides near the latch, at least 32" clear (404.2.3), and not swinging into the minimum compartment area. If you approach on the latch side, 42" of clearance is required. A front-partition door sits within 4" of the side wall or partition farthest from the toilet. A side-wall door sits within 4" of the front partition.
  • Toe clearance (604.8.1.4). The front partition and at least one side partition need at least 9" of toe clearance above the floor, extending 6" deep beyond the compartment face of the partition. It is not required on the front partition if the compartment is deeper than 62" with a wall-hung toilet or 65" with a floor-mounted toilet, or on a side partition if the compartment is wider than 66".

The toe clearance rule is where partition choice matters. A 12" floor clearance meets it. Lowering the gap to 6" for privacy does not, in a compartment of minimum size. Privacy lines solve this with a taller panel mounted higher (Bradley's 69" panel at 9" for ADA stalls is an example).

Ambulatory accessible compartment (604.8.2)

  • Size (604.8.2.1). 35" to 37" wide and at least 60" deep.
  • Toilet centerline (604.2). 17" to 19" from the side wall or partition. This is different from the wheelchair compartment.
  • Door (604.8.2.2). Self-closing, 32" clear, and it may not swing into the minimum area.
  • Grab bars (604.8.2.3). Required on both side walls.

Children's compartments (604.9)

Compartments designed for children's use must be 60" wide and 59" deep, and need 12" of toe clearance instead of 9" (604.8.1.1, 604.8.1.4). The advisory to 604.9 applies the children's dimensions to ages 3 through 12. The rest of 604.9 sets toilet centerline, seat height and grab bar positions for children's use. Take them from the Standards, because they change by age group.

Grab bars and hardware

Grab bars follow 604.5, and the compartment door hardware must work with one hand without tight grasping, pinching or twisting of the wrist, at 5 pounds of force at most (309.4). Coat hooks and shelves in an accessible compartment have to sit within reach ranges (604.8.3).

How many accessible compartments does a restroom need?

213.3.1 sets two rules:

  1. Where toilet compartments are provided in a toilet room, at least one must be wheelchair accessible, complying with 604.8.1. That applies even to a restroom with a single compartment. It is not true that every restroom needs "an ADA stall" in the sense of a separate extra stall. The wheelchair compartment is one of the compartments.
  2. Where a toilet room has six or more toilet compartments, or the toilets and urinals together total six or more fixtures, an ambulatory accessible compartment is also required, complying with 604.8.2.

A men's room with four stalls and two urinals reaches six fixtures, so it needs the ambulatory compartment too. The wheelchair and ambulatory compartments are separate stalls.

Urinal screens

Urinal screens are short partitions between or beside urinals. The spacing rule is the plumbing code's: 15" from the center of a urinal to a side wall or partition, and 30" center to center. Screens, spacing and the clear space in front of the fixtures are covered in our restroom layout guide.

How to measure a new restroom for stalls

A supplier needs a handful of numbers to quote a layout:

Start with the room seen from above. These four measurements decide how many stalls fit and where the doors can swing:

  • A. room width along the wall where the stalls go.
  • B. room depth, from the stall wall to the opposite wall.
  • D. entry door location and which way it swings.
  • E. toilet centerlines, measured from the side wall.

Then look at the room from the side, as if it were cut through. This view is about height and structure: what the partitions stand on and what they can hang or brace from, which decides the mounting style.

  • C. ceiling height, floor to finished ceiling.
  • F. floor construction (slab, tile over slab) and the structure above the ceiling (open steel, concrete deck, drop ceiling).

Add photos of the walls, floor and ceiling. Then get a quote. For where each mounting style needs structure, see mounting styles.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are standard bathroom stall dimensions?

A typical commercial stall is about 36" wide and 60" deep. The plumbing code minimum is 30" wide by 60" deep with a floor-mounted toilet, or 56" deep with a wall-hung one. Doors and panels are usually 58" tall, mounted 12" above the floor.

What are the ADA bathroom stall dimensions?

A wheelchair accessible compartment is at least 60" wide and at least 56" deep (wall-hung toilet) or 59" deep (floor-mounted toilet), with a self-closing door at least 32" clear (2010 ADA Standards 604.8.1). An ambulatory compartment is 35" to 37" wide and at least 60" deep (604.8.2).

How wide is a bathroom stall door?

It depends on the manufacturer and compartment. Metpar's standard powder coated doors run from 21-5/8" to 35-5/8". An accessible stall door must leave at least 32" of clear opening, so the door itself is wider than 32".

Does an ADA stall door have to swing out?

No rule says it must. The door can't swing into the minimum required compartment area (604.8.1.2), which in a minimum-size compartment means it swings out. A larger compartment can take an inswing door.

How many ADA stalls does a restroom need?

At least one wheelchair accessible compartment wherever there are toilet compartments, plus an ambulatory compartment where there are six or more compartments or six or more toilets and urinals combined (213.3.1).

Can the floor gap be smaller than 12" in the accessible stall?

Only with care. The front partition and one side partition need 9" of toe clearance (12" in children's compartments) unless the compartment is larger than the exception sizes in 604.8.1.4.
