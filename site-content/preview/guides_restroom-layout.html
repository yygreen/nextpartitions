Commercial Restroom Layout Guide

A commercial restroom layout starts with the wall the stalls go on, and ends with whether the doors, the fixtures and a wheelchair all have the room they need.

This guide covers how stalls fit along a wall, the plumbing code's fixture spacing, door swing and clear floor space, the accessible route, urinal screens, and which mounting style suits your floor and ceiling.

Have a plan or just measurements? Get a quote. Standard configurations are quoted within 2 business hours; custom projects within 24–48 hours.

Step 1: Measure the room

Start with the room seen from above. These four measurements decide how many stalls fit and where the doors can swing:

  • A. room width along the wall where the stalls go.
  • B. room depth, from the stall wall to the opposite wall.
  • D. entry door location and which way it swings.
  • E. toilet centerlines, measured from the side wall.

Then look at the room from the side, as if it were cut through. This view is about height and structure: what the partitions stand on and what they can hang or brace from, which decides the mounting style.

  • C. ceiling height, floor to finished ceiling.
  • F. floor construction (slab, tile over slab) and the structure above the ceiling (open steel, concrete deck, drop ceiling).

A and E decide how many stalls fit. C and F decide the mounting style. D decides what a person sees and where the doors can swing.

Step 2: Count the stalls along the wall

A row of stalls is the sum of its compartments, so the count comes from the wall length. Bradley's example layout shows how: an accessible compartment 60-1/2" wide, an ambulatory compartment 36-1/2" wide and two standard compartments 36" wide add up to 169" along the wall. The plan is 61-1/4" deep to the face of the doors and panels.

Use the compartment sizes in bathroom stall dimensions: 36" is the typical standard width, the IPC minimum is 30" wide by 60" deep with a floor-mounted toilet (56" with a wall-hung one), and the accessible compartment is at least 60" wide. Add them up and compare the total to dimension A. Then add the required accessible compartments before you fill the rest with standard ones:

  • Where there are toilet compartments, at least one must be wheelchair accessible (2010 ADA Standards 213.3.1).
  • Where there are six or more compartments, or six or more toilets and urinals combined, an ambulatory compartment is also required (213.3.1).

How the stalls sit in the room:

  • Between walls. Stalls span the full width of the room, wall to wall. Pilasters at both ends meet the side walls.
  • Corner or one end. Stalls run along one wall with a single end pilaster, common in small restrooms of two or three stalls.

Step 3: Fixture spacing

Spacing comes from two sources. The plumbing code sets the minimums for every fixture, and the ADA adds more for accessible ones.

Fixture Rule Source
Toilet, centerline to side wall or partition 15" minimum IPC 405.3.1
Toilet, centerline to the next toilet or fixture 30" minimum center to center IPC 405.3.1
Clear space in front of toilet, urinal or lavatory 21" minimum IPC 405.3.1
Toilet compartment, any toilet 30" wide minimum, 60" deep (56" if wall-hung) IPC 405.3.1
Accessible toilet, centerline from side wall 16" to 18" ADA 604.2
Ambulatory toilet, centerline from side wall 17" to 19" ADA 604.2
Urinal, centerline to side wall or partition 15" minimum IPC 405.3.1
Urinal, centerline to next urinal 30" minimum center to center IPC 405.3.1
Accessible lavatory, clear floor space 30" x 48", with knee and toe clearance ADA 305.3, 606.2, 306

Your jurisdiction decides which edition of the IPC applies, and it may amend it. Confirm with your plumbing inspector.

Urinal spacing

  • From the side wall or a partition to the centerline of a urinal: 15" minimum.
  • From one urinal's centerline to the next: 30" minimum.
  • In front of the urinal: 21" minimum clear.
  • Urinal screens, drawn between the urinals, give privacy. The spacing is measured to the urinal centerlines, so confirm the screen size and where it mounts with the manufacturer before you set the layout.

The spacing numbers the old page gave (13" to 15" from the wall, 24" to 30" between urinals) are below the IPC minimums. See the corrections below.

Step 4: Door swing and clear floor space

Every door needs floor to swing over.

  • Standard compartments usually have inswing doors, and the door sweeps part of the compartment. Check that the swing doesn't hit the toilet or the toilet paper dispenser.
  • The accessible compartment door must not swing into the minimum required compartment area (604.8.1.2), so a minimum-size compartment takes an outswing door. A larger compartment can take an inswing door.
  • The outswing door then opens into the room, so it must not block another door, a fixture or the route to the lavatory.
  • Latch-side approach. If a person approaches the accessible door on the latch side, 42" of clearance is required beside the door (604.8.1.2).
  • Door position. The accessible compartment door sits within 4" of the side wall or partition farthest from the toilet (front-partition door), or within 4" of the front partition (side-wall door).

Step 5: The accessible route and turning space

The ADA wants a turning space inside the room: a 60" circle or a T-shaped space (304.3). Plan the layout so the accessible compartment's door swing doesn't encroach on that space, or on the clear floor space at the lavatory. Doors on the accessible route need at least 32" of clear opening (404.2.3).

Step 6: Entry and sight lines

Look at what a person sees when the entry door opens. Place stall doors and panels so a person at the door doesn't look straight into an open compartment. That is a planning judgment, not a code number. Taller, lower-gap panels add privacy (see why do bathroom stalls have gaps), but the accessible compartment still needs 9" of toe clearance on the front and one side partition (604.8.1.4).

Step 7: Choose the mounting style from the floor and ceiling

Measurements C and F decide this.

What the building has Style that fits Why
No structure above the ceiling, floor takes anchors Overhead braced or floor mounted Bobrick recommends these when adequate ceiling support can't be provided
Floor can't take anchors Ceiling hung Nothing touches the floor; the building must supply engineered steel above the ceiling
High ceiling or open look Floor mounted No headrail; needs a sound slab (Bobrick: anchors 2" into concrete at least 3" thick)
Heavy use, schools, public restrooms Floor to ceiling Anchored at floor and ceiling

Frequently Asked Questions

How far should a urinal be from the wall?

At least 15" from the urinal centerline to the side wall or partition, and at least 30" center to center between urinals (IPC 405.3.1).

How much space between toilets in a commercial restroom?

At least 30" center to center, and 15" from the toilet centerline to a side wall or partition (IPC 405.3.1). Compartments are at least 30" wide.

How much clear space is needed in front of a toilet?

At least 21" in front of the fixture (IPC 405.3.1). Accessible compartments have larger minimums in ADA 604.

Which mounting style is best?

It depends on the ceiling and floor. See the table above.
