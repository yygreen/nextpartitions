The spec sheet

Our buyers trust manufacturer spec sheets and shop drawings, not lifestyle photos. So every visual looks like a good spec sheet: exact, quiet, measured, with one thing called out in orange. Built on the live brand: the orange N, Inter, the warm off-white and brown.

Three rules

Everything else follows from these.

1One subject, in orangeOrange marks only what the visual is about: the support, the gap, the measurement. Two orange things means two drawings.
2The building is grey, the product is whiteFloors, walls and ceilings are hatched warm grey; partitions are white with ink outlines. What we sell versus what the building provides, at a glance.
3Measured, and sourcedTo scale (3.2 px per inch). Every dimension comes from a cited source on the page it sits on. No decorative numbers.

Line

Product: white, ink outline 1.5 px
Building: hatched Structure, ink edge
The subject: solid orange, or a 2.5–5 px orange line
Dimension: muted 1 px with end ticks; label beside it
Movement or hidden parts: dashed 5 5
Callout: numbered disc, leader ends in a dot; the key is page text

The drawings

Ten so far, generated from shared tokens and parts (design/diagrams.mjs). Each carries a title and description for screen readers; its key sits under it on the page.

Do and don't

Do
  • Front elevations for how stalls stand; plans (seen from above) for hinges and swing
  • Real dimensions from the page's sources
  • Keys as page text under the drawing
  • Real job photos, straight on
Don't
  • Perspective or 3D views: they hide the measurements
  • Orange text, or more than one orange subject
  • Labels inside the image that the page doesn't repeat
  • Stock photos of restrooms
