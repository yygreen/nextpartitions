# Bathroom Partitions for Restaurants
 The restroom is part of your restaurant's brand. Pick a partition material that reads as part of the dining experience, not the back of house.
[Get a Quote](/get-a-quote)
 A restaurant restroom is a brand touchpoint. Guests read it as a proxy for the kitchen, so the partitions have to look intentional while surviving grease, mop water, and a full weekend rush. The right spec balances the finish you want in the dining room with the cleanability the closing crew needs at 1 a.m.
 We supply restaurant partitions in every material from budget powder coated steel to designer laminate and stainless steel, cut to your room dimensions and shipped nationwide. Most restaurant restrooms are a two- to four-stall run, which we can quote, configure, and ship as a complete kit with hardware.
## What Restaurants Need
- Finishes that coordinate with the dining room, not the back of house 
- Surfaces that wipe clean of grease film and hand-soap residue 
- Moisture tolerance for nightly mopping and hose-down floors 
- Compact layouts — restrooms are usually the smallest room in the plan 
- At least one ADA-compliant compartment in a multi-stall restroom 
- Fast lead times so the restroom isn't the item that delays opening 
- Hardware that survives constant use without loosening 
- Occupancy indicator latches so guests do not rattle doors

## Planning & Compliance
- Standard stall widths run 36 inches; ADA compartments are typically 60 inches wide by 56 to 59 inches deep. 
- Standard panel height is 55 inches set 12 inches off the floor — raise the panel height for a more private, upscale feel. 
- Confirm door swing: ADA compartments require an outswing door where depth is under 60 inches. 
- Measure wall-to-wall at floor level and at 30 inches high — restaurant walls are rarely square after tile. 
- Plan for a coat hook, occupancy latch, and door stop on every door.
