# Partitions for Public Restrooms
 Parks, transit stations, stadiums, and high-traffic public facilities need the most durable, vandal-resistant partitions on the market.
[Get a Quote](/get-a-quote)
 Public restrooms in parks, transit stations, stadiums, rest areas, and municipal buildings see traffic measured in thousands of users a day, plus daily washdowns and vandalism that would destroy a school restroom in a week. The partition has to be vandal resistant, hose rated, and still look consistent after a decade of service.
 Phenolic and stainless steel are the two materials that reliably pay back their premium in this category, with HDPE as the value alternative. We quote all three side by side so agencies can defend the spec on lifecycle cost, not just first cost.
## What Public Facilities Need
- Vandal-resistant surfaces that survive cutting, burning, and impact 
- Graffiti removal without repainting or replacing panels 
- Hose-down and pressure-wash tolerance 
- Tamper-resistant fasteners and heavy-duty continuous hinges 
- Corrosion resistance for coastal, outdoor, and unconditioned sites 
- Sightline control and emergency access hardware 
- Long service life to fit municipal replacement cycles 
- Availability of replacement parts years after install

## Planning & Compliance
- Standard stalls are 36 inches wide; ADA compartments are 60 inches wide with a 36-inch outswing door. 
- Add an ambulatory-accessible stall once a restroom has six or more compartments. 
- Specify tamper-resistant fasteners and continuous hinges on every door, not just problem stalls. 
- Consider raised panel heights and reduced floor clearance to limit reach-under and pass-through. 
- Stock a spare hardware kit — public facilities replace latches and hinges long before panels.
