### Strategy to explain

**Directory model: referral only.**
- Next Partitions keeps selling partitions only. Installers are independent businesses; the customer hires, schedules and pays them directly. So there are no installation sales and no liability for installers' work, and every directory page carries a disclaimer saying so.
- **How we keep installers from buying around us.** Five layers, each covering a different leak:
  1. *Order of steps:* the customer gets a partitions quote from us first, then installation. They're our customer before they meet an installer.
  2. *Contact details hidden:* installers see the job scope, not the customer's details, until the customer chooses one.
  3. *Listing agreement:* installers install the partitions on the customer's Next Partitions order, offer no substitutes, report job status, and are delisted if they break this.
  4. *Trade accounts:* installers get trade pricing or a rebate on partitions for their own jobs, so buying from us pays them too.
  5. *Call tracking:* calls from profile pages are counted, so we see which referrals happened and can match them to orders.

**Labels.**
- Every listed installer is **Approved**: installs customer-bought partitions, insurance and business or city registration on file, references contacted, signed listing agreement, one completed job.
- **Established** (10+ installs for our customers) and **Top rated** (4.8+ from 10+ reviews) are earned extras.
- The labels state facts, not endorsements, which keeps the liability position clean.

**Installer recruiting (from the call with Joseph).**
- Screening question: "Do you install partitions the customer already bought?" Installers who only install what they sell are a poor fit.
- Prefer labor-only crews: Division 10 freelancers, fixture and millwork installers.
- Find them through job posts and referrals from general contractors and other installers. Abe's own contacts are the fastest start.

**Content opportunities.**
- **Installer keyword cluster:** 147 keywords, about 1,700 searches a month, mostly difficulty 0–5 (very low competition). The two biggest wins:
  - an installed-cost guide ("commercial toilet partition cost installed"), one permanent page that covers all the year and word-order variants,
  - a how-to-install guide, with sections for ceiling-hung, hardware and common problems.
- **Licensing and job-site rules cluster:** who may install partitions by state, city and building type (e.g. school background checks, accessibility review). It feeds the city and installer pages and builds trust. Published only after legal review.
- **"Toilet partition installers near me"** is the directory's main target: $6.88 a click if bought as an ad, and Google shows a map pack, which means local intent. Tampa shows up in several variants, so it's a candidate for the next area.

**Competitor note.** RTI Supply came up as the recommendation in every AI tool we asked about Miami. Worth studying what they publish that makes them the cited source.

## 4. Decisions we need from Abe

For each one, get an answer in the call, or agree who decides and by when.

1. **Directory model.** Is he OK with referral only, no responsibility for installation, and the listing agreement terms (install the partitions on the customer's Next Partitions order, no substitutes, report job status, delisting on breach)? Everything else in the directory depends on this.
2. **Approval criteria and vetting.** Does he agree with the Approved checklist? *Agreed internally:* we do the vetting (checking insurance and registration, calling references), so it costs his team no time. After approval, ratings and reviews from verified customers keep quality up, with low ratings leading to a check and possible delisting. Ask him only to confirm, and to flag any installers he already trusts or wants excluded.
3. **Pilot area.** DFW, or does his order history point elsewhere (e.g. Tampa)? The orders-by-ZIP data settles it. The prototypes are built for DFW, but switching is cheap now and expensive later.
4. **Trade accounts.** Will he give installers trade pricing on partitions, or a rebate per referred order? This is the main reason an installer stays loyal, so it needs a number.
5. **Install-only requests** (the customer bought partitions elsewhere). Accept them, put them behind requests from our own customers, or decline? Accepting brings more installer interest; declining keeps the directory focused on partition sales.
6. **Homepage.** The three-ways-into-a-quote version, the facility-picker version, or test both against each other?
7. **Call tracking.** OK to use tracked numbers on installer profiles? It costs a small amount per number per month and is how we prove referrals.
8. **Publishing approvals.** Who signs off on content, and how fast? A 48-hour turnaround keeps the weekly shipping rhythm.
9. **Who quotes installation.** The installer does, never Next Partitions. *Our proposal:* each installer agrees a rate card up front (per stall, by material and mounting, plus extras such as removing old stalls, ADA stalls and a minimum charge). The quote then shows an instant installation estimate next to the partitions price, as a separate line: "estimate from the installer, quoted and billed by them". The installer confirms a firm price within an agreed time after seeing the shop drawings, and the customer accepts it with the installer directly. A single "full quote" from Next Partitions that includes installation would make us the seller of installation, which breaks referral only. His lawyer should check the wording.
10. **Money flow.** *Our proposal:* Next Partitions earns its margin on the partitions only. No markup on installation: the installer keeps all of their installation fee and doesn't mark up or resell the partitions on our jobs (already covered by "no substitutes" in the listing agreement). Trade pricing on the installer's own jobs stays separate (decision 4).
11. **Response rate and time as an approval check.** *Our proposal:* test each applicant with a sample job request during vetting, then track it on every real job. Suggested targets: reply to a job within 1 business day and confirm a firm price within 2. Show the fact on the profile ("usually replies within X hours"). Slow or missed replies lower the installer's place in the list and, if they continue, lead to delisting.
12. **Licensing.** Depends on the state and city (`research/installer-regulations.md`). In DFW there is no Texas state license, but Dallas requires city contractor registration to pull permits (about $120 a year, $300,000 general liability insurance with the City of Dallas as certificate holder). We'd add it to the Approved checklist for Dallas jobs. Fort Worth still needs research. School jobs in Texas need background checks. Customers need none of this to buy partitions.
