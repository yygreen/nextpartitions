## 1. What Are Bathroom Partitions Made Of?
**Q:** When a buyer describes their building, what makes you steer them to one material over another, and which detail do buyers tend to overlook?

> "I start with solid plastic (HDPE) or phenolic. Then I ask about moisture and abuse, because pools, locker rooms and schools need one of those two. If the budget is tight and the room is dry, like an office, powder-coated steel does the job. What buyers overlook is the hardware and the fire rating: cheap hinges fail before the panels do, and local code may require a Class A or B rating."

## 2. Why Do Bathroom Stalls Have Gaps?
**Q:** When a buyer asks for no-gap stalls, what's the first question you ask back, and why?

> "I ask whether it's new construction or a retrofit, and what the budget is. No-gap stalls need taller panels, tighter tolerances and good ventilation, so the room has to be able to take them. If it can, I always recommend them. They cost more, but privacy is what people actually want in a restroom."

## 3. Ceiling Hung vs Floor Mounted vs Floor-to-Ceiling
**Q:** When a buyer has already picked a mounting style, what do you check before you agree it will work?

> "For ceiling-hung, I check that there's structural steel above the ceiling to carry the load. For floor-mounted, I check the floor's condition and thickness for anchoring. For floor-to-ceiling, I check that the ceiling height is consistent and level across the whole run. Even when the support is there, I try to talk buyers out of ceiling-hung and floor-mounted, because they're only held at one end."

## 4. HDPE vs Phenolic Partitions
**Q:** When a school asks you to choose between HDPE and phenolic, which do you point them to first, and what would change your answer?

> "I point them to HDPE first. It's vandal-resistant, graffiti wipes off, it's waterproof and it costs less. I'd switch to phenolic if the fire code calls for a Class A rating, or if they want a thinner, more upscale look."

## 5. Plastic vs Steel Bathroom Partitions
**Q:** In a busy public restroom, what makes you recommend stainless steel over solid plastic, or the other way round?

> "I almost always recommend solid plastic. It won't rust or dent, it shrugs off moisture and graffiti, and it costs less. Stainless makes sense in high-visibility spaces like airports, stadiums and upscale offices, where the look matters and the budget allows."

## 6. When to Replace Bathroom Partitions
**Q:** What's the most common reason customers replace their stalls rather than repair them?

> "Rust on powder-coated steel partitions, usually starting at the panel bottoms and the hardware. Most customers try to repair first, but replacement hardware has to match the old parts exactly, and finding it is so complicated that they end up replacing the stalls. Once a panel rusts through or delaminates, replacing it costs less than fixing it. ADA updates during a renovation are the other common reason."
